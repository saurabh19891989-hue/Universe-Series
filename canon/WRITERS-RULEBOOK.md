# Writers' rulebook v1.0

For every agent that outlines, writes, punches up or reviews a script. Break a rule only on
purpose and say which one.

## Pace
1. **The first 20 seconds contain a problem**, not a place. The first minute contains the goal and what it costs to fail.
2. **Every scene turns.** Someone gains or loses information, leverage, trust or time. If nothing changed, cut it.
3. **Enter late, leave early.** No arrivals, no goodbyes, no "as you know".
4. **No scene over 2½ minutes.** Average 70 seconds. Around 30 scenes an episode.
5. **Never two scenes in a row with the same energy.** Loud then quiet. Talk then run. Joke then wound.
6. **Each act ends on a turn that makes the next act a different story.**
7. **Three threads, always crossing.** When A stalls cut to B. A thread may not vanish for more than 6 minutes.
8. **Voiceover skips time and tells lies; it never describes what we can see.** At most 12% of the words.
9. **One music-led montage per episode** to compress a day into a minute.
10. **No filler test:** remove any scene. If the episode still makes sense and nobody is poorer, it stays removed.

## Places (owner, 27 Nov)
1. **Get off the ship.** No more than a quarter of an episode's running time is inside *The Unscheduled*.
2. **Five or more distinct places per episode**, each with its own colour, light and texture — markets,
   ports, palaces, wrecks, landscapes — in the spirit of a big colourful space adventure.
3. **Bright by default.** Darkness is a story event, not the house style.
4. **Every act visits somewhere new**, and at least one place per episode is a spectacle the viewer has not seen before.

## Comedy
1. **Characters never know they are funny.** They are trying very hard at something.
2. **Jokes come from want, status and precision**, not from references. No memes, no winking at the audience.
3. **Understatement after disaster.** The bigger the explosion, the smaller the line.
4. **Rule of the callback:** plant it plainly, forget it, pay it off when it hurts or saves.
5. **Each character is funny differently.** Jax sells. Mira deflates. PIP is literal. Lucía is blunt.
   Idris embroiders. Seo is deadpan. Oyuna is gentle and devastating. Guests are mildly offended
   by how Earth told their story.
6. **A laugh never cancels a feeling.** After a costly choice, allow silence.
7. **No stereotypes.** Culture gives a character knowledge, music, food and stubbornness — never an accent as the joke.

## Drama
1. **Every episode costs something real.** If the crew ends where it started, the outline is not finished.
2. **The villain is right about one thing** in every scene he is in.
3. **Secrets are for the audience first.** Tell the viewer, then make them watch the crew not know.
4. **Betrayals are choices between two loves**, never stupidity.
5. **Romance is two people changing what they want**, shown through work, not speeches.

## Action
1. **The place fights back.** Use one real property of the location (heat, gravity, darkness, radiation, delay) as the obstacle.
2. **Geography is clear**: where everyone is, what they want, what is in the way.
3. **Action reveals character**: who they save first, what they drop.

## The guest
1. **Use the book, not the film.** Read the source card. Take one deep trait, one famous moment, one thing Earth got wrong.
2. **The guest wants something of their own** and it collides with the job.
3. **The guest changes a crew member**, and leaves owed or owing.
4. **Dignity.** We laugh with legends, never at a culture.

## Shape (guide, not a cage)
Cold open ≤ 1:30 · title 0:10 · Act 1 ≈ 8 · Act 2 ≈ 9 · Act 3 ≈ 9 · Act 4 ≈ 7 · tag ≈ 1 · "what was real" 0:30.
About 4,500–5,500 spoken words. Mark every scene with: thread (A/B/C), what turns, real-place property used, asset class.

## Pipeline for one script
Brief → outline (stop for showrunner ruling) → beat sheet → draft → comedy punch-up by a
different agent → independent critic (pace, continuity, rights, science) → revision →
table-read audio for the owner → lock.
