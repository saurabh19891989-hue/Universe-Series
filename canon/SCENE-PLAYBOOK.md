# Scene playbook

**Showrunner ruling, 28 Nov:** adopted as writers' guidance alongside the rulebook. Length is ruled by the
owner: 22–26 minutes in two halves with a midpoint cliffhanger (Part 5, option A). The examples are
illustrations, not canon: in particular Lucía's ribbon going to Seo in ep 10 and a Seo–Mattias scene in
Season 2 are NOT ruled in.

Writer/story analyst, 28 Nov 2026. Built from `research/story/scene-study.md` (53 entries: classic
cinema and TV from Hollywood, Britain, India, Japan, Korea, Hong Kong, Europe and Latin America;
animation; viral clips; YouTube-native channels). It sits beside `canon/WRITERS-RULEBOOK.md` and does
not replace it.

**Why it exists.** We are now a 2D animated comedy-drama: jointed cartoon puppets over real NASA video,
made in-house. Our motion will be a notch below a studio cartoon (see `production/TOON-FORMAT.md`: walk
cycles, two head angles, 9 mouth shapes, simple hands). So each scene must run a strong, nameable engine.
The famous scenes studied almost all run **one** device you can name in four words. This file names 29.

**Rights.** These are mechanisms only. We take no plot, character, design, line or music from any work in
the study. Every example below is original to our world.

## Cost key (animation terms, from what the rig can do today)
- **A — cheap.** Two or three puppets, one painted set or one NASA plate, mostly standing, talking, head
  turns, takes and brow work; inserts of hands and objects. About 15–25 min of keying per minute.
- **B — medium.** Four or more puppets in one shot, walks, a vehicle or prop that moves on a path,
  simple effects (glow, dust, frost), a cutaway in a second style.
- **C — expensive.** Crowds, fights with contact, many moving parts in depth, liquid, cloth, fire,
  camera moves through 3D space. Use once an episode at most, and lay it out side-on where possible.

Our standing assets: PIP's lens and stamp arm (a performance with no face), the Baron's moustache, Lucía's
ribbon, Mira's loupe, the parcel and its labels, the READ receipt, Noakes's board, the real Moon/Earth/Sun
footage, one-sixth gravity, vacuum silence and light-delay.

## Part 1 — The engines

### Comedy engines

**1. The Polite Escalation (three, then the break)**
- *Mechanism:* a list of three or more items, each worse, delivered calmly; the last one breaks the
  pattern (BBC Dad; Substitute Teacher; Seven Samurai's test).
- *Use when:* opening a scene or an episode; any time a character reports bad news.
- *Stage it:* PIP is built for this: one stamp THUNK per item, the lens never changes. Cut to the listener's
  face getting smaller each time; on the break, a full-body take.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* Mars, ep 3. PIP reads Jax the charges at La Mancha customs: dust in the airlock (THUNK),
  unlicensed dog (THUNK), "one knight, armed, mounted, declaring war on the weather" (no THUNK — the stamp arm
  hesitates, then stamps the dog form a second time, by mistake). Jax: "He does that when he's moved."

**2. The Rule, Then the Break**
- *Mechanism:* state a rule plainly, forget it, then the character we love breaks it while the audience
  screams "don't" (Pan's Labyrinth; Fawlty Towers; Road Runner's rules).
- *Use when:* any guest place with magic or law (the Valley, Phobos, Venus gala). Put the rule in the
  first 5 minutes; break it at an act end.
- *Stage it:* say the rule once, in a big clear card or a guest's line; show it again silently later as a
  sign or object so the audience remembers and the crew doesn't.
- *Cost:* A to set, B to pay off.
- *Example:* Phobos, ep 4. The waiting room's one rule, painted over the door: DO NOT TAKE A NUMBER. By
  minute 14 everyone has forgotten it. Lucía, bored, pulls a ticket from the dispenser: 1. Every clock in
  the room resets to the morning they arrived.

**3. The Lie That Grows**
- *Mechanism:* to avoid one small admission, a character tells a lie, then a bigger one to cover it, until
  the house is on fire (Steamed Hams; Fawlty Towers).
- *Use when:* Jax (sells) or Idris (embroiders) faces a status figure; best as a two-hander.
- *Stage it:* two puppets, one set, one prop that keeps giving the lie away in the background (smoke, a
  noise, the dog). Each lie is more specific than the last.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* Venus, ep 5. Idris tells a Voss steward that the dog is "a Basra truffle hound". The steward
  asks it to find a truffle. Idris says it only finds them in Basra. The steward offers a Basra truffle from
  the gala menu. The dog eats the menu.

**4. The Instruction Loop**
- *Mechanism:* two people who cannot admit they don't understand one instruction blame each other in a
  circle (fold in the cheese; the dead parrot's denial loop).
- *Use when:* the Directorate, forms, the gate-drive panel, PIP's regulations.
- *Stage it:* the instruction is on screen as text; the two keep pointing at different words. Cheap and
  very clippable.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* ep 5, the gate-drive panel. Seo and Lucía read the label PRIME BEFORE ENGAGING. Lucía: "Prime
  what?" Seo: "Before engaging what?" They each press half of the instruction. The ship arrives at the gate
  on time, backwards.

**5. The Status Flip Two-Hander**
- *Mechanism:* a conversation about who is on top, and the top changes, once or twice (Goodfellas'
  "funny how"; Charlie bit my finger; the Directorate's "courageous").
- *Use when:* every villain scene; every customs desk; every negotiation.
- *Stage it:* two puppets, the high one physically higher (desk, step, chair); on the flip, change the
  height — someone stands, someone sits, the camera drops.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* Ceres, ep 7. Lucía's mother, Carmen, sits across a Voss water clerk who owns her debt. He is
  kind and fluent. She asks one question about the meter he has never been asked, and he has to stand to
  find the file. When he comes back, she is in his chair.

**6. One Line Beats the List**
- *Mechanism:* one side offers a long list (money, titles, contracts); the other wins with a single short
  line about something they love (Deewaar's bridge; Jax's "Neither" in ep 1).
- *Use when:* every temptation scene with Voss or Chichikov.
- *Stage it:* the list is visual (contracts unfold, numbers climb on a screen); the answer is a cut to a
  close-up and silence.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* Saturn, ep 10. Soren Voss lays out what he will give each crew member, one gift per course of a
  dinner. Oyuna, who has said nothing all night, recites the name of the first port he delisted, and its
  street names. He puts his fork down.

**7. The Intrusion into Dignity**
- *Mechanism:* someone works hard to look important; the world interrupts in three entries, each funnier;
  they try to keep a straight face (BBC Dad; the bar flap; the chandelier).
- *Use when:* Noakes, Pell, Arakawa on an official channel; the Baron making an entrance.
- *Stage it:* the dignified one stays still (cheap); the intrusions do the moving (dog, Baron, a floating
  object in low gravity). Hold the face on each.
- *Cost:* A–B.
- *Example:* ep 8. Arakawa records a formal warning to the fleet, at attention. Behind her, through the
  window, the Unscheduled drifts past backwards. Then the dog's face appears in its porthole. Then a
  lifeboat with Idris standing in it, bowing. She finishes the warning without turning round.

**8. The Reaction Face (the sticker beat)**
- *Mechanism:* a frozen, readable face that people reuse for any situation (Vadivelu; Baburao). This is how
  clips leave the episode and advertise it.
- *Use when:* two or three times an episode, after the big laugh line.
- *Stage it:* design each regular's signature take as a fixed pose: PIP's lens shrinking to a dot; the
  Baron's moustache drooping on one side; Lucía's slow arm-fold; Mira lowering the loupe; Seo blinking once.
  Hold 1.5 s on a clean background.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* any. Write it in the script as "PIP — THE DOT" so the animator hits the same pose every time.

### Suspense and drama engines

**9. The Bomb Under the Table (we know, they don't)**
- *Mechanism:* show the audience the danger, then let the characters chat (Hitchcock). Suspense lasts
  minutes; surprise lasts seconds. Our canon already runs this with Seo.
- *Use when:* every Seo scene from ep 2 to 10; any sabotage; any gate approach.
- *Stage it:* one silent insert early (her hand, a cut wire, a message), then a long ordinary scene with the
  object visible in a corner of frame.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* ep 5, before the gate. A bright, warm breakfast on the bridge, Lucía teasing Idris. In the
  corner of every shot, the gate-drive panel's tiny service light is on. In the cold open we saw a gloved
  hand (no face) open that panel. Nobody looks at it for ninety seconds.

**10. The Rug-Pull Planted in Plain Sight**
- *Mechanism:* the twist was shown early as background (Drishyam's building site; Sholay's coin). The
  audience feels clever and cheated at once, and rewatches.
- *Use when:* act-four turns and tags.
- *Stage it:* the plant must be on screen, in focus, for at least 2 s, and mean something else at the time.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* ep 9. Holmes's "solution nobody wanted" depends on a teacup that stands on the wrong saucer in
  every earlier shot of the dead auditor's cabin. On first viewing it reads as a set-dressing slip.

**11. The Object That Carries It**
- *Mechanism:* a small object stands for a relationship; it changes hands or state at the climax and nobody
  has to say what it means (Sholay's coin; Up's jar; Paradiso's reel; the hatchet in ep 1).
- *Use when:* every B-thread. One object per pair of characters.
- *Stage it:* insert-only (cheap and strong in our format). The object must appear three times: plant, use,
  pay-off.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* Lucía's ribbon and Seo. In ep 7 Seo ties Lucía's ribbon back on after a fight without
  being asked, a proper wrestler's knot. In ep 10, when Seo is exposed, Lucía unties it from her own wrist
  and puts it in Seo's hand. No line. (This breaks the bible's "never takes it off" on purpose: the
  showrunner's call.)

**12. The Private Moment Overheard (by the audience only)**
- *Mechanism:* a funny or guarded character, alone, shows a grief we never guessed (Avatar's song on the
  hill; Seo's photo in ep 2).
- *Use when:* once an episode, for the character the episode is changing. Never for the guest's comedy.
- *Stage it:* wide, small figure, real NASA plate behind (Earth, Saturn), no score or one instrument.
  The character does a small, practical thing.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* ep 4. PIP, alone at night, files a report: RE: MISS ORTEGA. WAGES. He enters the amount
  and stamps it PAID, knowing it isn't. Then he files a correction. We never mention it again. (Report
  number and READ stamp follow the season ruling.)

**13. The Two Moles at One Table**
- *Mechanism:* two people who both know a secret talk about everything except it (Infernal Affairs'
  rooftop). The scene is about what each will admit.
- *Use when:* Seo and Mattias (once both are planted, eps 7–10); Seo and Holmes (ep 9); Jax and Voss.
- *Stage it:* static wide, both small against a big plate (Jupiter, the rings); cut to a close-up only on
  the one admission. Very cheap.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* Season 2 (or late Season 1 if an outline allows). Seo and Mattias Voss share a window over
  Jupiter's Red Spot, each sure the other is a loyal stranger. They talk about the best way to lie to a family. Both take notes.

**14. Bright, Empty Menace**
- *Mechanism:* danger in a wide, sunlit, silent landscape, with long waits (North by Northwest's
  crop-duster). Suits us: our plates are bright and our vacuum is silent.
- *Use when:* any exterior chase or ambush on a real surface (Mars canyon, Bennu, the Valley).
- *Stage it:* one held real-footage wide; a tiny figure; the threat appears as a dot that grows. No score
  until it's close. Then cut short.
- *Cost:* A–B (vehicles on paths are cheap).
- *Example:* ep 3, Valles Marineris. Jax waits alone at a canyon marker with the dog. A dust devil
  crosses the horizon, slowly. Then a second one, closer. The third one turns toward him. It has a Voss
  survey drone at its centre.

**15. The Sayable Question**
- *Mechanism:* end on one plain question an audience can say aloud and argue about (Baahubali's closing
  question became a national hashtag for two years).
- *Use when:* the end of Act 2 (mid-episode) and the tag of every episode.
- *Stage it:* end on an image, not a speech. The question must be five words or fewer when people retell it.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* ep 6 tag: Seo deletes the log of her own rescue signal. The sayable question: "Why did Seo save them?"

### Action engines

**16. Physics Is the Comedian**
- *Mechanism:* the real property of the place is the gag (Road Runner's gravity; Homer's double fall). Ours
  are one-sixth g hang time, vacuum silence, light-delay, radiation, heat.
- *Use when:* every exterior action beat (rulebook: "the place fights back").
- *Stage it:* hold the shot longer than feels right; the score holds one note; dialogue happens in mid-air.
- *Cost:* B.
- *Example:* ep 6, near Mercury. A spanner Lucía drops in weightlessness takes the whole of a two-minute
  argument to drift across the engine room, and arrives — gently — at the exact moment Idris says "Nothing
  can go wrong."

**17. Laying the Track Ahead**
- *Mechanism:* the solution is improvised as fast as the problem arrives, at full speed (Wallace & Gromit's
  train chase).
- *Use when:* Lucía's set piece in any episode; Mira calling the ground.
- *Stage it:* side-on, two layers: the problem advancing on the right, the fix being built on the left.
- *Cost:* B.
- *Example:* ep 7, Bennu. The crew's rover rolls off a rubble slope on a body with almost no gravity; Lucía
  runs ahead on the surface laying a line of anchor pins for the wheels to catch, each pin landing a
  second before a wheel does.

**18. The Disadvantage Fight**
- *Mechanism:* the hero starts weak and uses whatever is in the room; the action *is* the comedy (Jackie
  Chan's method: disadvantage, environment, clarity).
- *Use when:* Lucía's fights; any time the crew is unarmed (always).
- *Stage it:* establish three objects in the room early (a ladder, a mail sack, a coffee machine); the fight
  uses all three in order.
- *Cost:* B–C (do it side-on to stay B).
- *Example:* ep 8, the Io vault. Lucía, cuffed to a filing cabinet, fights two guards using the cabinet's
  drawers — she opens one into a shin, rides another like a step, and finishes by filing the guard.

**19. The Lateral Run**
- *Mechanism:* the camera tracks side-on along a corridor or ridge, so the whole fight or chase is in one
  plane (Oldboy's corridor). It is also how 2D puppets look best.
- *Use when:* any chase, escape or brawl.
- *Stage it:* one long painted strip or a panning NASA plate; walk and run cycles; characters enter and
  exit frame left/right. No depth.
- *Cost:* B (C if the crowd is large).
- *Example:* ep 3. The crew run the length of a Martian rail platform, side-on, while Quixote rides the other
  way on a mining trolley to charge the dust devil. The two lines of action pass each other mid-frame.

### Music, silence and image engines

**20. Music Against the Image**
- *Mechanism:* the score says the opposite of the picture: grandeur over a dirty trick, a lullaby over a
  heist, a hymn over a betrayal (Godfather's baptism; Parasite's con montage).
- *Use when:* the con, the heist, the betrayal.
- *Stage it:* pick the episode's music world (season-01 table) and play its most dignified cue over the
  scam. Cut on the music's phrases, not the dialogue.
- *Cost:* A–B.
- *Example:* ep 7, Ceres. Anansi's con plays as a montage under a solemn highlife hymn with talking drum;
  each forged water contract is signed on a downbeat.

**21. The Room Takes a Side**
- *Mechanism:* two groups, two songs (or two chants, two stamps), and the room chooses by joining in
  (Casablanca's anthems). No speech needed.
- *Use when:* the season finale; any big public scene (the Venus gala, the Ceres town).
- *Stage it:* start with one voice, cut to more faces joining (faces can be held stills with mouths —
  cheaper than a crowd moving), end on one face.
- *Cost:* B (stills), C (moving crowd).
- *Example:* ep 7. At the Voss water office on Ceres, the clerks drum their stamps in the company rhythm.
  Lucía's mother taps the old miners' rhythm on the counter. One by one, the queue joins her.

**22. The Wordless Montage**
- *Mechanism:* a long time in a short screen time with one theme and small repeated objects (Up; Bluey's
  Sleepytime). The rulebook already asks for one music-led montage per episode.
- *Use when:* compressing a journey, a job, or a relationship.
- *Stage it:* insert-heavy (cheap), one repeated object that changes each time.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* ep 3, the road trip. Each stop across four thousand kilometres of canyon is the dog moving one
  seat closer to Jax in the rover, until it sleeps on his boots.

**23. Ma — the Held Silence**
- *Mechanism:* a deliberate empty interval where nothing happens so the next blow lands (Miyazaki's *ma*;
  Spirited Away's train; the Blackadder ending).
- *Use when:* after a costly choice (rulebook: "a laugh never cancels a feeling"); before the last act.
- *Stage it:* a real NASA plate for 5–10 s with breathing only, or one character doing one small thing.
  It is our cheapest shot and among the most powerful.
- *Cost:* A (cheapest in the show).
- *Example:* ep 6 tag. Before Seo deletes the log, 8 s of the real Sun's surface filling the window, no
  score, PIP's lens dark.

**24. The Laugh-Machine Stops**
- *Mechanism:* a comedy turns to grief in its last minute; slow the motion, slow the theme, stop the jokes
  (Blackadder's finale; Futurama's dog).
- *Use when:* rarely — once or twice a season (eps 6 and 10).
- *Stage it:* the main theme on solo instrument at half speed; no dialogue; end on an image, not a death.
- *Cost:* A–B.
- *Example:* ep 8, "a crew member is badly hurt". The escape plays as comedy until the lifeboat is clear;
  then the theme on a single erhu, and one shot of the hurt crew member's signature object floating in the
  cabin (Lucía's ribbon, if it is Lucía).

**25. Repetition with Variation**
- *Mechanism:* the same place, the same tune, a little changed each time, so the change is the story
  (In the Mood for Love's stairs; ep 1's board reading 1,094).
- *Use when:* romance (Jax and Mira), running gags, and the season's continuity (the READ receipt).
- *Stage it:* lock the camera position and the music cue; change one thing per visit.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* Jax and Mira's "There are nine sections" exchange. Ep 1 plants it twice; ep 2 gives it to
  Arakawa. Ep 5: at the gala, Jax starts it, and Mira, dancing, finishes his half for him.

### Animation-only engines

**26. The Story Drawn (cutaway tale)**
- *Mechanism:* when a character tells a tale, cut to it, drawn in a different style, for 5–15 s. The picture
  can obey, exaggerate or contradict the teller (storytime channels; the bible's "the pictures contradict
  him").
- *Use when:* every guest legend's story; the Baron always; Idris's lies.
- *Stage it:* a second, simpler style per teller (for the Baron, an engraving-like look of our own after
  the 1862 Doré illustrations, which are public domain); flat, few moving parts; reuse cutaway assets.
- *Cost:* B (but each cutaway is short and reusable).
- *Example:* ep 3. Idris tells Sancho how he won a ship at cards from a sultan; the cutaway shows a sultan,
  a palace, a fleet. Sancho asks one question about the rules of the card game; the cutaway quietly
  shrinks the fleet to one rowing boat.

**27. The Impossible Frame**
- *Mechanism:* a picture only animation makes cheaply: the frame flipped so falling reads as rising
  (Spider-Verse's leap); a character too small in a huge real plate; time freezing in the frame.
- *Use when:* once an episode, at the emotional top of the action.
- *Stage it:* use the real footage: rotate the NASA plate (Earth "above" a figure falling toward it), scale a
  puppet to a speck against Saturn.
- *Cost:* B.
- *Example:* ep 10. Seo's choice is shown as one frame: the ship turns, and the real Saturn plate rotates
  so the rings stand upright like a door, and she flies through it.

**28. Pain Without Injury (the bounce)**
- *Mechanism:* cartoon physics lets the body take a comic beating that would be horrifying in live action
  (the gorge; Road Runner's "humiliated more than harmed").
- *Use when:* Jax's bravado; the Baron; never for real cost beats.
- *Stage it:* squash and settle on the rig (it has squash and snap eases); dust puff; a held reaction face.
- *Cost:* A–B.
- *Example:* ep 3. Jax insists he can ride Quixote's horse. In Mars gravity he goes up, and up, and comes
  down very slowly into a cactus-shaped antenna, and stays there through the next two lines of dialogue.

### Structure engines

**29. The Freeze and Rewind (a cold-open option)**
- *Mechanism:* open in mid-chaos with a comic detail; freeze on the worst moment; the narrator rewinds
  (City of God's chicken; Goodfellas' narration).
- *Use when:* an episode whose first act is quiet. Jax is a narrator who lies, so the rewind can be a lie.
- *Stage it:* freeze is free in animation; a colour shift marks the jump back.
- *Cost:* A.
- *Example:* ep 7 cold open. Freeze on the Unscheduled upside-down in a Ceres water tank, Lucía's mother
  waving from a balcony. Jax V.O.: "This was a good day. Let me explain." Title.

## Part 2 — The set-piece rule (every episode)

Three **signature scenes** per episode, chosen at the outline stage and named in the outline header:

| # | Kind | Where it falls | Built from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The comic set piece** | in the first 10 minutes (Act 1 or early Act 2) | engines 1–8, 16, 26, 28 |
| 2 | **The heart scene** | Act 3 | engines 6, 11, 12, 22–24 |
| 3 | **The action / spectacle set piece** | Act 4 | engines 14, 16–19, 27 |

- **One of the three (usually the comic one) is written to stand alone as a 30–60 s short.** Test: a
  stranger who has never seen the show understands it in the first 3 seconds; it has one escalation and a
  button; it uses no name the viewer must already know; it frames well for a 9:16 crop (the key action in
  the centre third). Mark it in the script header as **SHORT**.
- **Every signature scene gets the animation budget.** Spend B/C cost here, keep the rest A.
- **One sayable question** (engine 15) at the end of Act 2 and in the tag.
- **One held silence** (engine 23) per episode, after the most costly choice.
- **One reaction face** (engine 8) per regular per episode, written into the script by name.

## Part 3 — The opening rule and the re-hook cadence

Grounded in: YouTube's own advice that the first 15 seconds should tease where the video goes; YouTube
Studio's retention "intro" figure measured at 30 seconds; the leaked MrBeast production guide's treatment
of the first minute as the highest-risk stretch (alleged document); and the viral clips, which are all
understood in their first 3 seconds (see the study, section 53 and C).

**First 5 seconds:** motion and a problem on screen. No logo, no establishing shot without a problem in it.
**By 0:20:** the problem and what failure costs (already rulebook Pace 1).
**By 0:30:** one laugh and one image the viewer has never seen (a real NASA plate doing something funny
counts).
**By 1:30:** title sting. Never before the first laugh.
**By 3:00:** the guest legend or the episode's spectacle place is seen; the job has a clock; the
viewer knows which genre this week is.

**Re-hook cadence (after 3:00):**
- Every scene turns (rulebook). On top of that, **every 2–3 minutes something new appears on screen**: a new
  place, a new character, a reveal, a set-piece gag. Our scene average of ~70 s makes this natural if no
  two talk scenes sit back to back.
- **Every 6–8 minutes, a hard turn** (an act end or a mid-act reversal) that changes what the episode is
  about.
- **The midpoint (end of Act 2) is our "interval":** it must be the strongest cliffhanger in the episode
  after the tag, because it is where long YouTube viewing drops. (Indian cinema's interval block is the model.)
- **Never two static talk scenes in a row** (rulebook Pace 5, sharpened): if two people sit and talk, the
  next scene must move.

## Part 4 — What to do more, now that we are animation

**More**
1. **Cutaway tales** (engine 26) for every legend's story, in the teller's own drawn style. This is our
   clearest win over live action.
2. **Physical takes and reaction faces** written into the script by name.
3. **Impossible staging with the real footage**: rotate, scale and frame the NASA plates for comedy and awe.
4. **Running visual gags** that live in the background: Noakes's board, PIP's report numbers, the coffee
   machine, the dog. They reward rewatching and cost nothing.
5. **Fast scene changes**: a new place every act (rulebook Places 4), and hard cuts on a sound or a gag.
6. **Musical moments**: one diegetic tune per episode (the frozen post horn in ep 1 is the model), and one
   music-led montage.
7. **Side-on action** (engine 19): it suits the rig, keeps geography clear, and frames well for shorts.

**Drop or reduce**
1. **The "no visible mouth" staging rules** in the E01 and E02 scripts (backs of heads, insert-only lines,
   "Mouths are hidden or off-screen", the four VISIBLE MOUTH exceptions). They were written for a photoreal
   pipeline with no lip-sync. The puppets lip-sync now. Keep the insert grammar where it is strong (the
   signature fading, the READ receipt), but let the leads act their big lines on their faces.
2. **Long static talk.** Any scene over 90 s with two seated characters must earn it with an engine (5, 6, 13)
   or be cut, merged or made to move.
3. **Exposition of a plan that then works as described.** If a plan will succeed, show it in pieces or not at
   all; if it is explained in full, it should go wrong.
4. **Lines that describe what the picture can now show.** Animation can show the Baron's King of the Moon;
   the line can then be the joke against the picture.

## Part 5 — Honest note on episode length (the owner decides)

**What we plan:** 30–40 minutes; both locked scripts run about 35–36 minutes.

**What the evidence says:**
- **Long can work on YouTube.** Two indie animated pilots of about 26–32 minutes — The Amazing Digital Circus
  (~100 million views in its first month, 350 million+ later) and Hazbin Hotel (100 million+) — show viewers
  will watch half an hour of animation from a YouTube channel. [verified, study entries 48–49]
- **But those were fully animated by studios with existing audiences** (Glitch already ran a large channel;
  Vivziepop had a following). We are a new channel with limited motion. A weak minute costs more in a long
  video because viewers leave and YouTube learns from it.
- **The TV animation units are 11 and 22 minutes** (Smiling Friends ~11; US animated sitcoms ~22; Bluey ~7–8).
  They exist because a single comic problem holds about 11 minutes. [Smiling Friends and Bluey verified]
- **Our cost is per minute.** Keying is 15–25 min per minute of dialogue scene and render is ~30 CPU-s per
  second (`production/TOON-FORMAT.md`). A 22-minute episode costs about 60% of a 36-minute one; the owner wants
  as many episodes as fast as possible.

**Options, with the writer's recommendation:**
- **A (recommended): 22–26 minutes**, built as two halves of 11–13 minutes with a hard interval cliffhanger at
  the midpoint, plus a 30–60 s short cut from each episode. About 20 scenes instead of 30; three signature
  scenes; same four-act shape with shorter acts (5–6 min). Faster to make, easier to hold, more episodes.
  For E01 and E02 this means cutting about 10 minutes each. The audits find about 5–6 minutes of clean
