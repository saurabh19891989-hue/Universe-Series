# UNIVERSE UNDELIVERED — Series Bible v1.0 (draft)

**Names (owner, 27 Nov):** the show is **Universe Undelivered**; the YouTube channel is
**Legends Overheard**. "Secret Universe" was the working title and survives only in old file
names and script headers.

Status: showrunner draft, 27 Nov 2026. Replaces v0.1 (`research/v0.1/`). Guest rights and
location choices are provisional until the character bank and footage audit are reconciled.
Everything here is open to the owner's veto.

## 1. The idea in one breath

**Earth's legends were never made up. They were overheard.**

The universe is very large, and it turns out humans have very good hearing. Sindbad, Sherlock
Holmes, Sun Wukong, Don Quixote, Scheherazade, Anansi — every culture's heroes are real, alive,
and living out among the real planets and stars. They are called **the Storied**.

Someone is deleting them from the map, one port at a time.

A broke courier crew takes a parcel addressed to a place that stops existing while they are
on approach. By the time they work out what they are carrying, the government wants them
arrested, the richest family in the system wants them bought, one of their own crew is
reporting on them, and their captain is starting to suspect he was not always called Jax Vale.

**Why this beats v0.1:** v0.1 was about audits, ledgers and charters. This keeps its best idea
(places erased from the official map; the erasure began as protection and became power) and
makes it personal, visual and funny. Every episode has a face the audience already knows,
a real place they can see, and a reason to click: *Sherlock Holmes solves a murder under
Europa's ice.*

## 2. World rules (few, firm)

1. **The space is real.** Places, distances, gravity, heat, light-delay are real and create
   story problems. Settlements, ships, gates and people are fiction and never claimed as real.
2. **The Atlas** is the index of everywhere and everyone. Gates only open to a listed address.
3. **Delisting.** Remove a place from the Atlas and no gate opens to it: no trade, no rescue,
   no post. Worse, a delisted Storied begins to **thin** — they lose the thing that made them
   a legend. Holmes stops noticing. The Baron's lies stop coming true. Quixote sees only
   windmills. Thin long enough and you are nobody, with no memory you were ever anyone.
4. **Gates** are fixed doors between installed endpoints. No time travel, no teleporting,
   no instant messages across deep space without a gate. Travel costs money the crew lacks.
5. **Nobody explains the Storied.** Characters argue about it; the show never settles it.
6. **Every episode changes something that stays changed**: a debt, an ally, a secret, a loss.
7. **Respect.** Deities and worshipped figures of living religions are not comic characters.
   Epic heroes, tricksters, sages' students and literary characters are welcome, played with
   affection. The Storied are usually mildly annoyed at how Earth got them wrong.

**Against them**
- **Soren Voss** — head of the Voss Company, a chartered trading house in the mould of the old
  East India companies. He does not erase legends. He *acquires* them: delist, buy the wreck,
  relist as a Voss Heritage Destination with the legend under contract playing a tidier
  version of themselves. Gracious, patient, never raises his voice.
- **Ilse Voss** (heir, the knife) and **Mattias Voss** (younger son, the crew's rat).
- **Auditor Pell** — Directorate. Seo's handler. Could strangle a man with a subordinate clause.
- **Commander Tomoe Arakawa** — Directorate Enforcement. Honourable, relentless, always one
  port behind. The hunter you end up respecting.
- **Cass Rook** — rival courier captain, Nigerian-British, charming, for sale. Frenemy.

**Recurring Storied** — Baron Munchausen (stowaway and unreliable guest narrator), Captain Nemo
(ally with a submarine and a grudge against empires), Scheherazade (information broker; nobody
leaves her salon before the story ends), Sun Wukong (the cavalry, unfortunately).
**Anant** — an original wandering sage who turns up rarely and asks the one question nobody wants.

### Faces of the show (owner direction, 27 Nov)
The cast must look like the whole world, not one country. Core crew: Mediterranean captain,
Indian cartographer, Mongolian archivist, Mexican engineer, Arab navigator, Korean pilot,
British-voiced computer. Recurring: the Nordic Voss family, English Auditor Pell, Russian
Chichikov, Japanese Commander Arakawa, Nigerian-British Cass Rook, and a Chinese recurring
role to be added in Season 1 (candidate: the Relay Guild's chief dispatcher, Wen Jialing).
Guests bring every other region. No more than one core character from any one country.

### Visual direction (owner direction, 27 Nov)
**Photoreal, not animated.** The footage is real, so people, ships and sets must look real
enough to sit in the same frame. The illustrated ink style and the vector puppets are both
rejected. Characters are photoreal and consistent from shot to shot; interiors look like
physical sets; the grade matches the NASA plate in every shot.

## 5. Format

- **30–40 minutes.** Cold open (under 90 s, mid-trouble) → title sting → four acts of 7–9
  minutes, each ending on a turn → one-minute tag that moves the serial.
- **Three threads per episode:** A = the job and the legend. B = two crew members changing
  toward each other. C = the rat, Voss, or Jax's past.
- **About 30 scenes, average 70 seconds, none over 2½ minutes.** Voiceover is an accelerator,
  not an explainer. Some episodes are narrated by the guest (the Baron narrates one and the
  pictures contradict him).
- **Every episode is a different genre**: caper, siege, western, farce, heist, survival, con,
  prison break, murder mystery, dynasty drama.
- **Music is a character.** The ship's receiver picks up stations from every Storied port, so
  each episode's score leans on that guest's musical world, around a fixed main theme and
  character motifs. All original.
- **"What was real"** — a 30-second end note read by PIP: which places and facts were real,
  with footage credits. Honest, funny, and useful for YouTube's originality and disclosure rules.

## 6. The long secret (showrunner only until it lands)

Jax Vale is a thinned legend. The Directorate registry lists him as "nobody".
He once told a one-eyed giant that his name was Nobody.

He is **Odysseus** — the wanderer, the liar, the man trying to get home — delisted at his own
request, for a reason he no longer remembers. The parcel in episode 1 is addressed to
*Nobody, Ithaca*. It holds a living piece of olive wood from a bed he built himself.
Someone has been posting it for twenty years. Every courier before him failed to deliver it.

Planted from the start: he is strangely good with a bow, uneasy on water, talks his way out of
everything, and a stray dog on Mars refuses to leave him. The audience learns the truth in the
Season 1 finale; Jax learns only that Voss bought the rights to whoever he was.
The 150-episode shape is an odyssey home — with Mira falling for a man who has a wife waiting,
a son he has never met hunting him, and suitors in his house.

Deeper layers for later seasons: who built the Atlas; why the first delistings were acts of
protection; what the Storied were being hidden from.


**From the source library (27 Nov) — strengthens the secret.** In the Odyssey, Odysseus is kept
for seven years by Calypso, whose name means "she who conceals" and who is the daughter of
Atlas. Jax has no records before seven years ago. So in our story the Atlas is not only a map:
its keeper is the Concealer, and the first delisting was an act of love — she hid him to keep
him. This is the root of "the erasures began as protection". Do not reveal before Season 2.
Also from the library: if the parcel holds wood from the olive-tree bed, someone has cut the
tree at its root, so the bed can no longer prove who he is (finale stakes); and Nemo must be
Verne's original Prince Dakkar, a freedom fighter — the old English translation censored this.

## 7a. Where stories come from (owner direction, 27 Nov)
Every episode is built from several sources in different cultures, mixed, never one book
retold. We read the original texts (Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg, Wikisource and
similar), take mechanisms, images and characters, and write our own plot. Each episode's
`sources.md` lists at least three sources from at least two cultures and says what was taken
from each. Nothing is adapted scene by scene.

## 8. Rights and safety summary
- Guests: public-domain originals only, checked per character in `research/characters/`.
  Use the book, never the film: no screen costumes, catchphrases or designs.
- Core cast, ship, Atlas, Voss Company, Directorate: original and ours.
- Footage: NASA/ESA/ESO items logged with credit and licence in `assets/`.
- Not for children: aimed at teens and adults; mild language, no gore.
