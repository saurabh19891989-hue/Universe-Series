# Episode 6 — "Too Close to the Sun": sources

Rule (bible 7a): at least three sources from at least two cultures, mixed; mechanisms, images and
traits taken; nothing retold scene by scene. This episode draws on **four literary sources from
four traditions** (Roman/Greek, Indian, Arabic, Persian) and on a **living performance tradition**
(Mongolian epic singing) for Oyuna. Card numbers refer to `research/library/sources/`; the mix
recipe is `research/library/season-01-mixes.md`, section 6. I worked from the library's cards
(which name the editions the library actually read); I did not reopen the texts this session.

The plot is ours and is in none of the sources: the withdrawn Mercury Yard and its dead gate
ring, the tender *Vindhya* and its feather shield, the orbit lowered in secret at each Mercury
pass, the ice run, the sun-kettle, the middle course, the storm at the bottom, the hand-keyed
call, the roll-call epic, the feather given to the pilot.

## 1. Ovid, *Metamorphoses* VIII.183–259, Daedalus and Icarus, with Perdix (Rome; Greek myth) — card 09
- **Edition read (by the library):** Henry T. Riley's literal prose translation (Bohn, 1851),
  Project Gutenberg #26073, with Riley's notes. Public domain everywhere.
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **the middle course with two failure modes.** Daedalus warns against flying too
    low (the sea clogs the feathers) as well as too high. Ours: the escape depth round the Sun,
    too high for the kettle to boil, too low for the shield to live (3, 14). Icarus's complaint
    that he "only gets the credit for one" is our line on the card's point.
  - *Trait:* **joy, not defiance.** Ovid's boy comes to enjoy "a bolder flight" (the card's quotation from Riley). Ours:
    "I was happy. Have you never been so happy you forgot an instruction?" (3), and the old man
    laughing at the rim of the shield (23).
  - *Image:* **feathers laid in order, shortest to longest, like a rustic pipe.** Ours: racks of
    ceramic shield tiles that look like pan-pipes; the reed pipes at his belt; the reed pipe in
    the score (3).
  - *Image:* **yellow wax softened under a boy's thumb.** Ours: the old man's signature insert,
    and "the wax is for thinking"; the row of wax beads as the episode's thermometer.
  - *Moment:* **he calls his father's name as he falls.** Ours, inverted: "His father told
    everyone the boy called for him. Before that, he said, 'Look.'" (23). The "Look" is ours.
  - *Secret, touched only:* **Perdix**, the nephew Daedalus threw from a height and called an
    accident; the partridge that "dreads the higher regions". Ours: the low-flying boat called
    the *Partridge* after "a cousin" who "never cared for heights after", and one line: "He was a
    great explainer of accidents" (9). The secret itself is kept for Season 2 (library proposal
    6c).
  - *What Earth got wrong (card):* the moral of pride is a later gloss; the land and sea were
    named for him. Ours: "They named the sea after it." Oyuna's "And was not found" is ours.
- **Ours, not Ovid's:** that Icarus lived; that he is old; that he builds heat shields and has
  spent sixty laps secretly lowering an orbit to "touch" the corona; his friendship and quarrel
  with Sampati; "Everyone gets one."
- **Not taken:** the flight from Crete as a scene, Minos, the labyrinth, the burial, Daedalus as
  a character. No film, stage or game version of Icarus was consulted or echoed; no strapped-on
  feather wings appear.

## 2. Valmiki, *Ramayana*, Kishkindha Kanda: Sampati's story (India) — card 10
- **Edition read (by the library):** Ralph T. H. Griffith's verse translation (1870–74), Project
  Gutenberg #24869, Book IV cantos LVI–LXIII. Public domain everywhere.
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **the shield that burns the shielder.** Ours: the tender's shield is "his
    wings"; covering a second ship costs "feathers" (4); it is burned to its ribs by the end.
  - *Mechanism:* **a waiting set as a task, with a promise:** strangers will come; tell them what
    you have seen and you will fly again. Ours: what he saw is ours (a pilot braced before her
    ship fell, and a hand-keyed call four minutes later), and he finds it is owed to one person
    (12, 18).
  - *Mechanism:* **witness as cure.** In the text his wings sprout when he has told what he knows.
    Ours, with restraint: two burned vanes of the shield open after the telling (18); one small
    new feather on his shoulder (25). Nobody explains either.
  - *Mechanism:* **the far-seer who cannot move.** Ours: the sun-deck and its mirror; he reads the
    Sun's weather and calls the turn for a pilot who can fly (4, 18, 23).
  - *Mechanism:* **menace that is really need.** In the text his first words alarm the searchers
    and his next are a request for news. Ours: the formal salvage hail that sounds like a threat,
    followed at once by "Have you news?" (2, 4). His hunger is not used and not joked about.
  - *Images:* the vow before people the brothers wished to impress; cities "like chariot wheels"
    and rivers like thread from a great height (given in our own words: "cartwheels", "thread");
    the fall onto a mountain (the tender is named *Vindhya* for it).
  - *Behaviour:* he finishes mourning before he flies. Ours: one cup of water poured out "for my
    brother" (20). See the care note below.
  - *What Earth got wrong (card):* he is remembered as an informant; he is the hero of his own
    tragedy. Ours: "Earth tells it as though it were weather… I spread them." And then his own
    harder correction, which is ours: "The vow was mine."
- **Ours, not Valmiki's:** everything he does in this episode; his line that his brother, in old
  age, "went" when "a stranger called for help" and did not come back, which is the only
  reference to the brother's death and names nobody; that he therefore keeps an old rescue band
  open; his speech to Seo; the gift of the feather.
- **Care, as ruled in `canon/season-01.md`:** Sampati is heroic and dignified throughout. No
  deity appears or is named. Rama, Sita and Hanuman do not appear, are not named and are not
  alluded to in a way that could be a joke; the search party of the text is not used. The
  information he gives is about our own story. Jatayu is named once, with respect, as his
  brother. Garuda, who appears in the text's list of the sky's layers, is not named, and that
  list is not used. Nobody laughs at his burning. He has exactly one dry line and it is his own.
  **For the owner:** the cup of water (20) is written as a private gesture, not as a rite, and
  can be cut without loss of plot.

## 3. *The Thousand and One Nights*, the frame story (Arabic tradition) — card 08
- **Editions read (by the library):** Lang (1898), Project Gutenberg #128; Burton vol. 1 (1885),
  #3435, frame only, his notes and framing not used.
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism, inverted (mix recipe):* **a story told through the night to stay alive**, here
    not to delay a death at dawn but to keep a crew awake through heat (20–24).
  - *Mechanism:* **the confederate who asks on cue.** The sister's agreed words make the telling
    possible. Ours: Idris's cue from E05, "If you're still awake… tell us what happened next",
    becomes the watch-call; when he fails, Lucía says it for him (21, 24).
- **Not taken:** any tale; Scheherazade does not appear; the threat she lives under is not
  mentioned.

## 4. Ferdowsi, *Shahnama*: Zal and the Simurgh (Persia) — card 21
- **Edition read (by the library):** Warner and Warner (1905), Internet Archive. US public
  domain; the card flags the translators' death dates as unverified, so nothing is quoted.
- **Taken:** *Mechanism only:* **the feather to burn when in trouble**, a one-use summoning
  token. Ours: Sampati's first new feather, given to Seo: "burn this. I will come and say what I
  saw" (25, 27).
- **Not taken:** Zal, Sam, the Simurgh herself, any scene or wording. **For the showrunner:** this
  moves a Persian epic's mechanism onto an Indian epic's figure, which is how the series mixes,
  but say if you would rather the token were a shield tile from Icarus.

## 5. Mongolian heroic epic singing (tuuli) — background, with card 20
- **Basis:** as in E04's `sources.md` section 4: a living tradition (on UNESCO's urgent
  safeguarding list, 2009) of singers who carry very long epics by heart and perform them in a
  low epic voice to the two-stringed lute (topshuur). Card 20 (Zeitlin's *Gessar Khan*, 1927) is
  background only.
- **Taken, as custom, and flagged for checking with a performer:**
  - that epics are sung at night, in winter, over as many nights as they need;
  - that an epic, once begun, should be sung to its end and not cut short;
  - that a praise-song to the place (at home, the mountains) comes before the epic.
  These three are from general knowledge of the western Mongolian tradition, **not from a text I
  opened**. Please have them confirmed, and the song composed or checked, by someone from the
  tradition before lock. If any is wrong, Oyuna's lines in 20 and the first line of the song in
  21 change; the plot does not.
- **Ours:** every sung word. The praise is built from real facts about Mercury and the Sun. The
  "epic" is the crew's own story so far, sung as a roll-call that each person must answer.
- **Not taken:** Geser, who is venerated, does not appear and is not quoted; his forgetting and
  the arrow-letter stay reserved for episode 10. No throat-singing as spectacle.
- **Dignity:** Oyuna performs; she is not a device. The use of her art for survival is hers to
  propose, and it works because of what the art is (long, exact, unfinished until it ends).

## 6. Considered and not used
- *Popol Vuh* (card 18; the mix lists it for an image): nothing taken.
- Attar's *Conference of the Birds* and its Simurgh (character bank ME09): a Sufi religious
  allegory; kept out.
- Sampati's son, who feeds him, and the sage by name: not used.
- Phaethon: deliberately not used (a third sun-fall would crowd the two).

## 7. Real science and footage
Facts used, with their status, are listed in `outline.md` section 10. Numbers spoken on screen
that come from `research/locations/08-mercury-sun.md`: 430 °C, −180 °C ("a hundred and eighty
below"), fifteen million degrees at the Sun's core, a spacecraft through the corona. Numbers
spoken on screen from general knowledge, for the critic to confirm: "seven times" Earth's
sunlight; three turns to two orbits; a day of two Mercury years; "a third of a g"; eight
minutes for light to Earth; Parker Solar Probe's seven Venus flybys; "a million degrees" for
the corona. Nothing about how the pictures are made is said on screen.
Footage: only items logged in `assets/candidates.json`, location 8 (NASA Goddard/SDO, ESO; a
MESSENGER still for Mercury if one is logged with its credit). The eclipse plate is ESO's; ESO
items carry their own credit line and Creative Commons terms, as listed.
