# SECRET UNIVERSE
## Episode 4 — "The Phobos Exception"
### Shooting script, draft 2 (comedy and dialogue punch-up; for the independent critic)

Running time: RUNTIME_LINE
Visual style: photoreal characters and sets composited with real NASA and ESA/Hubble footage,
staged mostly as cinematic stills with camera moves and limited motion. Lip movement is scarce
or absent. Phobos and Deimos are THIN on real footage, so the moons' surfaces are composites on
real Mars-from-orbit plates and the two real HiRISE Phobos stills; every interior is a
generated photoreal set. **Bright by default:** Mars fills the sky over Phobos and pours
salmon-orange light through every skylight. Each place has its own colour world (see
`casting-notes.md`).

**How to read the headers.** Each unit lists its number, setting, thread (A = the job and the
legend: getting Oyuna out · B = the crew changing toward each other: PIP and the Registrar,
Mira and Oyuna, Lucía and Seo, Jax and Nasreddin · C = the spy, Pell and Arakawa, Voss and
Chichikov, Jax's past), honest target seconds, visual class (REAL FOOTAGE WIDE / PHOTOREAL SET
STILL / PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT / MONTAGE / DATA VISUAL), the real property of the place
used, and a STAGING note saying how it plays without lip-sync.

**Standing staging rules (they apply everywhere unless a scene says otherwise)**
- **Everyone on Phobos and Deimos talks on paper.** It is the house style of the two moons and
  the comedy of the episode. All slip, card and board text is set by us in post as overlays,
  never generated inside an image. **Three paper voices, three typefaces, never mixed:**
  - **The Registrar** is a machine behind the frosted glass of Window 17: brass drums, green
    enamel, card indexes, a printer slot and a desk bell. She "speaks" only in printed slips in
    **typewriter capitals**. Every slip is an INSERT, preceded by one *ding*. **Pace:** a slip is
    on screen only as long as it takes to read twice; the next line may start while it is still
    up. Nobody reads her slips aloud unless the script says so.
  - **Commander Arakawa does not speak on Phobos** ("nothing said here binds anyone"). She writes
    in **handwritten block capitals** in a small black notebook and holds the page up. Every page
    is an INSERT.
  - **Wen Jialing** speaks only through her dispatch printer, in a **clean modern sans-serif,
    sentence case, every slip timestamped to the second**, and almost every slip carries a
    **postscript added in pencil** in her own quick hand. The type is the dispatcher; the pencil
    is the woman. Slips are torn off and slid across a desk (INSERT).
- **Nasreddin sits backwards.** On benches, on the ferry: always facing the wall or the way he
  came, back to the room. His great grey beard covers his mouth anyway. Play him on the back of
  his hat, his hands, one eye over his shoulder, and the listener. He turns round to face the
  room exactly once (26).
- **Auditor Pell is always behind something:** the folded *Directorate Gazette*, a teacup held
  at his chin, or his own back. His eyes over the paper are his close-up. His furled umbrella
  goes everywhere, including onto a moon with no weather. **Pell never says the word "no"**
  until 24. Keep it that way in any later edit.
- **Oyuna** speaks from inside the ger with the door half open, from behind her lute, or with
  her back to us under the Mars skylight. Her hands (tea bowl, lute strings, the parcel) are her
  inserts. Match her locked master: long grey-streaked hair, quilted earth-brown wool, the
  satchel.
- **Exteriors are always in helmets**, all dialogue on suit radio over real Mars plates, with
  Phobos's charcoal grooved ground composited below. Mars hangs huge over every Phobos exterior
  and in every Phobos skylight. **Mars's phase is the audience's clock**: full in Act 1, gibbous
  in Act 2, half in Act 3, a thinning crescent in 19 as the eclipse comes.
- **The waiting-room board** reads NOW SERVING: 0 in every shot until 26. **The brass music box**
  beside it plays a very small march and always stops one bar before the end, until 26.
- **Tickets.** Jax holds 11,402, Pell 11,403. **The donkey's ticket, clipped to its bridle, is
  No. 1** (seen in 2, read in 13). The **man holding a ladder** is always in the back of the
  waiting room, ladder upright, hopeful (2, 4, 26).
- **PIP** travels as his brass-and-cream lamp unit, unbolted from the bridge and carried in a
  canvas postal satchel. His amber lens brightens when he talks; the stamp arm is his
  punctuation. **Every report he files gets an INSERT of its number** (#1,220 to #1,239; E05
  begins at #1,240). Earth is eleven minutes away each way, so any READ takes at least
  twenty-two minutes. **From Phobos his reports come back RETURNED — NO LAWFUL ORIGIN.**
- **Seo** is in her flight helmet or seen from behind; her hands and her wrist screen are her
  inserts. **Lucía** is half inside a machine or behind a visor; the ribbon is her insert.
  **Idris** plays on his hands and a deck of cards. **Mira** is at glass or behind her loupe.
- **Chichikov is never in sharp focus and never speaks.** He has not said a word since the Moon.
  This episode, for the first time, somebody remembers him.
- **The dog** stays on the ship. It is the big grey-muzzled dog from E03; never explain it.
- **Visible-mouth speaking shots are marked [MOUTH n/5].** There are two. Everything else plays
  on helmets, beards, newspapers, backs, glass, hands, paper and listeners.
- **When someone important speaks, hold on the listener's reaction.** That is the default cut.

**Voice and TTS notes**
- Nasreddin: warm, unhurried, amused, an old man who has won every argument he has lost. **No
  accent of any one country, and no accent as the joke.** Pell: soft, precise, English, never
  louder than a teacup; every sentence arrives at its full stop with a little bow. He is never
  sarcastic: he means every kind word, which is what makes them dangerous. Oyuna: low, calm,
  Mongolian-English, plain words; she never hurries. PIP: as E01.
- Oyuna's song (15) is sung, not spoken: a low, throaty epic voice over a two-stringed lute
  (topshuur). Have it composed and, if possible, sung or checked by a performer from the
  Mongolian epic (tuuli) tradition. Allow 30 s for its 60 words.
- Radio colours: suit radio is narrow; the cutter's and the ferry's channels are wider with
  hiss; the Deimos dish line is distant and clean.
- Say it: Phobos "FOH-bos" · Deimos "DAY-mos" · Stickney "STICK-nee" · Nasreddin
  "nas-ruh-DEEN" · Oyuna "OY-oo-na" · Tseren "TSEH-ren" · Wen Jialing "wun jyah-LING" · topshuur
  "TOP-shoor" · ger "gair" · Pavel Ivanovich "PAH-vel ee-VAH-no-vich" · Ulaanbaatar
  "OO-lahn-BAH-tar" · "#1,239" read "one thousand two hundred and thirty-nine".

