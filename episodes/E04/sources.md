# Episode 4 — Sources

Episode 4 mixes the **Nasreddin tales** (Anatolian, Persian and Central Asian joke cycle, via a
Turkish chapbook), **Pu Songling** (Chinese), the **Akan Anansi formula** (Ghana), the
**Mongolian oral epic tradition** and its **Geser** cycle (Mongolian), **Swift** (Anglo-Irish) and
real astronomical history (Kepler, Hall). It carries Gogol's Chichikov (Russian) and Ariosto's
swans (Italian) over from earlier episodes. Six cultures. We take mechanisms, images and traits;
**the plot is ours, and nothing is retold scene by scene.** Editions are those the library cards
record as actually read (`research/library/sources/`) unless marked. No film, animation, game or
modern translation was used.

---

## 1. Nasreddin tales (main source)
**Edition read (card 06):** George Borrow, *The Turkish Jester; or, The Pleasantries of Cogia Nasr
Eddin Efendi* (Ipswich, 1884), Project Gutenberg #16244. Public domain everywhere.
Care notes followed: the comedy is logic, never religion (no sermon, prayer or fasting jokes; the
religious title "Hodja" is never spoken; no turban); no nationality is assigned (his coat is
patched with many countries' cloth); the two offensive tales flagged on the card are not used.

| Taken | Kind | Scene | What is ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| The cauldron that "gave birth" and later "died": hold the other side to its own absurd rule | Mechanism, told by him as an anecdote in a few lines | 13 | Applying it to a law exempt from all law; "It's procedure. Logic is optional here." |
| "I neither buy nor sell the moon" (Borrow's line) | Line, quoted (PD) | 2 | Said to Chichikov, who can't buy anything on a lawless moon |
| What becomes of the old moon: "they make forty stars out of each" | Line, paraphrased | 26 | Applied to Phobos, which really is falling |
| The pot of stones: a corrupted ledger, rounded down "to sound moderate" | Mechanism | 2 | 4,018 days becomes "eleven years. It sounds more moderate." |
| Answers a question with a better question; never admits defeat | Trait | throughout | Every line |
| Riding the donkey backwards | Image | 2 and throughout | **Not checked in Borrow**: it is the best-known image of the tradition (the bank's entry). Our reason for it ("a division of labour") is ours. Verify before lock or credit to the oral tradition. |
| The donkey as a near-character | Image | 2, 13, 26, 27 | Its ticket, "Names get you listed" |
| "Earth got me wrong": the card notes many modern jokes are wrongly attributed to him | Trait (from the card's "what retellings get wrong") | 2 | "A thousand stories with my name on them. I was in about forty." |
| The house with two doors / procedural alibi | Not used here | — | Saved for ep 10 |

**Ours:** his eleven years as a substitute, Wen Jialing, the torn ferry slips, "spite is easier to
explain", sitting backwards in a waiting room, "eleven years for a typo is only a very long joke",
"Somewhere I'm expected".

## 4. The Mongolian heroic epic (tuuli) and the Geser cycle
**Edition read (card 20):** Ida Zeitlin, *Gessar Khan* (Doran, 1927), a literary retelling of
Schmidt's 1839 German translation of the 1716 Mongolian print. US PD. Background: Mongolian tuuli
is on UNESCO's List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding (2009); epic
singers learn very long works by heart and perform them, often to a two-stringed lute (topshuur).

| Taken | Kind | Scene | What is ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| A family line of epic singers who carry very long works by memory, singer to singer | Tradition, as Oyuna's background (canon) | 4, 9, 15 | That the Atlas itself is in their repertoire; "the Office shall hear the Singer"; "a recital is the same every time... the singing isn't" |
| Performance, not recitation: sung in a low epic voice to the topshuur | Image | 15 | The 1877 page as verse, written by us. **Have it composed and checked by a Mongolian tuul performer**; no traditional text is quoted |
| Ger etiquette: don't step on the threshold; salt milk tea for guests | Custom | 4 | Her ger built from the archive's dust sheets |
| Geser | Not used on screen | — | Geser is venerated; kept off screen. The forgetting-and-arrow-letter structure stays reserved for ep 10, per the card |

**Dignity:** Oyuna performs; she is never a database, never exotic. No throat-singing used as
spectacle. Her cousins and a lawyer from Ulaanbaatar are ordinary, competent people.

## 6. Carried over
- **Gogol, *Dead Souls*** (card 02): Chichikov, his patronymic Pavel Ivanovich (book-true), the
  inlaid box. Ours: he is silent; a lawless moon defeats a buyer; Nasreddin remembers him.
- **Ariosto, *Orlando Furioso* 35** (card 03): the swans who carry names to the temple, which E02
  gave to Oyuna's line. Here only implied ("the first Atlas was sung").
- **Homer, *Iliad* XV**: Phobos and Deimos as the war god's attendants; Idris's line only. No
  translation quoted.
- **Raspe** (E01): the fading signature mechanism, reused for a different reason.

## 7. What is wholly ours
The Phobos Exception and its 1877 typo; the Registrar, Window 17, Form 1 and Form Zero; the waiting
room and its long-term waiters; the music box march; Arakawa's silence on Phobos; the Relay Guild,
Wen Jialing and her slips; the substitution; PIP posting himself; the intercept; every line of
dialogue and the sung page.

## 8. Film and design echoes checked
- No screen Nasreddin (Soviet, Turkish or animated films) was used for his look or any line. His
  design is built from the card's brief, not from statues or illustrations of a specific country.
- The waiting-room-as-purgatory idea is common; we avoid the look of any specific film (no endless
  identical corridors, no grey office). Our hall is bright and orange.
- The ticket machine that speaks in slips is not modelled on any screen robot.
