## OVERALL VERDICT: READY AFTER FIXES

This is the funniest script of the four and the best built. The paper voices work. Pell arrives
fully formed. PIP and Window 17 are a real love story told in stamps, and scene 21 does exactly
what the ruling asked: funny first, then it hurts. Every showrunner ruling is honoured on the
page. The arithmetic is unusually careful (4,018 days, 132 slips, 108 + 24 postscripts, 52,596
hours, the 23-second card and the 4 m/s jump are all right).

It is not ready to lock. The reasons:

1. **The farce chain does not close.** Four links cheat (section 5A). The worst: the song that
   undoes the Exception was sung and recorded on Phobos, where the script says twice that
   "nothing said binds" and nothing has lawful origin.
2. **Jax's trade of Lucía's pay is not caused by anything.** Nobody had stopped the post. Pell
   says in the same scene that he never interferes with it. Jax gives away ten months for nothing.
3. **Oyuna has held the answer for six years.** She keeps both versions of every entry and sings
   the Atlas daily. The script never says why she did not know the Exception was a typo.
4. **The ending leaves a wanted crew parked on Deimos for a day with the law back on.** The
   episode's first rule was "the moment you leave, she arrests you". Nothing says why she doesn't.
5. **Hand-over numbers are wrong against E03 v2**: the READ stamp (00:22:04 here, 00:11:04
   there), three missing report numbers, "It fell off. Years ago." against "in the spring".
6. **The picture plan overstates real footage** and puts a donkey on an airless dock.

All of it fits in one revision pass. No act break moves.

## 3. DRAMA AND CHARACTER: **FIX**

**What works.** PIP wants to be received, gets it, and gives it up; "You never gave me yours." /
17 / "I knew that. I wanted it from you." is the best exchange in the season so far. Seo's choice
is shown by hands, not speech, and she can explain it: Oyuna did stay on Phobos until Phobos
changed. Nasreddin is moving, not sentimental: the truth arrives as arithmetic, and "a very long
joke" is his own way of carrying it. Pell is right in both scenes that matter ("I can't let one
person be the law. Not even a kind one."; "She'll need lawyers, Captain. Friends are what people
have instead."). The wage count turning serious ("It isn't friendlier." / three seconds / "Eleven.")
lands.

**What does not.**

1. **The cost is not caused (20).** Jax says: "Keep Miss Ortega's money. Let the post go." But
   nobody has stopped the post. The ferry runs on the eleventh as it has 132 times. Pell answers
   "The Directorate does not interfere with the post." So the trade buys nothing, and raising the
   post at all risks tipping Pell off. The ruling asks for "a real cost". As written it is a cost
   Jax invents. Lucía would be right to leave. Fix: make Pell hold the post and name the price
   (MUST 2).
2. **Oyuna's six years (9, 14, 15).** She tells Mira "Someone always writes it down. Then
   someone else writes over it. I keep both." She sings the Atlas every day. She therefore holds
   "Phobos. Expected. Pending." and the current "excepted". The reveal in 14 ("No, it isn't.")
   plays as if she has known all along, which makes her six years, and her silence in scenes 4
   and 9, a writer's convenience. Fix: what is new to her in 14 is that there is **nothing else**
   behind the Exception (MUST 3).
3. **The guest leaves without touching the crew.** Rulebook: the guest changes a crew member and
   leaves owed or owing. Jax sits backwards beside him twice and learns "ask the rule where it
   came from", but after 17 they never share a frame. The outline says the crew owe him; nobody
   on screen does. One wordless beat would do it (NICE 3).
4. **Mira's turn is one look.** "Mira looks at Oyuna, and doesn't look away quickly enough" (18)
   and "Pell was right about one thing" (27) are good. Nothing in between shows her weighing it.
   Acceptable for a farce; E05 and E06 must pick it up.

**Would a first-time viewer care?** Yes about PIP and about the old man. Less about Jax, who is
the straight man for 34 minutes and then is handed the tag.

**The cheats, and a one-line repair for each.**

- **L12: the evidence was made on Phobos.** Oyuna tells Jax "Nothing binds on Phobos, Captain.
  Not even a name" (4). Pell says "Nothing said on Phobos binds anyone" (8); it is why Arakawa
  writes. The song was sung in the vault and recorded at Window 17. By the script's own rules it
  is a thing said on Phobos, with no lawful origin. Pell would say so at once. Let him, and beat
  him with the cauldron. In 24, after "Attached: the Singer.":
  > PELL: Sung on Phobos. Nothing said there binds.
  > MIRA: It doesn't have to bind. It has to be heard. "The Office shall hear the Singer."
  > PIP: And it is being said here, Auditor. I am saying it now.

  This also gives Mira the winning move with Oyuna's rule, which her arc needs.
- **L6a: substitution is an open door.** If one in and one out satisfied the machine for Wen,
  then any of Oyuna's rescuers, or one of the crew, could be her stand-in. Nobody asks. A viewer
  will. In 17, after "The machine was satisfied.":
  > JAX: So one of us sits down, and she walks out.
  > NASREDDIN: She learns. Next morning there was a new slip. "Substitutions: use Form 1."
- **L6b: the count falls anyway.** Jax holds 11,402 and Pell 11,403. Both leave before the
  correction (22). By Nasreddin's own rule Wen's departure "comes undone" the moment they go. In
  18, add to Pell's Form Zero speech: "…stamped out today, lawfully, as if you had never come.
  The count won't feel a thing." That covers Jax, Pell and Pell's yearly visits in nine words.
- **L8: the bargain is made where nothing binds.** "We accept." / "Done." / the stamp coming down
  are all on Phobos, where Oyuna's signature faded an hour earlier. Move the stamp. In 20:
  > PELL: Done. The Commander's officer will stamp it at the edge. Nothing I stamped in here

  Then the officer in 22 stamps Form Zero instead of ticking a box. It also makes "Trust me."
  true for both sides: neither is bound until they are four kilometres up.
- **L10: let the recall follow from L3.** Pell: "I don't interfere with the post. I recall it.
  And a recall lodged from here would come straight back to me, stamped. So: in person, at

- **L13: why not send a second message?** Pell has 100 seconds before Earth sets. Add after "No.

**Does the typo produce every consequence claimed?** Yes for the lawlessness, the bounced
reports, the closed form, the correction and the ending. The standing order and Form Zero are
separate inventions, honestly labelled ("Not a law. A habit."), and need the patches above.

**1877.** Asaph Hall found Deimos on the night of 11 August 1877 and Phobos on the night of 17
August (12 and 18 August by Universal Time), so "Six nights on" and the order (Dread first, then
Fear) are right. Sources: https://science.nasa.gov/mars/moons/phobos/ ,
https://science.nasa.gov/mars/moons/deimos/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asaph_Hall . Kepler to
1877 is 267 years and Swift to 1877 is 151: both correct. Three small things:
- The song says each moon was "found, and named". The names came afterwards, suggested by
  Henry Madan of Eton from *Iliad* XV. This is poetic compression and "What was real" does not
  repeat it. Acceptable; say "and will be named" if the showrunner wants it exact.
- The card reads "SECOND MOON OF MARS". Phobos was the second found, but astronomers number it
  Mars I. Make the card "SECOND FOUND".
- "The first sale on Phobos since 1877" (26) and "for the first time since 1877, the last bar"
  imply a sale and a finished march in 1877. The Exception is as old as the moon's record. Say
  "the first sale on Phobos, ever."
- A free gift: Pell's "the eleventh" and ferry day "the eleventh" match the night Hall first saw
  a moon of Mars. Nobody says so. PIP could, in "What was real".

**Three things the script gets wrong about its own sky.**

1. **A full Mars means the Sun is down.** Seen from the Mars-facing side of Phobos (where
   Stickney is), Mars is full only when the Sun is behind the moon. Scenes 1, 11 and 12 are
   headed DAY with "hard white sun" under a full or gibbous Mars. They should be lit by Mars
   alone. That is not dark: a full Mars gives roughly a thousand lux, a heavily overcast day,
   all salmon. It is a better and truer look, the searchlight in 12 gains from it, and the Sun
   then rises for the first time in 19, on the order. The rest of the clock (half, crescent,
   eclipse, the ring of light) is right.
2. **The occultation clock (23–25).** The punch-up asked whether Earth can be hidden from Deimos
   and visible from Phobos at once. **In general, yes**: Earth is hidden from Deimos for about 84
   minutes an orbit and from Phobos for about 54, at different times. **By this script's own
   clock, no**: a nine-hour ferry that leaves during Phobos's eclipse meets Deimos on the sunward
   side of Mars, where Earth cannot be behind it. No viewer will work this out, and "What was
   real" claims none of it. Keep the stopwatch; do not add the claim to the end note. Note also
   that the Office faces Mars for ever, so "Phobos, where Earth is still in view" needs a dish on
   the far side.
3. **Tiny gravity is used for gags and ignored for tea.** The coffee does not tremble, the
   card falls for 23 s, two fingers lift Mira thirty metres, PIP weighs 3 g. But tea is poured,
   stove smoke rises in "a thin line", people walk, stroll and sit, and a donkey stands still. At
   this gravity a poured stream takes six seconds to fall a hand's width and smoke does not rise.
   Pick a convention and write it into the standing rules (weighted soles; lidded bowls). Better,
   make one of them a joke: in 4 Oyuna tips the pot and waits, not looking, while the tea takes
   its time. "The tea is good" then has a picture.

**A gift.** Phobos's gravity stops winning about 3.6 km above its Mars-facing side (my
arithmetic from the fact-sheet masses). The script puts the edge of the Exception "four
kilometres up". Mira could say so.

## Would a stranger watch all of it and click Episode 5?

Yes, with one soft minute in each half. The first eight minutes are the fastest in the series:
a problem in eight seconds, a new comic voice every ninety, and a machine that answers in rim
shots. A stranger does not need the earlier episodes to enjoy a queue that has not moved since
1877 or an auditor who takes a ticket. They may drift at the stove in scene 9 and again when the
standing order is explained in 17, and on a phone they will miss some of the paper. But the back
half pays them well: the auction, a lamp posting himself and being handed a receipt for it, a
man who cannot say no saying it to the speed of light, and an old man turning round. The tag is
the best hook the season has had, because it is seven words long and the stranger already knows
enough to feel it: the woman who remembers every name has looked at the captain and found
nothing. They click. What they should not be given a chance to notice on the way is that the
song which freed everyone was sung where nothing counts, and that the captain gave away his
engineer's wages for a ferry that was leaving anyway. Fix those two and this is the episode to
show people first.
