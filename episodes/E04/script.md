# SECRET UNIVERSE
## Episode 4 — "The Phobos Exception"
### Shooting script, draft 1 (head writer; for punch-up and independent critic)

Running time: about 36:25 honest (scene targets add up to 36:15 including "What was real" at 0:40, plus the 0:10 title; model: dialogue at 155 wpm, 0.4 s per speaker change, plus each scene's slips, cards, holds and insert-only action). 4,314 spoken words (counted by script: dialogue lines only, including the sung page and "What was real"; cues, parentheticals, action, slips and cards excluded). 29 units (cold open, 27 scenes, tag), plus "What was real". Acts: cold open 1:30 · Act 1 7:25 · Act 2 8:50 · Act 3 8:10 · Act 4 9:00 · tag 0:40. Jax V.O. is about 4% of the words.
Visual style: photoreal characters and sets composited with real NASA and ESA/Hubble footage,
staged mostly as cinematic stills with camera moves and limited motion. Lip movement is scarce
or absent. Phobos and Deimos are THIN on real footage, so the moons' surfaces are composites on
real Mars-from-orbit plates and the two real HiRISE Phobos stills; every interior is a
generated photoreal set. **Bright by default:** Mars fills the sky over Phobos and pours
salmon-orange light through every skylight. Each place has its own colour world (see
`casting-notes.md`).

**How to read the headers.** Each unit lists its number, setting, thread (A = the job and the
legend: getting Oyuna out · B = the crew changing toward each other: PIP and the Registrar,
Mira and Oyuna, Lucía and Seo, Jax and Nasreddin · C = the spy, Pell and Arakawa, Voss and
Chichikov, Jax's past), honest target seconds, visual class (REAL FOOTAGE WIDE / PHOTOREAL SET
STILL / PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT / MONTAGE / DATA VISUAL), the real property of the place
used, and a STAGING note saying how it plays without lip-sync.

**Standing staging rules (they apply everywhere unless a scene says otherwise)**
- **Everyone on Phobos and Deimos talks on paper.** It is the house style of the two moons and
  the comedy of the episode. All slip, card and board text is set by us in post as overlays,
  never generated inside an image.
  - **The Registrar** is a machine behind the frosted glass of Window 17: brass drums, green
    enamel, card indexes, a printer slot and a desk bell. She "speaks" only in printed slips in
    typewriter capitals. Every slip is an INSERT, preceded by one *ding*. Nobody reads her slips
    aloud unless the script says so.
  - **Commander Arakawa does not speak on Phobos** ("nothing said here binds anyone"). She writes
    in block capitals in a small black notebook and holds the page up. Every page is an INSERT.
  - **Wen Jialing** speaks only through her dispatch printer: slips torn off and slid across a
    desk (INSERT). The Relay Guild only counts what is logged.
- **Nasreddin sits backwards.** On benches, on his donkey's saddle, on the ferry: always facing
  the wall or the way he came, back to the room. His great grey beard covers his mouth anyway.
  Play him on the back of his hat, his hands, one eye over his shoulder, and the listener. He
  turns round to face the room exactly once (26).
- **Auditor Pell is always behind something:** the folded *Directorate Gazette*, a teacup held
  at his chin, or his own back. His eyes over the paper are his close-up. His furled umbrella
  goes everywhere, including onto a moon with no weather.
- **Oyuna** speaks from inside the ger with the door half open, from behind her lute, or with
  her back to us under the Mars skylight. Her hands (tea bowl, lute strings, the parcel) are her
  inserts. Match her locked master: long grey-streaked hair, quilted earth-brown wool, the
  satchel.
- **Exteriors are always in helmets**, all dialogue on suit radio over real Mars plates, with
  Phobos's charcoal grooved ground composited below. Mars hangs huge over every Phobos exterior
  and in every Phobos skylight. **Mars's phase is the audience's clock**: full in Act 1, gibbous
  in Act 2, half in Act 3, a thinning crescent in 19 as the eclipse comes.
- **The waiting-room board** reads NOW SERVING: 0 in every shot until 25. **The brass music box**
  beside it plays a very small march and always stops one bar before the end, until 25.
- **PIP** travels as his brass-and-cream lamp unit, unbolted from the bridge and carried in a
  canvas postal satchel. His amber lens brightens when he talks; the stamp arm is his
  punctuation. **Every report he files gets an INSERT of its number** (#1,220 to #1,231; E05
  begins at #1,240). Earth is eleven minutes away each way, so any READ takes at least
  twenty-two minutes. **From Phobos his reports come back RETURNED — NO LAWFUL ORIGIN.**
- **Seo** is in her flight helmet or seen from behind; her hands and her wrist screen are her
  inserts. **Lucía** is half inside a machine or behind a visor; the ribbon is her insert.
  **Idris** plays on his hands and a deck of cards. **Mira** is at glass or behind her loupe.
- **Chichikov is never in sharp focus and never speaks.** He has not said a word since the Moon.
  This episode, for the first time, somebody remembers him.
- **The dog** stays on the ship. It is the big grey-muzzled dog from E03; never explain it.
- **Visible-mouth speaking shots are marked [MOUTH n/5].** There are two. Everything else plays
  on helmets, beards, newspapers, backs, glass, hands, paper and listeners.
- **When someone important speaks, hold on the listener's reaction.** That is the default cut.

**Voice and TTS notes**
- Nasreddin: warm, unhurried, amused, an old man who has won every argument he has lost. **No
  accent of any one country, and no accent as the joke.** Pell: soft, precise, English, never
  louder than a teacup; every sentence arrives at its full stop with a little bow. Oyuna: low,
  calm, Mongolian-English, plain words; she never hurries. PIP: as E01.
- Oyuna's song (14) is sung, not spoken: a low, throaty epic voice over a two-stringed lute
  (topshuur). Have it composed and, if possible, sung or checked by a performer from the
  Mongolian epic (tuuli) tradition. Allow 30 s for its 60 words.
- Radio colours: suit radio is narrow; the cutter's and the ferry's channels are wider with
  hiss; the Deimos dish line is distant and clean.
- Say it: Phobos "FOH-bos" · Deimos "DAY-mos" · Stickney "STICK-nee" · Nasreddin
  "nas-ruh-DEEN" · Oyuna "OY-oo-na" · Tseren "TSEH-ren" · Wen Jialing "wun jyah-LING" · topshuur
  "TOP-shoor" · ger "gair" · Pavel Ivanovich "PAH-vel ee-VAH-no-vich" · Ulaanbaatar
  "OO-lahn-BAH-tar" · "#1,231" read "one thousand two hundred and thirty-one".


### 2 · THE WAITING ROOM
**Thread:** A/C · **Target:** 95 s · **Visual:** PHOTOREAL SET STILL / PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT · **Place:** Mars through the skylight
**STAGING:** The first spectacle: a hall the size of a railway station, cut into the crater
wall, under a vast round skylight full of Mars. Salmon-orange light on teal benches, brass
rails and plants grown wild. Nasreddin is introduced from behind, sitting backwards; his lines
play on the back of his enormous hat, on one eye over his shoulder, on the donkey, and on Jax
and Mira listening. Chichikov is a soft grey shape at the far end. INSERTS: Jax's ticket, the
dispatch slip, the box of torn halves, the contract fading in the inlaid box. The music box
stops one bar short under the first line.

```
INT. PHOBOS EXCEPTION OFFICE — WAITING ROOM — DAY

A hall as big as a railway station, cut into the rock, under a
round skylight full of Mars. Salmon-orange light lies across rows
of teal benches, brass rails, and plants that have been allowed to
do what they like for a long time. A tea urn. On one bench, a big
clay pot full of pebbles.

On the wall, a flip-number board: NOW SERVING: 0. Beside it a
little brass music box plays a very small march. It stops one bar
before the end. A silence. It begins again from the top.

A scattering of silent people who have been here a long time: a
lawyer with a briefcase, a choir in matching scarves, two young
cousins, a man holding a ladder.

Jax takes a ticket from a brass arm.

INSERT: the ticket. 11,402.

                         NASREDDIN (O.S.)
          Don't take a ticket. It encourages
          them.

On a bench facing the wall, sitting the wrong way round with his
back to the room: an old man in a coat patched with a dozen
countries' cloth, under an enormous felt hat. Beside him stands a
small grey donkey with a ticket clipped to its bridle.

This is NASREDDIN.

                         JAX
          Sorry. Why are you sitting
          backwards?

                         NASREDDIN
          Earth says I ride my donkey
          backwards because I'm a fool. I ride
          backwards so I can see where I've
          been. The donkey watches where we're
          going. It's called a division of
          labour.

                         JAX
          And where are you going?

                         NASREDDIN
          Nowhere. I'm waiting. They owe me a
          number.

                         MIRA
          How long have you waited?

His hand finds the pot of pebbles without looking.

                         NASREDDIN
          One stone a day. The pot says four
          thousand and eighteen. I say eleven
          years. It sounds more moderate.

                         JAX
          Eleven years. Why?

                         NASREDDIN
          Spite. It keeps well.

                         MIRA
          You're Nasreddin. There are a
          thousand stories about you.

                         NASREDDIN
          There are a thousand stories with my
          name on them. I was in about forty.
          Earth gives me every joke it can't
          find an owner for. I send most of
          them back.
                    (one eye over his shoulder, at Jax)
          And you? What are you waiting for?

                         JAX
          Nothing. I'm in a hurry.

                         NASREDDIN
          That's the same thing, faster.

A pneumatic tube on the wall goes THUNK. A brass capsule. He opens
it without turning round.

INSERT: a printed slip. FERRY, THE 11TH. YOUR SEAT IS RESERVED. —
WEN, CHIEF DISPATCHER, RELAY GUILD, DEIMOS.

He tears it in two and drops both halves into a box at his feet.

INSERT: the box. It is full of torn halves.

                         JAX
          Your ride?

                         NASREDDIN
          Every month. I don't go.

At the far, soft end of the hall, a plump grey man with a teacup
and a small inlaid box. Nasreddin raises a hand, still facing the
wall.

                         NASREDDIN (CONT'D)
          Pavel Ivanovich! Still here?

INSERT: a soft grey hand lifts its teacup an inch.

                         JAX
          Who's that?

                         NASREDDIN
          Chichikov. He's been trying to buy
          this moon for a week. Nothing can be
          sold on Phobos. There's no law of
          sale. I told him. I neither buy nor
          sell the moon.

INSERT: in the open inlaid box, a contract. Its fresh signature
fades, stroke by stroke, to nothing.

                         MIRA
          You remember him.

                         NASREDDIN
          Of course. Why wouldn't I?

Jax and Mira look at each other. Neither of them can.

                         JAX
          Chich—

                         MIRA
          Don't.
```
