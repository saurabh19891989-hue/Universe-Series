## OVERALL VERDICT: READY AFTER FIXES

This is the best-written of the three scripts. The cold open hooks in eight seconds. The sane
Quixano is a real, original and moving idea played from Cervantes, not from any film. Sancho is
funny and breaks your heart twice. Idris arrives fully formed. The charge is a true spectacle that
is also an engineering solution. All five showrunner rulings are on the page (table in section 5).

It is not ready to lock, for these reasons:

1. **It contradicts Episode 2 on Seo's mother.** In E02 scene 13 Seo told Lucía, and only Lucía,
   that her mother is dead. Here Lucía accepts "My mother" as someone on Earth who "always"
   writes back.
2. **The premise has a hole.** The next address is "in his head", but Chichikov's card says it is
   in "the station's forwarding book", which is in the room where the cold open happens.
3. **The cure happens before the cause is removed.** Ruling 3 says delisting thins him. He gets
   his colour back in 23, three scenes before the station is relisted in 26.
4. **The dawn clock on the summit does not work**, and two sums (Lucía's wages, the
   twenty-two minutes in 11) are wrong.
5. **The Mars science has six errors**, two of them in or beside the "What was real" note.
6. **None of the "real footage" ground plates is of the place named**, and four key plates are
   not logged in `assets/` at all.

All of it fits one revision pass. The act structure does not need to change.

## 2. COMEDY: **PASS, with fixes**

Each voice is distinct and nobody knows they are funny. Quixano's exact, sad corrections ("With
erosion." "There is no window.") escalate properly into "It is a convective vortex, and I will
prove it!" Sancho is earthy and wounded. Idris embroiders. PIP is literal except twice. Mira
deflates ("Then you drive an accountant up a mountain"). Seo and Lucía are underused but right.
**Jax barely sells**: he has one pitch (3), it is seen through at once, and from 19 to 23 he has
four short lines. See Drama.

**Jokes in the wrong mouth.**
- 24 IDRIS: "Chickpea." Idris embroiders; he does not fumble. He also worked for the man for three
  weeks.
- 4 PIP: "Here I must wait twenty-two minutes to learn if anyone still cares." PIP is literal, not

than Mars turns, so it rises in the west." passes, because explaining things as physics is his

## 3. DRAMA AND CHARACTER: **PASS, with fixes**

**Choices follow from wants.** Quixano wants one honest look and a rest. Sancho wants his master
and, for the first time, has a real island to lose. Mira wants a sane man's choice respected.
Idris wants a ship and a fool worth serving. Seo is ordered one way and presses the key the other
way. All clear.

**The guest is moving, not sentimental.** "I should like something accurate at the end." "Is that
what it is to be sane, Doctor? To look at something blue and be ashamed?" "I'm quite well. I'm
only less." The will is Cervantes II.74 and Sancho's "Blame me, and get up" is the book's own
line, used at exactly the right moment. "He leaned down to look at me." is the right first mad
line. "Teresa would have liked the pension." is the cost, in six words.

**The villain is right about one thing: yes.** "You have waited thirty years for an island that
was promised and never given. We deliver." And Quixano's "It's the accurate one. I've checked."

**Mira's oath: the cost is visible, the choice is not yet forced.** The lifted thumb, the shaking
hand and "Like lying. Then I checked." are right, and ruling 4 is met. But the kiosk says only
STATE WHAT YOU SAW. Nothing stops the woman who "says only what is true" from stating "I saw him
charge a dust devil." She goes straight to "a giant", so it looks like a choice of wording, not a
sacrifice. Let her try the exact truth first and have the machine refuse it (SHOULD-FIX 5).

**Jax is a passenger in his own Act 3.** His one real choice (turning down the free address)
happens off screen and is reported in 18. In 20–23 the cure is Sancho's lie, Idris's embroidery,
Mira's truth, Seo's warning and the weather. Give the salesman one visible act: he keeps the deal
of 18 by starting to sell and stopping himself (SHOULD-FIX 6).

**Does he stay "both"?** After 23 Quixote mostly plays as plainly mad (the enchanters in white).
Only 27 shows the lucid man still in there ("Then it was a survey."; "Sancho told me."). One line
in 25 would honour Mira's side: "Surveyors, Sancho. I shall charge them as enchanters."

**Lucía and Seo.** Lucía is now so close (timed replies, the hidden screen, "How do you know
that?") that the season plan's ep 6 ("suspicion lands on Idris") is under strain. Let her be
wrong about *who* Seo answers to: one line pointing at Voss, which also loads the gun for Idris,
the ex-Voss guide (SHOULD-FIX 7).

**Would a first-time viewer care and click?** Yes. See the last section.

## 4. CLARITY: **FIX**

**A newcomer can follow it.** Delisting is explained in two lines by Sancho (1) and thinning in
two by Mira (2). The rule for getting back on the map is planted plainly by PIP (7). Four
callbacks (hatchet, #1,204, section twelve, "courageous") all work as jokes without E01–E02.

**Three holes.**
1. **The forwarding book (MUST-FIX 2).** Why drive four thousand kilometres for an address that
   is written down in the post office?
2. **The dawn clock (MUST-FIX 4).** What happens at first light if he is not there? As written,
   lateness means no signature, which is what the crew wants. Lucía's "The Sun's on the kiosk in
   two minutes" is false tension.
3. **Nobody says "Don Quixote" until 29:13** (scene 26; I searched the dialogue). The E02 critic
   made the same point about Kaguya. Here the delay can be a design: the name is first spoken
   when he claims it. Keep that payoff, but let a stranger say the name once in the cold open and
   have Quixano decline it (SHOULD-FIX 1).

**Smaller points.** 19's insert is signed "— P.": a newcomer has no idea who that is; sign it
"— Auditor Pell" once. 21: why not call the ship? (SHOULD-FIX 8.) 9 and 26 disagree about which
part of the map is grey (SHOULD-FIX 9).

**Exposition in disguise (quoted).**
- IDRIS (4): "Idris al-Rahhal. Navigator. Poet in the evenings, when the evenings allow it. Voss
  hired me to guide their surveyors. They pay on completion. Completion is the old man's
  signature." A self-introduction nobody asked for. Keep the first three sentences; move the Voss
  half to after the game, where it becomes his reason for losing.
- LUCÍA (25): "Up here it rises before it does anywhere else." A science fact as dialogue, and wrong.
- MIRA (21): "Years ago a dust devil went over a rover called Spirit and blew its panels clean."
  Needed, short, but see section 6 for the wording.
- PIP (7), the rule: passes; it is in his register and it is 30 words.
- JAX (V.O., 29): "An old man got his giants back. A squire gave away his island." A recap of what
  we watched (rule 8). Start the V.O. at "Mira put her name to a giant".

### Does anything reveal too much of the captain's secret?
Jax learns nothing; that part is safe. The audience is given, in one episode: the old dog on the
heap that rises for him; the hidden tear; "Whose is he?" / "**Nobody's.**"; the bow turned over
at the horn tips with a string that sings like a bird; "I don't carry bows"; "carrying somebody's
bed" with an **INSERT of the parcel**; NO SUCH PERSON; and "I hoped she'd go easy on mine."
Scene 1 is Odyssey XVII beat for beat. A big Odyssey film came out this year, so those two scenes
are fresh in the audience's mind. Expect "Jax is Odysseus" in the comments under this episode.
The season plan asks for the dog here and the bow in 1 and 8. Cut two things: "Nobody's" → "No
one's. He came with the station." and the parcel INSERT in 13 (keep the line). Then the bed is
only readable looking back from ep 10.

## Would a stranger watch all of it and click Episode 4?

A stranger who clicks on "Don Quixote on Mars" gets a man stamping his own name NO SUCH PERSON
inside ten seconds, and a squire shouting that he apologised to a windmill inside a minute. They
will stay through Act 1 for Sancho and for Idris losing on purpose, though they may drift on the
roof, where a third quiet scene explains what a livelier one could show. The danger is Act 2. It
is ten minutes of very good talk between people whose faces we cannot see, on a road where
nothing happens, in an episode that promised a western. The inn and the blue sunset are lovely
and still; the camp is funny and still. A browsing viewer leaves around minute sixteen. Anyone
who reaches the flickering giant is safe: from there the episode does not put a foot wrong for
ten minutes. The fall, the will, "Yes, master", the antenna, the charge and "He leaned down to
look at me" are the best run this show has written, and the kiosk gives the scientist a price to
pay that we can see in her hand. The tag does three jobs in fifty seconds and the last picture is
a coin turning gold. They will click Episode 4, because a woman on a small moon remembers every
name and we have just watched a machine fail to find the captain's. Fix the mother, the book and
the clock so regulars are not thrown out of the story; put one dust devil and some eyes into
Act 2 so strangers reach the plain; and be honest in the last forty seconds about which Mars was
real.
