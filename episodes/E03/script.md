# SECRET UNIVERSE
## Episode 3 — "The Knight of the Red Planet"
### Shooting script, draft 1 (head writer; for punch-up and independent critic)

Running time: about 35:58 honest (scene targets add up to 35:48 including "What was real" at 0:45, plus the 0:10 title; model: dialogue at 155 wpm, 0.4 s per speaker change, plus each scene's stated silences, holds and insert-only action, listed per unit in `outline.md` section 3). 4,341 spoken words (counted by script: dialogue lines only, including "What was real"; cues, parentheticals and action excluded). 31 units (cold open, 29 scenes, tag), plus "What was real".
Visual style: photoreal characters, sets and vehicles composited with real NASA Mars footage,
staged mostly as cinematic stills with camera moves and limited motion. Lip movement is scarce
or absent. **Bright by default:** Mars by day is butterscotch sky over rust, ochre and violet
rock; La Mancha Station is whitewash, cobalt shutters, terracotta and hydroponic green.

**How to read the headers.** Each unit lists its number, setting, thread (A = the job and the
knight · B = Mira and Jax; Idris and Sancho; Lucía and Seo · C = Seo the rat, Voss and
Chichikov, Jax's past), honest target seconds, visual class (REAL FOOTAGE WIDE / PHOTOREAL SET
STILL / PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT / MONTAGE / DATA VISUAL), the real Mars property used, and a
STAGING note saying how it plays without lip-sync.

**Standing staging rules (they apply everywhere unless a scene says otherwise)**
- **La Mancha Station is full of dust.** Since the delisting no parts come, and the air filters
  have failed: fine red dust hangs indoors like smoke in a saloon. **Everyone indoors at the
  station wears a dust scarf over nose and mouth**, with goggles pushed up on the forehead.
  That is the western look, and it hides every mouth in Act One for free.
- **Outdoors on Mars everyone is in a pressure suit** with a tinted visor (gold for the crew,
  pale blue for Voss). All outdoor dialogue is suit radio over real footage. The one exception
  is Quixote's barber's basin, bolted over his helmet from scene 22 on.
- **Quixano is the colour of the dust.** From the cold open to scene 23 his skin, beard,
  cardigan and suit are graded to near-monochrome rose-grey (about 70% desaturated); in close
  inserts his hands are faintly translucent (the grain of the table shows through). Colour
  comes back in 23, as colour only: **never make him look younger.** His long drooping grey
  moustache covers his mouth completely, so his close-ups play like the Baron's.
- **Sancho is usually seen from behind**, round on top of very long legs, perched on Dapple, or
  as his hands with food in them (an onion, bread, a squeeze-pouch of wine). His hands are his
  signature insert. He cannot read or write (Cervantes); every document he receives is read to
  him.
- **Idris plays on his hands** (cards, rings, the bow) and on his eyes above an indigo silk
  scarf. He shuffles while he talks.
- **Chichikov is never in sharp focus and never speaks.** He has not said a word since the Moon,
  and nobody but PIP notices. His messages are cards and letters in his looping hand (INSERT),
  delivered by small white Voss survey drones.
- **PIP** is the amber lens on Jax's wrist in the field and on the bridge aboard. **Every report
  he files gets an INSERT of its number** (#1,215 cold open, #1,216 in 4, #1,217 in 5, #1,218 in
  23, #1,219 in 26). Earth is eleven minutes away each way today, so any READ takes at least
  twenty-two minutes.
- **Seo** is in her flight helmet or seen from behind in the pilot's seat; her hands and her
  wrist screen are her inserts. **Lucía** is half inside a machine or behind a welding visor;
  the red ribbon on her wrist is her insert.
- **The dog** is old, grey-muzzled and flea-bitten, a big rough-coated working dog of no breed
  anyone can name. It has a fixed spot: the compost heap by the hydroponics drain. It ignores
  everyone, and stands only for Jax. Outdoors it rides in Dapple's basket inside a clear bubble
  Sancho made from a water-cooler bottle. **Never explain the dog.**
- **Visible-mouth speaking shots are marked [MOUTH n/5].** There are two. Everything else plays
  on scarves, visors, moustaches, backs, hands, objects and listeners.
- **When someone important speaks, hold on the listener's reaction.** That is the default cut.

**Voice and TTS notes**
- Quixano / Quixote: older, precise, courteous English with a Castilian formality, **no comic
  accent**. Quixano is soft and flat; Quixote, from 23, rings. Same voice, different energy.
- Sancho: warm, quick, earthy, lower register; he talks with his mouth full. **No accent as the
  joke.** Idris: smooth, musical baritone, Iraqi-English, enjoying every word; no comic accent.
  Ilse Voss: cool, light, Nordic-English, unhurried. Separate Sancho and Idris widely: they
  trade lines fast in 13 and 24.
- Radio colours: suit radio is narrow; the post coach cab is dry; the ship-to-ground link is
  wider with a faint hiss; the kiosk has no voice (text only).
- Say it: Quixano "kee-HAH-no" · Quixote "kee-HOH-tay" · Sancho Panza "SAN-cho PAN-za" ·
  Rocinante "ro-see-NAN-tay" · Dulcinea "dul-sin-AY-a" · El Toboso "el to-BO-so" · Chicharrón
  "chee-cha-RRON" · Caraculiambro "ka-ra-koo-lee-AM-bro" · Pandafilando "pan-da-fee-LAN-do" ·
  Cide Hamete Benengeli "SEE-day a-MEH-tay ben-en-HEH-lee" · al-Rahhal "al-rah-HAAL" ·
  Noctis Labyrinthus "NOK-tis lab-ih-RIN-thus" · Tharsis "THAR-sis" · Phobos "FOH-bos" ·
  "#1,218" read "one thousand two hundred and eighteen".

# ACT ONE — "Alonso Quixano the Good"

### 1 · LA MANCHA STATION — THE COURTYARD
**Thread:** A/C · **Target:** 65 s · **Visual:** PHOTOREAL SET STILL / INSERT · **Mars:** the dome; dust; delisting cuts supply
**STAGING:** A bright courtyard under a dusty pressure dome: whitewash, terracotta pots,
hydroponic beds of vivid green, a drain, and a compost heap. Through the dome, the real canyon
wall. Jax and Sancho walk; scarves up, played as a two-shot from behind and then on the dog.
The dog's rise is three stills: head up, forelegs, standing. Jax's hand to his eye is ONE fast
INSERT, played as wiping dust. Do not linger, do not score it, do not explain it.

```
INT. LA MANCHA STATION — COURTYARD — DAY

Sancho leads Jax past green beds under a dome the colour of weak
tea.

                         SANCHO
          Before the Atlas took us off, we had
          everything. Travellers. Wine. A
          barber. Then no ships, no post, no
          parts. The air filters went in a
          week.

                         JAX
          You're Sancho Panza.

                         SANCHO
          Earth says fat and stupid.

                         JAX
          I didn't say—

                         SANCHO
          I'm fat.

                         JAX
          And him?

                         SANCHO
          First the giants went. Then the
          enchanters. Then Dulcinea. Last
          Tuesday he looked at Rocinante and
          said, "That is a rover."

                         JAX
          It is a rover.

                         SANCHO
          I know it's a rover! I've known for
          thirty years it's a rover! That isn't
          the point of it!

They pass the drain. On the compost heap lies an old dog, grey
in the muzzle, chin on its paws, flies in its coat.

It lifts its head as Jax goes by.

It gets its forelegs under it. Shakes. Stands.

Sancho stops dead.

                         SANCHO (CONT'D)
          He hasn't stood up in a year.

Jax looks at the dog a moment too long, and turns away.

INSERT: the back of Jax's hand, quickly, at the corner of his
eye. Dust.

                         JAX
          Fine animal. Whose is he?

                         SANCHO
          He came with the station. He won't
          eat from anyone.

                         JAX
          He should.

                         SANCHO
          Where one door shuts, señor, another
          opens. That's what my mother said.
          She never said what to do when they
          all shut at once.

The dog's eyes follow him all the way to the door.
```

INSERT: the lamplight comes through his palm, faint and pink,
