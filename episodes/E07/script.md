# UNIVERSE UNDELIVERED
## Episode 7 — "The Spider's Share"
### Shooting script, draft 1 (writer's draft, for punch-up and the independent critic)

Running time: about 35:55 honest (scene targets add up to 35:45 including "What was real" at 0:40, plus the 0:10 title; model: dialogue at 155 wpm, 0.4 s per speaker change, plus each scene's stated silences, holds and insert-only action, about 6½ minutes in all, most of it in the montage, the two Bennu surface scenes and the text-only tag). Cold open 1:25 · Act 1 8:30 · Act 2 7:55 · Act 3 7:10 · Act 4 9:30 · tag 0:35. 4,137 spoken words (counted by script: dialogue lines only, including "What was real"; cues, parentheticals, slips, cards and action excluded); Jax's V.O. 226 words (5%). 29 units (cold open, 27 scenes, tag), plus "What was real". No scene over 2:20 (the longest is 6). Time inside *The Unscheduled*: about 5:55 (16%).
Visual style: photoreal characters and sets composited with real NASA footage, staged mostly as
cinematic stills with camera moves and limited motion. Lip movement is scarce or absent. Bennu is
RICH in real footage (OSIRIS-REx) and carries the web, the landing pad and the throw; Ceres and
Vesta exist only as Dawn stills, so the town, the Exchange, the hall and the kitchen are generated
sets, and the salt flats are generated wides matched to the real Occator stills.

**How to read the headers.** Each unit lists its number, setting, thread (A = the con and the
legend · B = the crew changing toward each other: Lucía and her mother, Idris under suspicion,
Lucía's wages · C = the spy, the Voss family, the parcel's first label, Jax's past), honest target
seconds, visual class (REAL FOOTAGE WIDE / GENERATED WIDE / PHOTOREAL SET STILL / PHOTOREAL
CLOSE-UP / INSERT / MONTAGE / DATA VISUAL), and a STAGING note saying how it plays without
lip-sync.

**Standing staging rules (they apply everywhere unless a scene says otherwise)**
- **The catcher is the lip-sync plan.** On Ceres water is money and Voss charges for what you
  breathe out, so indoors in public everyone wears a *catcher*: a light half-mask over nose and
  mouth with a little flask at the jaw that wrings the water out of your breath. Visitors are
  issued plain grey ones at Arrivals. Residents' are painted and embroidered, bright, each one
  different: the town's folk art. Wrestlers' are full-face masks; the sport grew out of them.
  The crew push theirs up only for silent beats.
- **Catchers come off at home.** In Carmen's kitchen they hang on hooks by the door, so kitchen
  scenes play on hands (cooking, shelling, the spoon she points with), backs, the tap and its
  lock, glasses, the drawer, and the listener.
- **Mattias Voss never wears a catcher** (the Resident can afford to breathe out). His Company
  lines are read from printed answer cards held up in front of his face: card up is the Company,
  card down is himself, played on his eyes or on the listener. On the salt flats he is a suit and
  a voice.
- **Kwaku Anansi talks through a knotted counting cord** held taut between his two hands at his
  lips, and looks through spectacles with too many lenses. Play him on hands, cord and lenses. On
  Ceres he wears a gold catcher and does not take it off. His voice can also arrive down a cable,
  through metal, with no face at all. He lowers the cord to speak once (19).
- **Nothing in the Counting House rests on anything.** Every object hangs on its own thread and
  turns slowly; loose things drift and are caught. The debts are **tallies**: white tiles pressed
  from Occator salt, each stamped with an account number, carried in nets.
- **Exteriors are always in helmets**, with dialogue on suit radio: the salt flats in hard white
  sunlight under a black sky; Bennu over real OSIRIS-REx footage.
- **Jax never drinks the water he is given and never faces the cistern.** Nobody explains it.
- **Oyuna has no voice this episode.** She told a story all night to keep the crew awake near the
  Sun (Episode 6) and is resting it. She speaks three times, in chalk, on a small slate (INSERTS).
- **Chichikov is never in sharp focus and has no lines.** He is a soft grey shape with a teacup
  and his inlaid box, at a far desk, or a grey pressure suit on a cable.
- **PIP travels as the amber lens of a wrist comm** (Jax's or Lucía's), unstrapped onto a table
  when he is the subject, and is the lamp on the dash aboard. **Every report gets an INSERT of its
  number** (#1,250 in the cold open, #1,251 in 27; renumber to follow Episode 6's last report).
- **Lucía**: the red ribbon on her wrist, and from 5 a small steel key tied to it (the gate
  panel's). In 14 and on the old posters, her full-face mask, red and silver.
- **Day cards.** The story runs nine days; a plain card (DAY 1 … DAY 9) marks each jump.
- **When someone important speaks, hold on the listener.**
- **Music:** cumbia (accordion, scraper, drum) is Carmen's radio and the town; highlife guitars
  and an Akan talking drum are Anansi. From 7 the two lock together under the con, and split
  again at 19. All original; quote no existing song.

**Visible-mouth speaking shots: 4 (maximum 5).** Marked **[MOUTH 1]** to **[MOUTH 4]** in the text:
1. Wen, 3, "The parcel people." (her first spoken words in the series)
2. Anansi, 19, "I threw in your mother. Nobody asked me to."
3. Carmen, 26, "Not in this kitchen."
4. Lucía, 26, "That one's ours."

**Voice and TTS notes**
- Anansi: warm, grand, unhurried, exact; he enjoys consonants. An educated Ghanaian English
  register, **never a comic accent**. No contractions when he is being formal, which is nearly
  always. His first line (end of the cold open) is the Akan storyteller's opening formula.
- Carmen: fast, dry, warm underneath; Mexican Spanish is in her rhythm and in "mija" and "Mamá",
  nowhere else. Wen: crisp and quick, a shift supervisor's voice with a laugh in it. Mattias:
  quiet, tired, exact; soft Scandinavian vowels like his father's, with none of the polish. His
  card voice is flatter and a little slower than his own.
- PIP, Jax, Mira, Lucía, Idris, Seo: as before. Jax's V.O. is quicker and drier than his scene
  voice: the narrator of a crime story who thinks he was in charge of it.
- Radio colours: suit radio is narrow; PIP on a wrist is small and bright; Anansi down a cable is
  wide, low and slightly metallic.
- Say it: Kwaku Anansi "KWAH-koo ah-NAHN-see" · Aso "AH-soh" · Ceres "SEER-eez" · Occator
  "oh-KAY-tor" · Bennu "BEN-oo" · Vesta "VESS-tuh" · La Blanca "lah BLAHN-kah" · La Pluma "lah
  PLOO-mah" · La Tercera Ley "lah tehr-SEH-rah LAY" · mija "MEE-hah" · Mattias "mah-TEE-ahs" · Wen
  Jialing "wun jyah-LING" · 4471-C read "four-four-seven-one-C".
