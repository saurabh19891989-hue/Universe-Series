# Episode 7 — "The Spider's Share": sources

Rule (bible 7a): at least three sources from at least two cultures, mixed; mechanisms, images and
traits taken; nothing retold scene by scene. This episode draws on **five** texts from **four**
traditions (Akan, K'iche' Maya, Russian, British), plus the Greek motifs of the season secret.
Card numbers refer to `research/library/sources/`. I worked from the library cards and did not
reopen the scans, so every wording marked **QUOTED** below must be checked against the scan
before lock.

The plot is ours and appears in none of the sources: the water company and the metered breath,
the two desks, the light-delay float between Ceres and Vesta, the Counting House on Bennu, the
salt tallies, Carmen as the planner and guarantor, the throw, the brine, Mattias, the label clue.

## 1. Akan tradition (Asante), *Anansesem* — R. S. Rattray, *Akan-Ashanti Folk-Tales* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1930) — card 11
- **Edition (read by the library):** Rattray's bilingual Twi/English collection, tale 17, "How it
  came about that the Sky-god's stories came to be known as 'Spider-stories'", and his
  introduction on the opening and closing formulas. Rattray d. 1938: public domain in UK/EU/India;
  in the US since 1 January 2026. The stories are traditional and belong to the Akan people;
  **credited on screen** in "What was real" and in the end credits.
- **Taken:**
  - *Character:* Kwaku Ananse, "a mere masterless man" who bought all the stories; brags upward,
    haggles sideways, takes the credit. Ours: a banker on Bennu who owns every story and is losing
    them as places are delisted.
  - *Mechanism:* **the impossible price paid in instalments, with receipts.** Ours: Voss's price
    for the mislaid stories is "four deliveries"; the con itself runs as net after net of tallies,
    each receipted aloud.
  - *Mechanism:* **the staged argument overheard**, so the victim volunteers (the python and the
    palm branch). Ours: Jax and Idris quarrel in the Exchange until the Company itself insists its
    paper is good at face (8).
  - *Mechanism:* **the false weather** (water sprinkled, a leaf held overhead, the hornets invited
    in out of the "rain"). Ours: the captain goes into the bank dripping wet on a world where water
    is money; "It's been raining at the bank" (7, 8).
  - *Mechanism:* **the sticky figure that will not answer**, slapped for its rudeness (the doll
    with yam in its hands). Ours: the stuffed clerk on Bennu's landing pad offering a cup of water
    on a tray; the bailiff strikes the tray and sticks (9, 22).
  - *Mechanism:* **the bonus nobody asked for**: he throws in his own mother. Ours: said in 6;
    turned twice: Carmen threw *herself* in as guarantor (15), and Anansi throws in Lucía's mother
    as the price of her doing nothing (19).
  - *Mechanism:* **the real planner is his wife, Aso, uncredited.** Ours: every trap is Carmen's;
    "And Company" is Aso; he says so at last (24).
  - *Correction of Earth:* most retellings give three prices; Rattray's has four and the mother.
  - **QUOTED (three short formulas from Rattray's English, to verify against the scan):**
    the opening formula, "We do not really mean, we do not really mean, that what we are about to
    say is true" (card: "…(that what we are going to say is true)"); the receipt formula, "My hand
    has touched it; what remains still remains"; the closing formula, "If it be sweet, or if it be
    not sweet, take some elsewhere, and let some come back to me."
- **Care:** the receipt formula is the Sky-god's in the tale. In the script Anansi uses it as his
  own way of giving a receipt and **the Sky-god is never named, shown, quoted as a character or
  joked about**; "I bought every story… for a price" is as near as the script goes. Nyame stays
  off-screen, as canon requires.
- **Not taken:** the four captures themselves (python, leopard, hornets, fairy) are not staged;
  only the leopard is named, in the tiger correction. No picture-book, novel, comic, film or
  television Anansi; in particular nothing of the suited, hatted American "Mr Nancy".

## 2. Akan tradition (Gold Coast), W. H. Barker and Cecilia Sinclair, *West African Folk-Tales* (London: Harrap, 1917) — card 11
- **Edition (read by the library):** tales I–III. US public domain (1917).
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **the pot of wisdom, hoarded, thrown down, and so shared** ("How wisdom became
    the property of the human race"). Ours: the whole town's debt gathered into one net and thrown
    off Bennu so that it spreads beyond recovery; Anansi tells the pot story himself in two
    sentences and recognises the feeling (24).
  - *Trait:* he is not humbled by the pot; he is offended. Ours: "Earth says I learned humility.
    I was furious."
  - *Mechanism:* **"Anansi and Nothing"**: he wears a rich man's clothes and is taken for him.
    Ours: one speech to Jax (19), a seed for the season secret; the murder in that tale is not
    used or mentioned.
  - *Correction of Earth:* this 1917 English version turns the leopard into a tiger. Ours: "An
    Englishman's pen slipped" (7).
- **Not taken:** the plots of the three tales.

## 3. K'iche' Maya, *Popol Vuh* — Lewis Spence, *The Popol Vuh* (London: David Nutt, 1908) — card 18
- **Edition (read by the library):** Spence's English summary of Book II. Public domain. The Popol
  Vuh is sacred to K'iche' communities today; we take two mechanisms and no persons.
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **the lords ask for the trick to be done to themselves, and are not revived.**
    Ours: the Board, pleased with both desks, asks the House to take the entire Ceres book (13);
    the book does not come back.
  - *Mechanism:* **wooden lords set among the real ones.** Ours: nine carved wooden directors in
    the Exchange; the staged insult makes the one living person in the room speak (2, 8).
- **Not taken:** the Twins, Xibalba, its lords, the ball game, any ordeal, any imagery of temples
  or sacrifice. Lucía's home is Mexican-family and Belt-born; nothing Maya is attached to it.

## 4. Nikolai Gogol, *Dead Souls* (1842), tr. D. J. Hogarth (Everyman, 1915; Project Gutenberg #1081) — card 02
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **wealth raised on assets that exist only on a register**: Chichikov buys the
    dead who are still listed as living, to pledge them. Ours: debts sold as dead at a tenth and
    pledged as living at their face, in the same hall.
  - *Character (recurring):* Pavel Chichikov, the forgettable buyer. Ours: silent, soft-focus, the
    "auditor" who is in fact Soren Voss's private collector; he takes two notes.
- **Not taken:** any scene or dialogue of the novel.

## 5. Arthur Conan Doyle, "The Final Problem" (1893; Project Gutenberg #834) — card 13
- **Taken, image only:** the spider motionless at the centre of a web, feeling every quiver. Ours:
  given to *both* spiders: Anansi ("I felt it. I feel all of them") and, in his mouth, Voss ("He
  sits very still at Saturn with a finger on every thread. I know the posture").
- **Not taken:** any character or wording.

## Season-secret motifs (not counted as sources for this episode) — card 15, *Odyssey*
- The sender "smelled of the sea"; Jax will not drink and turns his back on the cistern; "a man
  called Nothing" (Barker) rhymes with Nobody. No Homeric character, name or scene appears, and
  nothing is explained.

## Cultural detail used for design (not texts; to be checked with a knowledgeable reader)
- **Akan:** brass goldweights (figurative weights for gold dust) as the Counting House's weights
  and as the "stories" on their threads; the board game oware on Carmen's table; the atumpan
  talking drum in the score; the adinkra sign *ananse ntontan* (the spider's web) proposed as the
  House's seal. No kente as costume shorthand; no generic "African" dressing.
- **Mexican:** cumbia on the radio and in the hall; the masked-wrestling tradition, moved to a
  few percent of Earth's gravity. Lucía's ring name "La Tercera Ley" is ours; check it against
  real wrestlers' names before lock. No real wrestler, mask design or song is copied.
- **Chinese:** Wen Jialing is original (E04). **Nordic:** the Voss family is original.
