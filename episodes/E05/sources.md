# Episode 5 — "A Gentleman on Venus": sources

Rule (bible 7a): at least three sources from at least two cultures, mixed; mechanisms, images and
traits taken; nothing retold scene by scene. This episode draws on **seven** sources from **six
traditions** (French, Arabic/Persian, Chinese, French-Indian, Russian, Korean), plus Greek motifs
for the season secret and the Venetian mask tradition for design. Card numbers refer to
`research/library/sources/`. The plot (the Viewing, the Reliquary, the Night House and its air
deed, the Lupin Ball, the double, the climb) is ours and appears in none of them.

## 1. Maurice Leblanc, *Arsène Lupin, Gentleman-Burglar* (France, 1905–1907) — card 07
- **Edition read (by the library):** *The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsène Lupin,
  Gentleman-Burglar*, tr. George Morehead (Chicago, 1910), Project Gutenberg #6133. Stories used:
  I "The Arrest of Arsène Lupin" (1905), II "Arsène Lupin in Prison" (1906), IX "Sherlock Holmes
  Arrives Too Late" (1906). **Only these 1905–1907 stories** (canon: rights confidence medium). No
  later novels, no screen, stage, anime or TV version, no poster imagery.
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* the theft announced in advance by letter, with a **postscript of expert
    appraisal** about which piece is a copy ("In Prison"). Ours: the letter is printed on the
    host's invitations; the postscript is withheld and turns out to be a jab (both are copies).
  - *Mechanism:* the **detective hired by the victim is the thief's creature** ("In Prison", where
    a false Ganimard is Lupin's man). Ours: Lupin plays Ganimard himself.
  - *Mechanism:* **the narrator is the thief** ("The Arrest", told by a charming passenger who is
    Lupin). Ours: Lupin narrates the episode as an unnamed guest; the audience can hear it is him.
  - *Mechanism:* **the woman who holds the evidence lets it fall** between ship and pier (the Kodak,
    "The Arrest"). Ours: Mira lets the envelope about Jax fall into the cloud sea; not about Lupin,
    and not about love for the thief.
  - *Image:* the **white rose** taken and kept ("The Arrest"). Ours: the gala's dress code, the
    week-old rose in Ganimard's buttonhole, the calling card left in Jax's pocket.
  - *Trait:* the **scar on the right forearm** that identifies him ("The Arrest"). Ours: Voss paints
    it on every man's arm at the door.
  - *Trait:* "Lupin is caught in the very first story" (card, "What retellings get wrong"). Ours:
    his correction of Earth: "My first story ends with my arrest. I'm rather proud of it."
  - *Trait:* keeps his word to women he respects, enjoys the people he robs, unnerved by someone who
    really sees him (Holmes's look in IX). Ours: his promise to Scheherazade; Voss at the rail is
    the one man who rattles him.
  - *Name:* Inspector Ganimard (Leblanc's detective, public domain).
- **Not taken:** any plot of any story; Nelly; the ship; Thibermesnil's riddle; Holmes (kept for ep 9,
  and Leblanc's use of the name is a separate rights question); period remarks about women; the
  monocle-and-top-hat look of later posters and films.

## 2. *The Thousand and One Nights*, the frame story (Arabic/Persian tradition) — card 08
- **Editions read (by the library):** Andrew Lang, *The Arabian Nights Entertainments* (1898),
  Project Gutenberg #128; Richard F. Burton, *The Book of the Thousand Nights and a Night* vol. 1
  (1885), Project Gutenberg #3435 (frame opening only; his framing and notes not used).
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **the confederate who asks on cue** in agreed words (the sister, Dinarzade /
    Dunyazad). Ours: Idris sits as Scheherazade's questioner because her sister is under Voss
    Heritage contract; Seo watches him do it. The words are ours ("If you're still awake, Lady,
    tell us what happened next").
  - *Mechanism:* **story as ransom, nested** (a life bought a night at a time; the frame repeats
    inside itself). Ours: each year she keeps the Voss heir listening, and the heir renews her air;
    inside her tale a doorkeeper lets a courier wait one more night for a story.
  - *Trait:* **the scholar, not the beauty** (Burton: she had read the annals and collected a
    thousand books). Ours: her salon is a library and an observatory.
  - *Trait / correction:* she volunteered to save the other women. Ours: "Earth says I told stories
    to save my own life. I told them so the next girl wouldn't have to."
  - *Rule:* nobody leaves before the story ends (bible); ours, from the frame's dawn deadline.
  - *Sindbad, voyage 7 (Lang):* the retired hero's last voyage was a **delivery** (a letter and a gift
    for a king). Ours: Idris's line on the bridge (29); a plant for Idris's family legend.
- **Not taken:** the king, the executions, the adultery backstory (kept off screen, and never a
  joke); the merchant-and-jinni tale itself; any harem imagery; Burton's framing. Rimsky-Korsakov's
  *Scheherazade* is not quoted.

## 3. *Journey to the West*, ch. IV (China, c. 1592) — card 12
- **Edition read (by the library):** Timothy Richard, *A Mission to Heaven* (Shanghai, 1913),
  Internet Archive.
- **Taken:** *irony only:* the **title with no rank** ("It has no rank." "Then it must be very
  high." "The position before getting any rank at all"), offered by the envoy of the planet Venus.
  Ours: Ganimard, on Venus, makes the crew "deputies"; Jax: "What rank is that?" "None at all."
  "Then it must be very high." "That's what they all say."
- **Not taken:** any character. Sun Wukong is reserved for ep 8 under the showrunner's ruling; the
  Venus envoy (Taibai Jinxing) is a figure of Daoist worship and does not appear.

## 4. Jules Verne, *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea* (France; Nemo is the Indian prince Dakkar) — card 14
- **Edition read (by the library):** Project Gutenberg #164.
- **Taken:** *image:* **the collector who lives off what empires lost** (Nemo's gold from the
  galleons sunk at Vigo Bay, 1702). Ours, inverted: Voss's Reliquary holds "a crown nobody would
  ransom" and "a figurehead from a bay full of sunken gold", and his father said the world loses
  things "the way a child drops toys". Nemo funds freedom with lost treasure; Voss keeps it.
- **Not taken:** Nemo, the Nautilus, any scene. (Ep 9 owns Verne.)

## 5. Nikolai Gogol, *Dead Souls* (Russia, 1842) — card 02
- **Taken:** the established trait of our recurring Chichikov (polite, forgettable, everywhere). New
  here: even the narrator, a master of disguise, cannot describe him; Voss blanks on his name.

## 6. *The Tale of Hong Gildong* (Korea) — card 17
- **Taken:** the established canon that "Hong Gildong" is Korea's sample name on forms, and Seo's
  papers carry it (E02). New here: Idris recognises the lie as art and keeps it.

## 7. Homer, *Odyssey* (Greece) — card 15 (season secret; motifs only, never named)
- **Taken:** the shape of the season secret: a man who gave up his name; unease at sea. Ours, and
  **original wording**: the line in Scheherazade's tale, "You may keep my name. I have no further
  use for it. And the sea, which keeps everything, kept it." It quotes nothing in Homer, and
  nothing in the episode states who it is about.

## Design source (not a story source)
- **The Venetian *bauta*** (Italy, 18th century): a full-face mask whose jutting jaw let the wearer
  speak, eat and drink anonymously. Used as the house mask of the gala and the episode's lip-sync
  plan. Historical design, not any film's.

## Venus facts
From `research/locations/07-venus.md` [file]: the surface at about 467 °C, hot enough to melt lead;
Magellan radar mapped more than 98% of the surface. From general knowledge, **for the science
check** [gen]: conditions near 50 km are the most Earth-like beyond Earth (about Earth's pressure;
temperate around 50–55 km); the atmosphere is carbon dioxide with sulphuric-acid clouds, so
breathing air is a lifting gas there; Venus rotates retrograde and its rotation (about 243 days) is
longer than its year (about 225 days); the cloud tops super-rotate round the planet in about four
days; Earth is never closer to Venus than about two light-minutes (about 38 million km at closest).
Voss's "more often than once a day" is true of the rotation, not of sunrise to sunrise (about 117
days); keep or soften on the science check.
