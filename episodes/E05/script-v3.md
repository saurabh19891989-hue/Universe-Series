## Episode 5 — "A Gentleman on Venus"
### Shooting script, draft 3 — FINAL, LOCKED (critic fixes and showrunner rulings on the punch-up draft 2)

*(Working title in older files and headers: Secret Universe.)*

Spoken words: **4,081** (counted by script: dialogue lines only, including "What was real";
cues, parentheticals and action excluded; the same counter gives draft 2 its 4,286). 432 cues.
Narrator V.O. 232 words (5.7%). Units: 31 (cold open, 29 scenes, tag), plus "What was real".
Unit numbers are kept from draft 2 so the outline's plant references and the critic's notes still

**How the targets were estimated** (the Episode 2 model, which replaced 155 wpm after the Episode 1
table read): speech at **184 wpm**, plus 0.97 s per speaker cue, plus 1.5 s for each stand-alone
INSERT / STILL / WIDE / CLOSE and each stated beat or silence, plus the picture-only time listed by
hand in each header as **Held** (held wides, silences, silent action, text the viewer must read).
Targets are that sum rounded to 5 s; per-scene numbers are in `revision-notes.md`. Do not slow
Voss or Scheherazade to make them grave: their stillness is in the picture and the silences.

Visual style: photoreal characters and sets composited with real NASA footage where it exists,
staged mostly as cinematic stills with camera moves and limited motion. Lip movement is scarce or
absent.

**What is real footage and what is not.** Venus is THIN (location file 07). The city, the cloud
sea, the gate and every interior are generated. **Three real frames are in the story**, each a
Magellan radar picture shown as a chart, which is what it is: the global view on PIP's console
(cold open; PIA00478, NASA/JPL), the southern-hemisphere view on Scheherazade's desk (2; PIA00008,
NASA/JPL/USGS), and the Alpha Regio radar image on PIP's watch face (25; PIA00147, NASA/JPL). "What
was real" runs over the same verified Magellan stills. The clear band of air the city hangs in is
our invention (the real cloud deck runs from about 48 to about 70 km); nothing on screen or in
"What was real" claims otherwise.

**How to read the headers.** Each unit lists its number, setting, thread (A = the heist and the
legends · B = the crew changing toward each other: Jax and Mira, Idris and Seo, Lucía and PIP ·
C = the spy, Voss, Jax's past, PIP's reader), target seconds, the hand-listed picture-only time
inside that target (**Held**), visual class (REAL FOOTAGE / GENERATED WIDE / PHOTOREAL SET STILL /
PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT / MONTAGE / DATA VISUAL), and a STAGING note saying how it plays
without lip-sync.

**Standing staging rules (they apply everywhere unless a scene says otherwise)**
- **This episode is narrated by its guest.** NARRATOR (V.O.) is Arsène Lupin's own voice, light,
  amused, French. He lies; **the pictures contradict him** (5, 14 five times, 28). On screen he
  spends Acts 1–2 disguised as INSPECTOR GANIMARD, the detective Voss has hired: the same voice,
  aged and slowed. An attentive viewer can catch it. The crew cannot. Jax has no V.O. this episode.
- **Ganimard's heavy grey moustache covers his mouth completely.** Play him on eyes and eyebrows,
  like the Baron. When the moustache comes off (13) it is one silhouette INSERT.
- **The masked gala is the lip-sync plan.** Indoors at Hesperus every guest wears a house mask: a
  full-face lacquered mask after the Venetian *bauta* (gold, ivory or lapis), whose jutting jaw lets
  the wearer talk, eat and drink unseen. **A mask pushed up uncovers the mouth, so masks go up only
  for silent beats** (the four seconds at the end of 17 are the one that matters). Gala masks do not
  muffle the voice.
- **Outside, everyone wears a hooded wrap, goggles and a breathing mask.** The air is carbon dioxide
  with acid haze, but the pressure and warmth a little over fifty kilometres up are close to
  Earth's, so there are no pressure suits. All outdoor dialogue is behind masks. The city rides
  the wind, so there is almost no wind on its terraces.
- **Clock and light.** It is evening and then night by the clock, and full daylight outside, all
  episode: the wind carries the city round the planet in under a week, and it is three days into
  its light (Seo says so in 1). Slug lines give clock time. The sky is a ceiling of even
  gold-white light with no visible Sun and soft shadows; below, a sea of cloud to every horizon.
  Each interior has its own colour world (see casting-notes.md).
- **The gate and the gate drive.** The VENUS GATE is a ring of brass and white light standing in
  space above the cloud; it is seen twice, in the first shot and the last shot before the tag. The
  ship's **GATE DRIVE panel** is a brass panel low on the bridge's centre console, between the
  two front seats, under a hinged cover with an engraved plate that reads GATE
  DRIVE (in-house text, always legible). It is seen four times: closed on arrival (1), open and
  cut (14), closed (26), set by Idris and closed again (29). The dog's place is against it.
- **Jax never looks down.** Near any edge he is framed facing away from the drop, or looking up.
  No one explains it.
- **Scheherazade's lines play on her hands** (writing, turning pages), on the rings of listeners,
  on the dome and the lamp; her face is held in silence. Dignity always: a library and an
  observatory, never a harem, never dancers, never lounging. The threat she lives under is never
  played for a laugh.
- **Ilse Voss speaks rarely and never in company. Tonight she is silent.** Her silver knife lies
  on the arm of her chair while she listens.
- **Chichikov is never in sharp focus.**
- **PIP travels as a lens in a brass pocket-watch case** on Jax's waistcoat chain, and through
  Lucía's wrist comm; aboard ship he is his own console, lens and stamp arm. The lens brightens
  when he talks. **Every report gets an INSERT of its number** (#1,240 in 4, #1,241 in 29; Episode 4
  ends at #1,238, with #1,239 reserved). **A READ stamp counts one way**, from SEND to the moment the
  report is read, on the reader's clock; PIP waits the round trip to learn it. So tonight's
  receipts appear 2.6 seconds after SENT and read 00:00:01.3.
- **Label one** (the fragment) is always the same prop: a torn strip of old brown parcel paper.
  The surviving half shows exactly three things and nothing else: **PIP's small square audit
  stamp with one chipped corner; half a round date mark (an office number and a date twenty years
  ago, no letters); and the last letter of the address line, a slanted "…A" in blue ink at the
  torn edge.** No other letter of any name or place is ever legible. Never show the missing half.
  Voss's two copies show the same three things.
- **The parcel** (seen once, in the cold open) carries thirty-two labels, the newest in a big
  knightly hand, and one pale clean rectangle low down where label one used to be.
- **Lucía** wears the red dress she hates, her boots under it, the ribbon on her wrist; from 9,
  kitchen whites over it. Anything she keeps goes in her boot.
- **Mira wears her brass loupe as a pendant.** Reflections are for silent beats only.
- **When someone important speaks, hold on the listener.**
- **Music:** a swing band with an oud and a qanun in the front row, all original. Do not quote
  Rimsky-Korsakov.

**Visible-mouth speaking shots: 5.** Marked **[MOUTH 1]** to **[MOUTH 5]** in the text:

2. Lupin, 23, "A double-cross is only a favour done rudely."
