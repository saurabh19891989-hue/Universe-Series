## OVERALL VERDICT: READY AFTER FIXES

This is the most confident script of the five. Voss is a first-class villain on his first
appearance and is right about something in every scene. Scene 19 (PIP's stamp) is the best single
scene the series has. Idris and Seo (10, 15) are delicious and end on a true thing. Mira dropping
the file is a real cost, quietly played. The heist is fair in its two big surprises. All the
showrunner's rulings are honoured on the page.

It is not ready to lock, for six reasons:

1. **The sabotage that Episode 6 is built on rests on a thing the series has never shown.** The
   words "gate" and "gate drive" do not occur as travel in E01–E04. The first time a viewer meets
   the gate-drive panel is a silent 3-second insert inside a comic montage. PIP, who is on the
   bridge, somehow does not see it. And Seo walks out of a room the script says three times nobody
   leaves.
2. **PIP's deduction about his reader contradicts the last two episodes.** "One point three. Same
   as always" is false: it was 11:00 in E03 and 22:04 in E04. E03 and E04 also use different rules
   for what a READ time means, and this scene's number depends on which rule is right.
3. **Four small holes in the heist** (none is a cheat on the audience, all are cheap to plug): an
   unplanted key, a thing Lupin knows but could not, an alarm nobody raises, and a double-cross whose
   only stated reason is a joke.
4. **A stranger is not told what the label is a label of.** The parcel is never seen in this
   episode and is first named at 9:30. Scheherazade's name is never spoken at all.
5. **The physics of the Act 4 set-piece argues against itself** ("We are the weight", then the
   weight climbs into the balloon), and four Venus facts are off (section 6).
6. **There is not one second of real footage in the story.** The header promises Magellan and
   Parker views "on screens"; no scene has one.

## 1. HOOK AND PACE: **FIX**

**First 20 seconds / first minute: PASS.** Light, PIP's lens, "Someone is going to steal our label
tonight" at 0:05. Goal and deadline by 0:45. The breakout wide is a real picture. The cold open is
95 s against a 90 s rule; trim "I don't own a dress / I own three" (the dress has five more jokes
coming). What the first minute lacks is the cost: see Clarity.

**No-filler test.** Removable without loss:
- **7 (the card), 20 s.** It is the act-out, but the same information ("Honey, not sugar", "He knew
  before we did") is delivered better 30 seconds later by Voss in person. Fold the tray into the
  end of 6 and end Act 1 on the card insert, not on Mira's shrug.
- **The second half of 6** (rank joke, 15 s). See Comedy.
- **4's first 20 s** (the dress at cards). The scene's turn is the READ receipt; start nearer it.
Everything else turns.

**Over 2½ minutes.** None on the model. **8 (tea) is 2:21 at 155 wpm**, but Voss is written "never
louder than tea"; at 140 wpm it runs 2:34. Cut 20 words (suggest "Everyone has a beginning… I find
that restful", which restates "a short read").

**Same energy in a row.** 5–6–7 are three talk scenes in one room (4:10 with scene 4's car). The
whole of Act 2 (8–13, 9:23) is six conversations: tea, kitchen, whisper, dance, gallery,
walk-and-talk. The first physical danger in the episode is the plank at **22:54**. The genre can
carry that, but only if the talk scenes keep changing temperature; 8→9 is quiet menace followed by
quiet grievance about the same card.

**Thread absent more than 6 minutes.** The salon (Scheherazade, Ilse, the knife: the only life at
stake) is off screen from **5:10 to 12:54, 7:44.** Seo is off screen from 2:40 to 12:54 (10
minutes) and has no reason given for being in the salon when she appears. Fix: a 10-second salon
insert between 6 and 8 (the knife on the chair arm, Idris's first cue, Seo arriving), which also
shortens 10.

**Act turns.** Act 1 (7): soft; the turn is real but lands on a quip. Act 2 (13): the turn is at
the top of the scene and the act ends 90 s later on a watch gag; acceptable. Act 3 (20–21): good.
Act 4 (29): good. Tag: good.

### Honest runtime
House model (155 wpm + 0.4 s per cue + stated holds + title): **35:47.** My parse agrees. The model
undercounts action told in inserts: 18 has eleven inserts and held pictures and claims 10 s of
holds; 14 has eight pictures; 20, 22, 26, 27 and 28 are similar. I count about **1:40** of unstated
picture time, plus about 0:30 for Voss and Scheherazade at their written pace.
**Honest runtime: about 37:45 (range 36:00 at 165 wpm to 38:30).** Inside the 30–40 window, near
the top. The fixes below add about 40 s and the suggested cuts remove about 60.

### The two minutes where we are most likely to lose a viewer
- **About 8:00–9:20 (scene 6 into 7).** Fourth and fifth minutes in the ballroom, low stakes, a
  borrowed joke, then a 20-second card. Nothing has happened to anyone yet.
- **About 19:40–21:00 (the first half of 15, into 16).** The montage has just promised a robbery.
  We get an abstract exchange about records ("It's in no record." "Whose story?" "That's another
  night.") and then a philosophy scene at a rail. Both are good; together they hold the heist off
  for three minutes at the moment the audience wants it. Start 15 on Seo's return (the grease, the
  customs-form line), put Oyuna's line second, and cut 16 to its last four speeches.

## 2. COMEDY: **PASS, with fixes**

Distinct voices: PIP literal, Mira deflating, Lucía blunt, Idris embroidering, Seo deadpan, Oyuna
gentle: all yes. **Jax barely sells** (two attempts, in 2 and 6, both swatted). **PIP has 735 of
4,291 words (17%)**, more than Jax (475); he is the lead comic voice of the episode, which is fine
this once because of 19.

Running gags: the reports escalate properly (a correct form; a report on himself). Chichikov
escalates (the narrator; Voss blanking, then "I have never forgotten anyone in my life"). The
spoon is planted and paid three times and is the best-built gag in the script. The dress repeats
rather than escalates (six beats). "Owes me money" is used three times (18, 29 twice).
"Someone has to" is said by three characters in four places (2, 2, 15, 16); keep it for
Scheherazade and Voss only, so the echo means something.

**Repeated shape across episodes:** Voss's "do give him my regards" (16) is Pell's act-out line from
E04 scene 13 ("Do give it my regards"). Two villains, the same exit, consecutive episodes.

### Ten weakest jokes
1. "What rank is that?" "None at all." "Then it must be very high." "That's what they all say." (6)
   A reference, not a want; Jax is not naive enough to say it. Worse, it is the central joke of
   **Episode 8** (a grand title with no power in it) spent three episodes early. Cut.
2. "Before one o'clock, that is one every eleven seconds." (5) The sum is wrong. 313 suspects at 11 s
   is 57 minutes, which puts this scene at 12:03; midnight strikes six scenes later. PIP cannot be
   wrong by a factor of three. At about ten o'clock it is "one every thirty-four seconds".
3. "I've never been offered honey by a villain." "You've never been offered honey." (7) The act
   ends on a shrug, and Mira cannot know that.
4. "Idris won this dress at cards. Off a man." "Why did a man have it?" "He'd won it at cards." (4)
5. "A fortune, our label, and now a flower." / "It's a nice flower." (20) The list is unclear, and the
   quip lets the air out of the one moment the audience is meant to believe they were betrayed.
   Rulebook: a laugh never cancels a feeling.
6. "The couriers' ball." … "Which is why it's so exclusive." (11) Third use of the shape.
7. "Which side was Sindbad on?" "The winning one. Eventually. He was a very patient man." (10) The
   one soft patch in the best comic scene. Cut to "On my mother's side." and go to "Hong Gildong."
8. "I'm doing it in a dress." (27) Sixth dress beat, and it steps on Seo's urgency. Keep "Finally."
9. "Everything owes me money." (18) and "Everyone on Ceres owes someone money." (29) Same shape.
10. "She's brought a knife to a story." (3) See Culture and taste.

Jokes that explain themselves: PIP's "I was rounding down. He was not." (9) explains a number the
audience never heard. Wrong mouth: none serious.

## 3. DRAMA AND CHARACTER: **PASS, with fixes**

**What works.** Voss is right about Lucía's wages (8), right that thieves and collectors want the
same thing (16), right that fortunes are robberies (20), and gracious to the last frame. Lucía's
thumb over the call key (23) is a choice between two loves in three inserts. Mira's envelope (27)
is earned by two set-ups ("the only one who knows what she's looking at", 8; "the only person here
who's looking", 12): the woman who always looks chooses not to. The lie afterwards ("He took a lot
of things") and the hand under the console are the right size.

**Findings.**
- **Jax decides nothing after scene 8.** He refuses Voss, then does what Voss predicted, to
  Lupin's plan; he dances, is robbed, climbs. In 23 he "looks at the strip of paper as if it had
  changed weight" and says nothing. Give him the choice there: he could leave the label on the seat.
  One line of decision, for example: JAX: "He wants me to follow it. Then he can try to keep up."
- **Mira's drop needs its third option closed.** She could pocket the envelope and decide later.
  Lucía's "This dress has no pockets" (1) is the plant; extend it to Mira ("Nor has mine.") and the
  choice on the cable is honestly read-now or let-go.
- **Scheherazade has nothing to do while her house sinks** (25–27). She "has not left her desk".
  Give her one insert: frightened guests, her hand opening the book again, a first line ("There
  was once a house that learned to fly"). She keeps sixty people calm with the only tool she has.
  It costs five seconds and gives her agency in the act where she is otherwise rescued by a man.
- **The stake set in scene 2 evaporates in 21.** "The year she leaves, the air stops." Ilse leaves.
  Nobody in the room reacts to what that means; the script moves on to "That was early." One line
  from Oyuna or Idris ("She left. The renewal.") and Scheherazade's answer make the audience fear
  for her until the deed burns.
- **Ilse's intent at the clamp is unreadable.** Did she mean to kill five people and drown a
  library of her father's guests, or to hang the car where the guards could collect it? Voss's "I'd
  have done the same, once" needs us to know. One insert of her eyes on the car's position before
  she cuts would say "strand, not kill"; or let Voss say what she nearly did.
- **Scene 28, [MOUTH 4]: "Two people have left this room before the end. One of them came back."**
  The line gets one of five face shots and I cannot tell who the second person is. Idris and Lupin
  are both standing there. Seo left for eleven minutes and returned. Ilse did not leave before the
  end, because the story was ended for her. Followed at once by "Captain. You're the courier", it
  can even be read as Jax. Replace: "People leave before the end. You are the first who came back
  to pay for it."

**Would a first-time viewer care and click?** See the last section.

## 4. CLARITY: **FIX**

- **The parcel.** "Our label" (0) is a label of nothing. The word "parcel" is first spoken at
  about 9:35 (8), and the parcel is never on screen in this episode. Add to the cold open one
  insert (the parcel strapped down by the dog, thirty-one labels, one pale rectangle) and change
  PIP's line to "The parcel is missing one. The oldest."
- **Why it matters / the cost.** Nobody says what label one will tell them. One line for Mira in
  0: "The first label says where it started. Find who posted it, we find who it's for."
- **Scheherazade is never named aloud.** Zero spoken uses; everyone says "Lady". Nor is Dunyazad
  (the voice notes give a pronunciation for a word nobody says). Name her in 1: IDRIS:
  "Scheherazade's door. The only one in this city Voss doesn't own."
- **Mattias** is never named or called a Voss; Lucía's "Oh. Sorry." at a crest is all we get, and
  Episode 7 needs him. LUCÍA: "Oh. You're one of them. Sorry."
- **Two glass cases.** Why does a collector show two of the same strip? It is never said. One clause
  in Voss's speech (5): "In the thief's honour, item seven will be shown twice. He may choose."
- **Midnight in daylight.** Scene headers say NIGHT; the sky is gold throughout; a bell strikes
  midnight in a ballroom "bright as noon". Nobody says why. It is a real property of the place and
  it is unused: a city riding the wind has days that last days. Give Seo's line in 1 the job: "The
  wind carries the city round the planet in under a week. Three days of light, three of dark.
  Tonight's midnight is at noon."
- **"Run" (20).** They have just been found clean; running makes them look guilty, and they have
  no reason to think Lupin is at the station. MIRA: "The blue one. It's the only door Voss doesn't
  own." Then they are chasing, not fleeing.
- **The clamp plant (4)** is ambiguous and wrong. "The car is clamped to the cable at each end"
  reads as clamps on the car. And when one clamp is released they do not "continue for fifty
  kilometres"; they swing. PIP is the one voice that may not be wrong. Replace: "The cable is
  clamped at each end. If one let go, we would swing. If both did, we would continue for fifty
  kilometres."

**Exposition in disguise.** PIP, 1: "The city floats on the air you breathe. Outside is carbon
dioxide, so ordinary air rises in it." Scheherazade repeats the same fact 90 seconds later (2: "A
balloon floats on its own air, Doctor."). Keep hers, which carries the threat; cut PIP's to the
punchline ("They live in a lifeboat made of breath."). Seo's wind line (1) is a fact with no job
unless it carries the midnight point above.

### The sabotage (the Episode 6 hand-off): **present and visible, not yet safe**
What the audience gets: the order (10), the act (14, three seconds), the grease (15), "Done?" left
unanswered and the dog (26), the panel again (29). That is a real, visible chance and a good trail.
Problems:
1. **No gate has ever been shown.** Establish the panel in the cold open or scene 1 (an insert:
   VENUS GATE — ARRIVED. GATE DRIVE COOLING; a line from Idris). In 14 the open panel must carry
   the words GATE DRIVE, set by us.
2. **Three seconds, no sound, no change in score, as picture four of eight in a comic game.** A
   casual viewer will read it as part of the joke about the empty chair. Hold it 4–5 seconds and
   take the band out under it (one held note or nothing). Silence makes people look.
3. **PIP is on the bridge.** E03 says he is "on the bridge aboard"; in 26 he speaks to Seo there. An
   incorruptible audit unit is a metre from the cut. Either show his bridge lamp dark in the
   insert, or (better for Episode 6) have Seo lay something over it first, so that next week PIP
   has eleven minutes of nothing and knows when but not who.
4. **"Nobody leaves my salon before the story ends"** (2, twice; 3). Seo leaves for eleven minutes
   and returns to a joke about a queue. Let Scheherazade notice: her pen stops as the chair
   empties, then goes on. It keeps the rule, and it means the best-informed woman in the system
   knows Seo was gone, which pays "two stories at once" (21).
5. **Seo's "Checked" (29) points at Seo.** The last hand on a sabotaged panel, saying it is sound,
   is the first person a crew would ask. The season plan needs suspicion on Idris. Now that the
   cut is shown in 14, cut her hand and her line from 29; let Idris set the gate alone while her
   hands stay on the controls and the dog watches them.
6. **The episode does not end on the danger.** The last gate image is at 33:00. Give 29 one final
   picture before the tag: the gate ahead in the window, the closed panel, the dog lying against it.

## 6. SCIENCE: **FIX**

| Claim in the script | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Fifty kilometres up… pressure and warmth close to Earth's… light wraps" (rules, 0, 13) | **Half right.** At 50 km the pressure is about one atmosphere but it is about 75 °C. The temperate band is about 52.5–55 km (20–37 °C), at roughly half to two-thirds of an atmosphere. Say "a little over fifty" or "fifty-five". [1][2] |
| Breathable air is a lifting gas in carbon dioxide (1, 2, 28) | **Correct.** About 0.5 kg per cubic metre at 50 km, about 0.28 at 56 km. [3][4] |
| Lupin leaves under a globe "big as a carriage" (28) | **Wrong by size.** A carriage-sized globe (about 15 m³) lifts 4–7 kg. A man needs 160–290 m³: a globe seven to eight metres across. Make the lantern-globes "big as a house". It is a better picture. (My arithmetic from [3][4].) |
| Sulphuric-acid clouds; a clear band with a cloud sea below (0, 4, 8) | Acid: **correct.** The clear band is **our invention**: the cloud deck runs from about 47–48 km to about 68–70 km, so the city is inside it, in haze. Acceptable as fiction; do not let "What was real" imply otherwise. [1][5] |
| "The wind takes all of it round the planet every four days" (1) | **Cloud-top figure applied to the wrong height.** Four days is at 60–70 km (about 100 m/s). At 53–54 km the Vega balloons measured about 66–70 m/s, which is nearer six days. "In under a week" is safe. "What was real" says "cloud tops… about four days": correct. [6][7] |
| "Nobody feels a thing" (1) against "wraps lifting in a warm wind" (13) | Minor contradiction. A city drifting with the wind feels little steady wind. |
| Outdoors: a mask over nose and mouth only | Acid haze on bare eyes. Add goggles; it also hides more face. |
| "Once a year, and on Venus that is more often than once a day" (5) | **Correct** for the rotation (243.02 days, retrograde) against the year (about 225). Not for sunrise to sunrise (about 117). Fine as a toast. [8] |
| Surface about 467 °C, melts lead (25, note) | **Correct** (lead melts at 327 °C). [9] |
| "From Venus, a reply from Earth takes at least four minutes" (4) | **Correct**: closest approach about 38 million km, 127 s each way. Not re-verified today. |
| The sky: even gold-white light, no visible Sun, soft shadows | **Plausible.** Inside the deck light is scattered many times; no sharp disc. I found no source that describes the view at this height directly. [5] |
| The hot Reliquary "steams" (12) | Hot metal does not steam. Call it acid mist fuming off it: true to the place, same picture. |
| An open-bottomed shaft and belly hatch keep their air | **Correct**, and unclaimed: lighter air stays in an open-bottomed room. Worth a PIP line. |
| "She was built to lift a library. Not a library and us." / "We are the weight." / they climb in / she cuts the car / the house rises (25, 27) | **Self-defeating.** Five people climb into the balloon; their weight stays on it. Only the car is shed. Make the car the weight: MIRA: "…Not a library and a brass tram." LUCÍA: "The car's the weight. We're only in it. We climb, then I cut it loose." |

## Would a stranger watch all of it and click Episode 6?

Probably yes, if they get past minute nine. The first image is a city hanging in a gold sky, the
invitation is funny, and three hundred men dressed as the thief is a premise anyone can enjoy
without knowing the series. Voss at tea is the scene that will hold them: a villain who is kind,
exact and right. From the plank onward the episode does not let go; PIP's stamp, the rose in the
pocket, the thumb over the call key, the envelope falling and "He'll take it home for us" are five
strong beats in twelve minutes. The risks are the ballroom run before tea, the three talking
minutes between the plan and the robbery, and a stranger not knowing what a "label" is attached to.
The click itself is weaker than it should be. The audience knows the ship is flying into a gate
that Seo has cut, and the episode never shows it to them again after minute 33. One last picture of
that panel, and they will click.
