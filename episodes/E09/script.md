# UNIVERSE UNDELIVERED
## Episode 9 — "Murder on the Nautilus"
### Shooting script, draft 1 (writer's draft, outline and script written in one go; for the punch-up writer and the independent critic)

Running time: about 35:58 honest (scene targets add up to 35:48 including "What was real" at 0:40, plus the 0:10 title; model: dialogue at 155 wpm, 0.4 s per speaker change, plus each scene's stated **Holds** (silences, held wides and insert-only action, 5:26 in all), counted by script). Cold open 1:23 · Act 1 7:18 · Act 2 9:13 · Act 3 6:59 · Act 4 9:17 · tag 0:58. 4,358 spoken words (dialogue lines only, including "What was real"; cues, parentheticals and action excluded); 340 speaker changes; Jax V.O. 79 words (1.8%). 29 units (cold open, 27 scenes, tag), plus "What was real". Longest scene 2:19 (21, nobody aboard). Time inside *The Unscheduled*: 0:58 (3%), the tag only. Places: 9, all bright, none of them our ship.
Visual style: photoreal characters and sets, staged mostly as cinematic stills with camera moves
and limited motion. Lip movement is scarce or absent. Europa is THIN on real footage: the real
plates (Voyager, Galileo and Juno stills of the cracked shell; Hubble and Juno Jupiter) carry the
surface in 27, the tag window and "What was real". The ocean, the ice ceiling and every room of
the Nautilus are generated sets.

**How to read the headers.** Each unit lists its number, setting, thread (A = the murder, Holmes
and Nemo · B = the crew seeing each other: Idris's debt, Mira and Jax, Oyuna's blank, Lucía's bed ·
C = the spy, Pell and Voss, Jax's past, PIP's reader), honest target seconds, the stated silences
and holds inside that target (**Holds**), visual class (REAL FOOTAGE WIDE / GENERATED WIDE /
PHOTOREAL SET STILL / PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT / MONTAGE / DATA VISUAL), the real property of
the place that the scene uses (**Place**), and a STAGING note saying how it plays without lip-sync.

**Standing staging rules (they apply everywhere unless a scene says otherwise)**
- **Luminous, never murky.** No sunlight reaches this ocean, so every light is the ship's or the
  sea's own: brass lamps with green and amber glass, floodlamps on the ice, and a drifting
  blue-green glow in the water that nobody explains. Faces are always clearly lit. Each room has
  its own colour world (see casting-notes.md). The only black in the episode is the water below
  the ice walk (18, 19), and it is there because the story needs it.
- **Holmes speaks behind his hands.** Fingertips together in front of his mouth is his thinking
  pose and our lip-sync plan; otherwise he is a back at a window, a huge eye behind his convex
  lens, or a chin on a violin. No deerstalker, no cape, no curved pipe, no catchphrase: a tall,
  thin man of about sixty in a mouse-grey dressing gown over a dark suit.
- **Holmes's lens and Mira's loupe are a pair.** Two magnified eyes, one grey, one brown. When
  his lens passes over a clue and her loupe stops on it, that is the episode in one cut.
- **Nemo's silver beard covers his mouth completely.** He can speak on a still face. At the organ
  he is a back and two hands. He is never a joke and never the butt of one; the camera gives him
  height and stillness. No turban, no jewels: a plain dark sea-coat of his own cut.
- **The Auditor is seen alive only in the dinner stills (5) and one flashback (25).** Her voice
  comes from PIP's recording. Her body is a woman asleep at her desk, seen from behind or covered.
  No wound, no distress.
- **The Nautilus crew never speak on screen.** Dark-blue jerseys, faces from every sea. They move
  things, light lamps, and look to Nemo.
- **PIP is the lens on Jax's wrist comm**, unstrapped and laid lens-up on tables, as in E01; the
  lens brightens when he talks. Under the ice nothing can be sent: **every report gets an INSERT
  of its number with QUEUED — NO SIGNAL** (#1,266 in 2, #1,267 in 21, #1,268 in 22; all sent in
  27; E08 ends at #1,265). On the surface (27) the wrist lens goes dark in Jupiter's radiation,
  as in E08, and he speaks from the titanium strongbox aboard our ship.
- **The drum.** Every cabin has a brass recording drum by the door: a paper band, an ink line,
  brass time marks. On screen it is a DATA VISUAL, the band laid flat. The Auditor's band reads,
  in order: DOOR 01:40–01:44 · DOOR SEALED 01:52 · VENT SHUT 01:53 · AIR FOUL 01:58. All four
  marks are legible every time it is shown, from scene 2 on. It is the fair-play clue.
- **The air glass.** A small glass tube of crystals in a brass mount on every cabin wall. Blue is
  good air. The Auditor's is black.
- **Jax never looks at the water.** In the gallery he faces inboard; at the moon pool he sits
  against the far wall. Nobody explains it. He looks at it once, in 19, because he has to.
- **Seo's left inside pocket.** A flat oblong worn into the lining of her grey jacket, over the
  heart. It is full in the dinner still (5) and empty from the cold open on. Never point at it
  before 13.
- **The dog** is always within a metre of Jax, and watches Seo's hands.
- **Under the ice (18, 19, 22) everyone is in Nemo's armoured diving dress**: brass and glass,
  a lamp on the helmet, a breathing piece that hides the mouth. Salt water stops radio, so they
  talk on a wire that also ropes them together. The wire sound is close and dry.
- **The ceiling walk is shot upside down**, so the ice is a glowing floor and the black water is
  the sky. The figures' air bubbles run "down" along the ice. That is the one tell.
- **When someone important speaks, hold on the listener.**
- **Music:** solo violin (Holmes), pipe organ (Nemo), sonar as percussion. All original; quote no
  existing piece. The two instruments are never in the same key until 26.

**Visible-mouth speaking shots: 5 (maximum 5).** Marked **[MOUTH 1]** to **[MOUTH 5]**:
1. Holmes, 1, "A dying woman doesn't spend a word carelessly."

# ACT ONE — "Eight Souls, a Lamp and a Dog"

### 1 · THE VIEWING GALLERY — THE DRESSING GOWN
**Thread:** A/C · **Target:** 129 s · **Holds:** 11 s · **Visual:** GENERATED WIDE / PHOTOREAL SET STILL / PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT · **Place:** the ice ceiling; no sunlight (every light is the ship's or the sea's)
**STAGING:** The first spectacle. A long glass gallery on the Nautilus's back. Overhead, close
enough to feel, the underside of the ice: a blue-white ceiling veined with green, lit by the ship's
