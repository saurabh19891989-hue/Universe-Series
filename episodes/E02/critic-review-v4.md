## 5. The three ruling checks

### 5.1 Kaguya never seen face to face
The blind is the best idea in the draft: a silhouette costs almost nothing to animate, acts with the whole
body, and is true to the source's world (Heian noblewomen received visitors from behind blinds). The slats
parting in 4 is a perfect silent beat. **It costs warmth in one place only: 14**, the "evening of two
people who can't remember what they miss" (season-01, ep 2 B). There she is a veiled shape for 67 s and the
romance has no face.

**One partial reveal pays better.** In 14, on "If I remembered how, I'd ask you to stay.", her white glove
lifts one side of the veil; we see her face in three-quarter through the clear dome, lit by Jax's helmet
light, through "I'd say yes." / "And go."; she lets the veil fall at the HOLD. It mirrors 4 (she saw him
through the slats; now he sees her). It is cheap (one face drawing from the locked master, no lip-sync
needed if her "And go." plays on the veil falling) and keeps the blind everywhere indoors. This needs the
veil outside the dome (M8). The showrunner ruled the rule; this is a recommendation and the showrunner's call.

### 5.2 The midpoint line against canon
Canon relied on:
- season-01, ep 2: "**B:** … Jax and Kaguya share an evening of two people who **can't remember what they
  miss**." / "**C:** Kaguya recognises thinning in Jax and **won't say what she sees**." / "**Changes:** …
  **First clue about Jax.**"
- season-01 rulings (27 Nov evening): "**The captain's past:** the audience may be told that Jax is the hero
  of a story taken out of the world; '**he did it to himself' is held for ep 8**; who he is, for ep 10."
- season-01 reconciliation: Sun Wukong's scratched-out name in ep 8 is "the first hint that Jax did it to
  himself".
- Bible §6: "delisted at his own request, for a reason he no longer remembers"; "the first delisting was an
  act of love — she hid him to keep him… **Do not reveal before Season 2.**"

**Finding: within the allowance.** "You've worn one before" says that Jax has already forgotten what he
loved. That is the season plan's own "can't remember what they miss", and it is the "first clue". It does not
say who did it, why, or who he is. "Worn" is agency-neutral (in the tale the robe is put on Kaguya by
others). Approve as ruled. Three guards:
1. **PIP volunteering in the same scene primes "he chose it"**, which is ep 8's hint. Do not add any line,
   in E02 or later, that says he put it on himself or chose to.
2. Kaguya must refuse to say more. She does (14: "What did you see?" / "Someone who's very good at
   leaving."). Keep that exchange; it is the season plan's "won't say".
3. Never confirm a literal robe. Canon's mechanism is delisting and concealment; "one" must stay readable as
   "a forgetting". (Calypso is a weaver, so a woven concealment would not contradict it, but that is
   Season 2's business.) No later script picks the line up (E03–E09 searched); Mira writes it in her
   notebook (10), so E03 must not have her solve it (handover 8).

### 5.3 The new escape physics (sunset run)
Worked through. Geometry of the episode: the Valley lies just beyond the west limb (~95°W); sunset there falls
just after new Moon, when the evening terminator sits on the west limb and moves west onto the far side; the
ship flies east, toward the near side, into ground already in night.
- **A shadow a hundred metres deep:** on a smooth Moon the height still in sunlight at distance *d* past the
  terminator is about d²/2R (R = 1,737 km). 100 m at about 19 km; it deepens by about 11 m per km there.
  "A hundred metres deep here. Deeper every kilometre." and "Twenty metres" lower are right. Real ridges near
  the terminator change it locally, which "here" covers. **Plausible.**
- **Would a pursuer above see a sunlit fin?** Yes, and better than the writer says. Near new Moon the Sun, the
  L2 point and the far side's centre are nearly in a line, so from L2 the *Steadfast* looks from almost the
  Sun's own direction: it sees the fin fully lit against dark ground. And because her line of sight nearly
  matches the sunlight, the moment the fin drops into shadow it also drops out of her sight. "The gold goes
  out" is honest. Caveats: a few-metre fin at ~60,000 km is a point of light for a scope, not an eyeball
  sight (fine: she is a warship); and the run is right at her limb, so soon after, the Moon itself hides them,
  which is what 17 says ("The Moon is between them now"). **Consistent.**
- **The plan is not legible** (logic, not physics): Arakawa promised not to come down *before* sunset, so a
  viewer asks why they don't leave at S−1 h. The cut "In the dark" line was the answer. Must-fix M5 puts one
  clause back each side (her "if you lift, I will see you"; Seo's "in the dark"). By the playbook that is
  allowed: the plan is stated and then goes wrong (the fin).
- **One error elsewhere:** the tag's "The far side dark in her window now" is wrong. At this sunset the far
  side seen from L2 is nearly full; only its western edge has gone dark (M6).
- **"What was real":** "After sunset, anything flying above the ground stays in sunlight for a while." is
  accurate. Approve; the season table's "escape through the L2 halo" can now be amended.

- **Physical business:** excellent throughout (the watch set down "as if it might tell on him", the

## 8. Rights, rules, science

**Rights.** Sources are public domain and used as the books have them: *The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter*
(the jewelled branch from the mythic peak, renamed "the Peak of Eternal Light"; the craftsmen who come to be
paid; the dragon's-neck jewel; the feather robe that ends human feeling; the letter; the glowing stem; the
old bamboo cutter; the elixir burned on the highest mountain, if restored); *Orlando Furioso* (Astolfo of
England; the vials of wits; the swollen bladders; lovers' sighs; gold hooks; "madness never leaves Earth";
his horn); Raspe's Munchausen (the hatchet on the heap of chaff and chopped straw; the cannonball; the fish).
Nothing I can trace to any film or anime treatment of Kaguya: no procession, no sketch-watercolour look, no
added characters, no screen design. The woodblock style is "in general, never traced". **Keep the 14 face
reveal to the locked master**; it must not drift toward any screen Kaguya.

**Living religions — two art-direction flags, no script faults:**
- **The Keeping House has "red lacquered doors" and "red pillars".** Vermilion pillars are the signature of
  a Shinto shrine. Combined with bowing toward a screened figure, a Japanese viewer may read it as a shrine
  with Kaguya as its object of worship. Make it a Heian palace: unpainted cypress pillars, white plaster,
  black lacquer trim, bamboo blinds. (S6)
- **Jax's poster monster** ("a huge silver serpent with four white teeth") must not look like an East Asian
  dragon (no horns, whiskers or pearl): dragon kings are worshipped. Seo's "There are no dragons." is from the
  tale's task and is a pilot's literalism, not mockery; fine. (S6)
- Low-risk note: some medieval Fuji traditions identified Kaguya-hime with the mountain's goddess. She is
  played with dignity, she makes the jokes rather than being one, and there is no shrine imagery, so this is
  fine.
- "The Distant voice" (14): tell the composer it must not resemble chant or sutra. (N8)

**Known lines.** None I can place in a film or TV show. "Courageous" is the bible's known *Yes, Minister*
borrowing (one word, canon).

**AI-disclosure wording:** none in the script (searched). Draft 3's staging note is gone.

**Science, scene by scene:**
- *No direct line to Earth; relay:* correct throughout (0, 3, 5, 11, WWR).
- *Delay:* 1.7 s one way through L2: correct; the 3.4 s display: correct.
- *Earth visible?* 0, from orbit: yes, it rises in seconds: correct. 5, a ledge high on the rim near the limb,
  by libration: correct as a prediction; the "thin blue edge clears the ridge" must be a sliver, already there
  (libration is far too slow to watch) (N4). 2, 4, 8–10, 13–15 on the floor: not visible, correctly. 14
  "looking for it": correct. 17, flying over the limb: correct, nearly full. 18, from the near side: correct.
