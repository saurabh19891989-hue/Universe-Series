## The three weak scenes

**23, the robe on PIP (35 s → 65 s).** The forgetting now goes in the order of what he loved, so
each step is a laugh and worse than the last. (1) Lucía's wages, the number PIP has guarded for two
episodes: "You are owed something. Is it a lot?" / "Ten months." / "Goodness. Well. I'm sure it'll
turn up." (2) The labels, then the parcel itself: "What were they labels on?" / "What parcel?" (3) A
report with no subject, and he likes it: "I can't think who it's about. Do you know, it's rather
nice." (4) Jax, remembered only by E01's first report: "You're the one who says 'take it'. I was
against it." (5) Then the worst. He is bright and polite, a stranger, and he asks them for exactly
what he asked for in 20: "Would you all introduce yourselves? In writing, if you can." (6) "Whose
ship is this?" Nobody can say, because that is how the robe works. "No one's? How restful." The
voice note keeps him warm and well: no glitching, no slurring. It frightens because he sounds happy.
**What "Stop" costs Seo:** in 16 she promised "I'll save it some other way. Not with a lie." 24 now
shows that she knew the other way all along and let PIP put the robe on: "How long have you known?"
/ "Since this morning." / "And you let him put it on." / "Yes." Her reason is true and sounds like a
fugitive's: "Because I'll have to fly it where Enforcement can see me." / "You're shy?" / "Yes." So
she pays twice. She loses some of Lucía's trust. And she flies where Arakawa can watch her, which
lands at once: "Pilot. Whoever you are. That is very good flying" (28), Arakawa's "I would like to
meet her" (tag), and Pell's reply, which now adds "SHE ASKED ABOUT YOUR PILOT" (tag). The helmet
coming off is staged as the cost too. Her glove rests on the clasp, still, through two of PIP's
lines before it opens.

**18, Arakawa and the silent Chichikov.** Now a duel of manners. He answers only with objects. The
letter gets "That is not an answer. That is stationery." The teacup he is holding is hers,
stencilled T. ARAKAWA: "And that is my cup." He sets it back on its own ring, bows, sits. She
learns two things from what he does *not* say. First, Voss was here before her: his notebook's
FAR SIDE · VALLEY is in old ink. Second, Voss is not here for the courier. She tests him the way a
fencer tests a guard, one word at a time, watching his pencil: "The courier." (still) "The
Princess." (still) "The names that fall in that valley." (it writes). "Ah. Then you are not here
for the courier at all." This makes the season plant (Voss harvesting fallen names) visible without
stating it. The Chichikov gag escalates: the most observant officer in the Directorate looks away
and back, and he is in a different seat. She can't say when he moved. The cup comes back in the tag,
sitting on its ring.

**3, under the ship's belly.** Its one good idea comes out of Jax's want. He has ignored the
Baron's warning (1), and he pays for the air with a gold watch off the heap. The tag reads "Lost. A
grain merchant of Smyrna. Eighteen fifty-one." / "He's not using it." PIP files #1,208: "Theft,
from a place that does not exist." / "You can't steal from nowhere." / "Then I shall file it under
nowhere." The watch pays off three times: in 4 Kaguya, behind her blind, says "Put back the watch"
("The one in your left glove. He's still looking for it."); in 24, "I know. I had one in my glove";
and in 25 PIP sees that someone on Earth has read it ("Even the watch"). Gone: the clamp-key and
regulations lines and the staircase-inheritance joke. Kept: "It isn't dark. It never was."

## What the story may still get wrong (for the showrunner and the critic)
- **Light time.** The READ stamps say 1.3 s. Through an L2 relay, the path from the far side to
  Earth is longer (roughly 1.6–1.7 s one way). 1.3 s matches E01 and is the running gag. The science
  critic should decide whether the stamp means "read on arrival" or needs a new number. "What was
  real" is not affected.
- **Seo's silence.** She knew the route "since this morning" but had no way to pay the bank. A
  critic may ask why she didn't speak in 20. The answer on the page is "where Enforcement can see
  me"; check that it reads as fear of exposure, not as callousness. It must not tip the rat reveal
  before the tag.
- **Pell's extra line** in the tag ("SHE ASKED ABOUT YOUR PILOT") adds information. Check that it
  doesn't step on ep 4 (Pell and Arakawa in person) or ep 8.
- **Kaguya knowing the watch** is in Jax's glove from behind a blind is played as her power over
  lost things, not explained. If the critic wants a mechanism, Astolfo could have seen it (one word
  of staging).
- **Suits filled from jars** (17). How suit air is topped up from the ship's hose is waved through.
- **Scene 6** is now a 25-second tail of 5. It keeps its number to match the outline; merging it is
  harmless if the critic prefers.
- **The 1786 hatchet history** (Kaguya testing the Baron) is gone. Nothing depends on it, but the
  Raspe link now rests only on 22.
- **"What was real"** still says "The Valley is invented. I am told it is very real." It is
  unchanged by rule, but a critic may want the joke checked against "honesty on screen".

## Lines I'm unsure are in good taste
- **PIP's forgetting (23)** could read as dementia. It is written as magic, with PIP warm and
  content, and none of the shorthand ("senior moment", confusion played for mockery). Keep the TTS
  clean (no slurring, no glitch effects), and keep it out of any promo cut.
- **"Not one of the wives."** (22) The Baron's marriages. Mild, and aimed at his vanity, but flag it.
- **"He hid it from his wife. She'd have spent it better."** (25) The joke favours the wife. Fine,
  I think.
- **"Men are so dramatic."** (21) Kept from draft 1. It is Kaguya's dryness about the emperor who
  burned the elixir, not a dig at men in general.
- **"Lost. A grain merchant of Smyrna."** A neutral historic port name (now İzmir). Swap it if the
  owner prefers a modern name.
- Removed for taste: "Old ladies love a riddle" (17). It belittled Kaguya, who is never the joke.
