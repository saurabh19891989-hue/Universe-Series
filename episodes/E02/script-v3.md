**How the targets were estimated** (this replaces the 155 wpm model, which the Episode 1 table read
disproved): speech at **184 wpm** (the measured Episode 1 voice pace), plus 0.97 s per speaker cue
(the Episode 1 shot plan's 0.5 s between lines and 0.6 s of breath per dialogue shot), plus 1.5 s
for each stand-alone INSERT and each stated beat, silence or hold, plus the listed picture-only
time (montage, the two plates, held stills, text the viewer must read). The same formula run on
Episode 1's `script-v3.md` gives 35:27 against its shot plan's 35:34. Per-scene numbers are in
`revision-notes.md`. Do not slow the voices to hit the time; the silences carry it.

Visual style: photoreal characters and sets composited with real NASA footage, staged mostly
as cinematic stills with camera moves and limited motion. Lip movement is scarce or absent.

**How to read the headers.** Each unit lists its number, setting, thread (A = Kaguya and the
siege · B = Mira and Jax; Lucía and Seo · C = Seo the rat, Voss and Chichikov, PIP's reader,
Jax's past), target seconds, visual class (REAL FOOTAGE WIDE / ILLUSTRATED PLATE / PHOTOREAL SET
STILL / PHOTOREAL CLOSE-UP / INSERT / MONTAGE / DATA VISUAL), and a STAGING note saying how it
plays without lip-sync.

**What is real footage and what is not.** No ground-level footage of the far-side surface exists
in our library (location file 02). **Every view from the surface is an ILLUSTRATED PLATE**: a
generated landscape, built on real LRO/LOLA terrain where possible, with ships and figures
composited. REAL FOOTAGE is used only for the far side seen from space (the *Steadfast*'s window,
27), the Earthrise (29), Earth in the window (30) and "What was real". Build **one hero wide of the
Valley** and reuse it with crops (2, 3, 15, 19, 22, 26).

**Geography (keep it the same in every shot).** The Valley lies just beyond the Moon's west limb.
Earth is just below the horizon in one direction (toward the near side) and peeps over it when the
Moon wobbles; the Sun is going down in the opposite direction, so the Valley's Earthward wall is the
sunlit one. The cold open sets the ship on **a ledge high on the Valley's rim**, in line of sight of
where Earth will peep over. In 8 Seo moves it **down into the Valley, tight under the Earthward
wall**, so the wall itself stands between the ship and Earth. That is "the Valley's shadow": a
shadow from Earth, not from the Sun, and the ship sits in it in full sunlight for the rest of the
episode. The Keeping House is cut into the same sunlit wall, further along; its steps look across a
bay of heaps to the ship. The *Steadfast* and the relay ("the Lantern") are far beyond the Moon at
L2, looking straight down on the whole sunlit far side; the cutter needs two hours to reach the
surface (25). Sunset here falls near new Moon, so the Earth that rises in 29 is **nearly full**, and
the near side under it is in night.

**Standing staging rules (they apply everywhere unless a scene says otherwise)**
- **Mouths are hidden or off-screen whenever someone speaks.** The episode has **four** marked
  exceptions, each tagged **[VISIBLE MOUTH n/4]**. For those, paste only the mouth region onto
  the still (IMAGE-PIPELINE §3a). If the paste looks wrong on Lucía's two (9, 30), play them on her
  ribbon or the back of her head; nothing is lost.
- **Kaguya speaks from behind a bamboo blind** indoors, as Heian ladies received visitors. Her one
  great gesture is to lower her fan and show her eyes, and that is always a silent beat. She writes
  with a brush (INSERT). Outside, she wears a white suit with a smoked visor.
- **Astolfo keeps his visor down**, an old knight's helm over a pressure helmet. He lifts the
  helm's visor once, in 22, for a silent beat (a glass faceplate stays between him and the vacuum).
- **Seo is in a flight helmet or seen from behind in the pilot's seat.** Her gloved hands on the
  controls are her signature insert.
- **Arakawa is never seen speaking.** On her own bridge she is a profile against the far side from
  space (real footage), lit by the console. To the crew she is a voice on the local band. **She
  counts down to sunset at intervals** (12, 15, 17, 21, 23, 26), exact and courteous, and never raises
  her voice.
- **The two clocks.** From 5 on, the crew's wrist screens and the bridge show SUNSET (a countdown)
  and, from 12, AIR (a percentage). They are INSERTS every few minutes and they only go down, until
  25. The air: 31% (0), 30% (3), 22% (12), 9% when the sighs go in and 52% after (15: about two
  days' worth, against a night of fourteen), then 38, 36, 35, 34 through Act Three, stopping at 33%
  when the bank is paid (25) and rising in 30.
- **The Baron's moustache covers his mouth completely.** He is bareheaded and coatless (his coat
  and tricorn are on the horse's head at Rim Port); outdoors he wears a patched suit and a bare
  helmet. His colour, about 60% at the start, **comes back** in the Valley: 70% in Act 2, full by
  22. Never make him look younger.
- **Chichikov is in soft focus and never speaks.** No line all episode; nobody remarks on it.
- **PIP is the brass lamp with the amber lens.** Away from the bridge he speaks through **a small
  brass wrist relay with its own amber lens** on Jax's forearm (introduced in 3). The lamp itself is
  unbolted and carried to the vault in 10, stays there for 20 and 23, and is bolted back in 25.
  Every report gets an INSERT of its number and status. They continue from #1,207: #1,208 (3, the
  watch), #1,209 (10, the dead letters), #1,210 (19, the crow) and #1,211 (23, on no one) come back
  UNSENT until 25.
- **Lucía**: welding visor, back to camera, half inside machines; the ribbon on her wrist.
- **Mira**: the loupe, her notebook, the back of her head; her clamp key on its lanyard (28).
  Reflections are for silent beats only.
- **Exteriors are always in helmets**, with dialogue on suit radio. **No sound in vacuum**: the
  bladders in 2 "shout" only in Astolfo's telling, never on the soundtrack.
- **Default cut: hold on the listener.**
- **Places and light (rulebook, Places).** Seven bright places, each with its own colour world:
  **the Valley** (hard white sun, black sky, the glint of silver and gold heaps), **the Keeping
  House** (warm amber lamplight, red lacquer, honey-coloured bamboo), **the Lost Workshop** (forge
  orange on a wall of every tool ever dropped), **the dead-letter vault** (a rainbow mountain of
  envelopes and stamps under paper lanterns), **Seo's wreck** (a red-and-white shuttle in a crater
  of blinding sun), **the *Steadfast*** (steel blue, the sunlit far side filling the window) and
  **the Valley's edge** (low gold sun, black sky). **The far side is not dark.** Darkness is used
  twice, as a story event: the sunset run (27–28) and the shelf at night (tag).
