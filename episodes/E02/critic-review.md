## OVERALL VERDICT: READY AFTER FIXES

This is a better script than the E01 draft I would have expected at this stage. Kaguya is a real
character in the tale's own key: a sharp, amused examiner. The branch-and-invoice scene (14) is
the best comic set piece the show has. PIP's forgetting (23) and the vote before it (20) are
genuinely moving. Arakawa is formidable from her first line. The tag hurts. All six of the
showrunner's rulings are honoured on the page, but one (the "What was real" note) is honoured
only in part.

It is not ready to lock. These are the reasons:

1. **The geography of Act 1 breaks.** The cold open hops the ship into the Valley. Scene 3 has it
   parked "among the glittering heaps". Then in scene 7 it is suddenly "on the ridge", where Earth
   will see it in nine minutes. Save 1 is built on a ship that is somewhere it was never moved to.
2. **Two story-maths errors that a regular viewer will catch.** Lucía's wages jump about a month
   in four days. The parcel's label count contradicts E01.
3. **The money that solves the episode was always available.** Kaguya pays the bank in 24 with
   no reason why now and not in scene 4. That makes PIP's sacrifice look like something she
   allowed for no purpose.
4. **The siege has no squeeze between minute 10 and minute 31.** After Save 1, nothing external
   presses on the crew. Arakawa is off screen for 13 minutes, and the "why leave at all"
   question (the Keeping House is pressurised and Kaguya survives every lunar night) is never
   answered.
5. **The guest's name is never spoken.** Nobody says "Kaguya" aloud in 35 minutes, and nobody
   says "Munchausen". On YouTube the name is the click, and a newcomer never learns either.
6. **The footage plan assumes ground-level far-side plates that don't exist.** Every far-side
   ground wide labelled REAL FOOTAGE has to be illustrated. "What was real" must stay honest
   about that, and it still has one factual error (Queqiao) and one joke that breaks the ruling.

All of this fits in one revision pass without touching the act structure.

## 1. HOOK AND PACE: **FIX**

**First 20 seconds: PASS.** "Captain. Earth is rising." at about 0:03; "In ninety seconds it will
see us. So will the bank." at about 0:12. That's a problem with a clock on it. **First minute:
PASS with a note.** The goal (stay unseen) and the cost (the air) both land. But only a returning
viewer knows *why* the bank seeing them costs the air. Nothing in the cold open says the bank
repossesses by signal (see Clarity).

**Act turns.** Act 1 ends on Save 1 and the Lantern: a real turn, because a stranger is now flying
the ship. Act 2 ends on "You've worn one before.": a turn for thread C, but **not for the plot**.
Act 3 begins with the same problem the act-two break left (no way out), and the robe's price only
arrives in 19. Act 3 ends on "Stop. There's another way.": strong. Act 4 ends on the limb, then
Mars: a turn. Verdict: three of four are real story turns. The Act 2 break is a character hook.
Fine, but it means Act 3 opens with two scenes (17, 18) that don't advance the siege.

**No scene is over 2:30.** On my clock (below) the longest are 4 (about 2:05), 21 (about 1:50), 14,
24 and 25 (about 1:45 each). PASS.

**Same energy twice in a row (rule 5).**
- **19 → 20 → 21 → 22** (about 20:30–26:05): four seated talk scenes in a row. Two are long and
  tender (21, 22), and 23, which follows, is slow on purpose. Five consecutive scenes, about 7½
  minutes, with no movement, no external event and no comedy set piece. **This is the episode's
  sag.**
- **9 → 14 → 16 → 19 → 22**: the *same picture*. The crew stand in a row before a bamboo blind.
  Spread across the episode it's fine, but 19 and 22 are both blind scenes in the sag.
- 4 → 5: scene 4 (two minutes of the blind) then Arakawa's terms (exposition by radio). Talk then
  talk, at the episode's most expository point.

**Threads and characters absent for more than 6 minutes (rule 7).** The C thread itself never
vanishes. But **Arakawa, the besieger, is absent from scene 5 (about 7:15) to scene 18 (about
19:25): 12 minutes.** Nothing she does is felt in Acts 2 and early 3. The sunset clock appears
twice (12, 19) and nobody acts on it. The Baron is absent from 14 (about 16:05) to 22 (about
24:45), and the decision to stay comes with no build. Lucía, by contrast, is everywhere and is the
best-served character.

### Honest runtime

Method: dialogue at 155 wpm (and at 165), plus 0.4 s per speaker cue, plus about 2 s per held
INSERT that does not play under a line (about half of the 93 INSERTs), about 2 s per stated
silence or beat (33), the montage, the two 20-second plates, and the text the viewer must read
(three Voss slips, Temi's message, the READ stack, Seo's report and Pell's reply).

- Speech alone: **28:49 at 155 wpm**, 27:35 at 165 wpm.
- Silent picture: about **5:45–6:30**.
- Title: 0:10.
- **Honest total: about 34:30–35:30 at 155 wpm; about 33:30–34:30 at 165 wpm.**

The header's **35:40 is the top of the honest range, not the middle.** It holds only if the voices
are paced at 155 and every stated hold is honoured. WHERE-I-STOPPED records that the E01
table-read ran at **184 wpm**. At that pace this script plays at about **31:30**. So 35–36 minutes
is honest *only* if the audio is deliberately slowed. Say so in the brief to the voice pass,
because the risk is a short episode, not a long one. The script is also **below the rulebook's
4,500–5,500-word band** (3,966). That's acceptable for a show of stills, but it means about 18% of
the running time is silent picture, so each silent beat must be a good image.

Scenes whose targets are light (honest vs stated): 0 (about 80 vs 70), 4 (about 125 vs 120), 18
(about 78 vs 65, because of the ten inserts and the pencil rhythm), 21 (about 110 vs 100), 23
(about 80 vs 65, because of the five held lens stills), 25 (about 105 vs 95), tag (about 45 vs 30:
Pell's 17-word reply and the deletion must be readable). These are offset by short scenes that run
under target (2, 6, 9, 10, 26). The header also says "What was real" is 0:45, but its own heading
says 0:40 and the rulebook says 0:30. At 116 words it is about **45 s**.

### The two minutes where we are most likely to lose a viewer

1. **About 4:30–6:00, the middle of scene 4.** It follows two walk-and-explain scenes (2, 3).
   It is one static picture (a blind and a fan) for two minutes, and the episode's real engine
   (the siege, the sunset clock) doesn't arrive until scene 5 at about 6:30. The jokes are good,
   but a browsing viewer has not yet been given a reason to stay that is bigger than "a nice
   lady behind a screen". *Fix:* move "Put back the watch" earlier, before the Baron/horse run, and
   cut "A wooden one with prospects" and "I was there in spirit / Your spirit was in Russia"
   (keep one). Better still, open 4 on the click of Arakawa's band, then play the audience.
2. **About 21:00–22:00, scene 19** (the robe's terms), in the middle of the 7½-minute sag. It is
   rule exposition ("The bank's lien is on the ship's registry. So is her warrant. Everything
   hunting us is hunting the registry."). It comes two seated scenes after an act break that
   didn't move the plot, and nothing external has happened for 11 minutes. *Fix:* see MUST-FIX 6
   (an external squeeze in Act 3), and play 19 somewhere that is not the blind.

## 2. COMEDY: **PASS, with fixes**

Each character is funny in a distinct way, and mostly in the rulebook's way. Jax sells and
deflects ("Then the bank's contributing"). Mira deflates, though less often than in E01; she is
written more as a detective here, which suits her. PIP is literal ("This is a forwarding
address"). Lucía is blunt and owed (the invoice, "In a bad wrestler"). Seo is deadpan ("Most papers
are"). Kaguya is dry and exact, which is right for the source. The Baron is magnificent and
wounded. Arakawa is formal ("It is not sweet. It is paperwork"). Astolfo is cheerful
literal-mindedness. Nobody knows they are funny. Understatement after disaster is used well
(Temi's P.S. after the save; "Thirty-one." after the robe).

**Jokes in the wrong mouth.**
- 5: Mira's "That's the polite word for no." explains Arakawa's "courageous" joke, and it's a
  *callback* that the E01 audience already gets. In E01 Mira's own line was "Courageous. Yes. I've
  heard." Give her a flat recognition instead: MIRA (off-band): "Courageous." (that's all), or "Oh,
  she's good."
- 17: "She said they were going cheap." It's unclear who "she" is and why Jax would say it. The
  laugh is in *Jax's* poetry, so Jax should own the realisation: MIRA: "Lucía topped your suit up
  from the Petrarch jars." JAX (a beat): "That explains the sonnet."
- 8: Lucía's "Neither does anyone, first time." It reads as an innuendo she doesn't mean, and it
  is the wrong joke for the beat (she's suspicious, not wry). Better: LUCÍA (watching the back of
  Seo's helmet): "Usually she's mine."

**Jokes that explain themselves.** 5 (above). 3: PIP's "Unsent. I am very poor company." The setup
("I am talking to myself") was cut in punch-up, so the punchline is now orphaned and reads as
self-pity. 14: "She hasn't done that since the Normans." / "Earlier. I'd say the Heian period."
The topper needs history to land, and it is arguably wrong (the Norman Conquest falls *inside*
the Heian period, 794–1185).

**Repeated joke shapes.**
- **"X isn't Y." / "No. It's Z."** appears four times: 4 ("That isn't the same as yes." / "No. It's
  much more interesting."), 14 ("That's not paying it." / "No. It's framing it."), 15 ("That isn't
  passing." / "No. But it isn't failing."), and 16 ("They're the only honest kind." / "No. You
  answered truthfully."). Keep 4 and 14 (the best). Recast 15: MIRA: "Is that a pass?" KAGUYA: "It's
  a map. Of the only thing I keep that isn't lost." Leave 16, because "truthfully, not honestly"
  is a different turn.
- **"That is rather the point."** PIP in 0, then Kaguya in 19 ("I wouldn't know. That is rather the
  point."). Two characters using the same tic flattens Kaguya. Change 19 to "I wouldn't know. That's
  what it's for."
- **"Tuesday"** three times (0, 3, 21). The third ("It sounds like a Tuesday.") is a tic, not a
  callback. Cut it with "Does that sound familiar".
- **"Restful"**: the Baron in 11 ("very restful for them") steals the key word of 23. Change 11 to
  "I'm told they're very happy."
- **"Sir Astolfo"** six times in PIP's mouth in scene 10. Keep two.
- **Earth-peeps-over-the-ridge, hop away**: the same action shape twice in one act (0 and 8). The
  second one is better motivated (Seo knows the wobble). See MUST-FIX 1, which turns the repeat
  into an escalation.
- Jax's "Temi! If you can hear me, I am about to make a very large payment—" (0) is E01's cold-open
  line again, not an escalation. Escalate it: "Temi! I'm about to make a payment! It's a big one!
  It's imaginary, but it's big!" Or cut it, since PIP's next line still lands without it.

**Running gags from E01: do they escalate?**
- PIP's reports: **yes, well.** They are all UNSENT, then they go out, then PIP *sees* READ. A
  genuine escalation, and an emotional one.
- Chichikov unremembered: **yes, superbly.** He has no lines, he takes her cup, he changes seats,
  and his name is "not recorded" in the tag.
- Correcting Earth's version: **yes.** Kaguya corrects the Baron, and Astolfo corrects Ariosto
  ("I used a funnel. Like a gentleman.").
- Section twelve: **yes** ("I wrote four of them").
- The coffee machine: **yes, three times**, ending in Temi's P.S.
- "Darts": **yes.** The screwdriver throw is the best single plant in the episode, and Jax
  doesn't notice he did it.
- "Courageous": *repeats*, with Mira explaining it (above).
- "Don't make it ten": the payoff is right, but the arithmetic is wrong (MUST-FIX 2).

## 3. DRAMA AND CHARACTER: **FIX**

**Do choices follow from wants?** Mostly, yes. Jax wants air and takes the watch (3), then sells
the lie (14). Mira wants proof, maps the unmappable and leaves her reputation (15). Lucía wants
pay and invoices a princess. Seo wants aboard and cuts her own fuel line. PIP wants to be useful
in a way nobody files, and volunteers. The Baron wants to stop fading, and stays. Arakawa wants a
lawful arrest and keeps her word. Kaguya wants to be surprised honestly, and is (by Lucía, then
Seo). **The one broken link is Kaguya's money (MUST-FIX 3):** her choice to pay the bank in 24
follows from nothing on the page.

**Is PIP's sacrifice earned, and does its cost land? Yes, on both counts. It is the best thing in
the episode.** Scene 10 builds his love of the vault. Scene 20 lets him choose in his own register
("I would like, once, to be useful in a way that nobody files"). Scene 23's order of forgetting
(Lucía's wages, the labels, the parcel, Jax, then a stranger asking for introductions "in
writing") is devastating, and nobody is laughing at him. The cost lands three times: "Thirty-one."
/ "Nobody speaks." (24); "Label one. I had it. I have the shape of it. Blue ink. A slanted hand.
I keep reaching for it, Captain. Like a step that isn't there." (30); "That is not a regulation.
That is a promise." (30). Two cautions: (a) it only works if the label arithmetic is right
(MUST-FIX 2); (b) if Kaguya could have paid all along, the sacrifice was hers to prevent
(MUST-FIX 3). Fix (b), and the scene gains, because then the robe was her test.

**Is the Jax–Kaguya scene (21) moving or sentimental? Moving, once trimmed.** It is restrained,
specific ("An old man who cut bamboo. I know it the way you know a word in a language you've
stopped speaking"), and it ends on a matched pair of refusals ("I'd say yes." / "And go." /
"...And go.") rather than a kiss or a speech. "Men are so dramatic." is correctly dry. Two lines
tip it toward explaining itself: "Does that sound familiar, Captain?" (Kaguya tells the audience
the parallel that "He says nothing. That's an answer." has just shown), and "It sounds like a
Tuesday." Cut both. Also fix "Everyone does, the first night": the scene is marked DAY, LATE, and
there is no night until 27. Use "Everyone does, the first day."

**Is Seo likeable enough that the tag hurts? Nearly.** The photo scene (13), "No hurry" paid off as
trust (28), "Your hands. I've seen them." (24) and the deleted "THE CREW ARE KIND." do the work. The
danger is 20 and 24. She sits through the vote saying "It isn't my ship.", then admits she knew
another way "since this morning" and let PIP put the robe on. Her stated reason ("Because I'll
have to fly it where Enforcement can see me") reads as self-protection. The helmet-clasp inserts
show the struggle, but nobody *says* what it cost her, so a casual viewer may file her as cold, not
torn. *Fix:* one line in 24, after "You're shy?" / "Yes." (helmet back on), then: SEO: "I was
wrong to wait. I won't be twice." This is honest in the air, and it plants ep 6. Also, Pell's
reply "SHE ASKED ABOUT YOUR PILOT" is confusing, because Seo *is* the pilot and "your" suggests a
third person. Make it **"SHE ASKED ABOUT YOU."** It is shorter and colder, and the tag hurts more.

**Is Arakawa formidable and honourable without being a fool? Yes, with one logic gap.** "I wrote
four of them", the pencil duel and "It wasn't to you" are a hunter you respect. The gap: in 25,
"Eleven minutes on the ground, and she would have them." She is at L2, about 60,000 km above the
far side. She cannot be on the ground in eleven minutes, so the temptation is false, and a sharp
viewer sees her honour as costless. Make the slip and the cost real (MUST-FIX 5). Her promise "I
will not come down before sunset" also needs a visible reason she'd give it. It's there in 18 (the
army that "could not lift its bows"), but only as a log line. That's fine.

**Does Mira have her own story? Yes, a quiet one:** the impossible map ("Wrong already. Things keep
arriving."), the reputation left on the shelf, the offer to wear the robe, the J.V. page, and
calling the ground. What she lacks is a moment where her method *fails* and costs her, as the
bible's "believes evidence is enough" flaw invites. Kaguya's half-pass ("I'll think of a word") is
never paid off. NICE-TO-HAVE: Kaguya's word arrives in 26: "Dr Sen. The word is 'cartographer'."

**Does the Baron's goodbye hurt? It hurts in 26 ("Ah. You'll have to remember the hat." / "I'll
remember the hat." is the episode's best goodbye beat). It would hurt more if anyone asked him to
come.** In 22 Jax says only "You're really staying." and "Then why do it?" Nobody fights for him.
E01 ended with "We definitely left him," so the crew, and especially Jax, should *ask* this time.
Add in 26, before "This is a real goodbye": JAX: "The trunk's empty. I checked." BARON: "Then it is
travelling light at last." And give Lucía, who carried him, one beat. Hers is already there ("You
weighed nothing."), which is good. Also, his choice to stay has no build between 12 (a whiter
moustache) and 22. One silent INSERT in 15 or 19 (the Baron alone at the doorway, looking at the
Valley, not at the ship) would plant it.

**Lucía–Seo (B thread).** This is the strongest B thread in either episode. One logic gap: in 13
Lucía finds hard proof that Seo staged her crash, and she never tells Jax or anyone. Seo's "Don't
tell the others" refers only to the photo. Make it cover both (SEO: "Don't tell the others. Any of
it."), and Lucía's silence becomes a choice she will regret in ep 6.

## 4. CLARITY: **FIX**

**Could a viewer who missed Episode 1 follow this? Mostly, with three holes.**
1. **Nobody says the guest's name.** "Kaguya" is never spoken; the dialogue calls her "the
   Princess" and "Your Highness". "Munchausen" is never spoken; he is "the Baron" and
   "Hieronymus". The episode is sold on "the Princess of the Far Side", and a newcomer never learns
   they are watching *the* Kaguya or *the* Baron Munchausen. MUST-FIX 4.
2. **Why the bank seeing them costs the air** is assumed from E01. The nearest line is "So will the
   bank" (0) and Arakawa's "First in the queue is your bank" (5). Add the mechanism in one clause in
   the cold open: PIP: "In ninety seconds it will see us. So will the bank, and the bank is still
   repossessing our air."
3. **Why they are on one engine and half a recycler.** E01's final script shows the left engine
   *restored* after the fee (cold open), and the six o'clock recovery cut off mid-word as Earth set
   (30). E02 says the bank took the engine and "the good half" of the recycler. That contradicts
   what an E01 viewer saw. One line in 3 fixes both: LUCÍA: "Temi got two seconds before Earth set.
   She used them well. One engine, half the recycler."

**The siege geometry (far side, relay at L2, the bank's signal from Earth, sunset deadline): clear
at the level of "two faces", once Seo draws the envelope (24).** The envelope is a good device. It
is shown *late*, though: at 28:50, for a siege that began at 6:30. Before that, the viewer must
assemble it from Arakawa's radio speech (5) and Seo's libration speech (7). Move a simpler version
of the envelope forward: in 8, Seo's glove points up at the Lantern ("Except from up there."). Add
a DATA VISUAL there of the same three dots, the far face shaded SHE SEES, and no words. Then 24's
envelope is a payoff, not a lecture.

**What is still unclear, and will draw comments:**
- **Why leave at all?** The Keeping House is pressurised, lit and warm, and Kaguya has lived
  through every two-week night for a thousand years. Lucía's "Not two weeks" is about the *ship*.
  Once the bank is paid, the only reason to run is Arakawa coming down after sunset, and the script
  never says she will. *Fix:* in 5, Arakawa adds "At sunset I come down for you, lawfully." And
  give Kaguya one line on why the crew can't shelter with her, which also serves the theme and the
  Baron: KAGUYA (in 9 or 19): "You may stay as long as you like. Everyone who stays is still here."
- **Why fly low, and why at sunset?** Low flight does not hide anyone from L2, which looks straight
  down on the far side. See MUST-FIX 5 for a version that makes the timing come from Arakawa's word.

**Exposition in disguise (quoted):**
- SEO (7): "The Moon wobbles. Near the edge, Earth peeps over. Your ship is parked where Earth will
  see it in nine minutes. I've been sitting here two days watching it peep. Like a cat at a window.
  There's nothing else on. Nothing from Earth reaches the radio out here. Best silence there is."
  These are 50 words of science, and they repeat what the cold open showed. Keep "Your ship is
  parked where Earth will see it in nine minutes. I've been sitting here two days watching it peep.
  Like a cat at a window." Cut the rest; the radio quiet is in "What was real".
- ARAKAWA (5): "I hold the relay at L2. It is the only way any signal reaches where you are." This
  is in character (she is formal and states terms), so it passes.
- MIRA (19): "The bank's lien is on the ship's registry. So is her warrant. Everything hunting us is
  hunting the registry." It's necessary, but it is the sag's heaviest line. Give the second half
  to PIP: it is *his* registry and *his* realisation, and it makes "Me." his own discovery.
- ASTOLFO (2): "Everything Earth loses comes here. Time, vows, reputations, wits. Everything except
  madness." Acceptable: it is Ariosto's own inventory and in a delighted voice.
- MIRA (3): "It isn't dark. It never was. It just doesn't face us." This is a science correction
  dressed as a line, but it is the episode's thesis and short. Keep it.

**Small clarity points:** PIP is "the lens on Jax's wrist" (3, 4, 19), the unbolted brass lamp
under Astolfo's arm (10, 20, 23) and back on the bridge (25), with no on-screen bridge between
the two. One staging note, plus one INSERT in 3 of Jax clipping a wrist relay with PIP's lens on
it, prevents "how is he in two places". The relay is called "the Lantern" only on a screen INSERT
in 5. Nobody says it aloud until 8's action line, so say it once in 5.

## Would a stranger watch all of it and click Episode 3?

A stranger who arrives for "a princess on the far side of the Moon" gets a strong cold open, a
delightful first ten minutes (the Baron in the trunk, Astolfo dusting in a vacuum, "Put back the
watch", "I wrote four of them", a calm stranger who cut her own fuel line), and then the best
twenty minutes of comedy and feeling this show has written. The branch and the invoice, the sighs,
PIP's vote, the robe, "And go.", "You'll have to remember the hat." Then a real escape over a real
Earthrise and a tag that tells them the pilot they've just learned to like is a spy. That is
enough to click Episode 3, especially with "Next stop, Mars. The invoice said so." pointing at
Don Quixote. The risk is not the ending. It is the middle. From about minute 20 to 27 nothing
presses on the crew from outside. Five seated scenes in a row, several of them before the same
bamboo blind, explain a magic robe's terms to a viewer who has not heard Arakawa's voice for
twelve minutes. A browsing YouTube viewer leaves in that stretch, not because it is bad but
because it is still. Fix the geography and the arithmetic so the regulars stay loyal. Say
"Kaguya" and "Munchausen" so strangers know what they are watching. Put one physical threat and
Arakawa back into Act 3. Then most strangers who reach minute 10 will reach the Earthrise, and most
who reach the Earthrise will click.
