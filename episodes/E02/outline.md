# SECRET UNIVERSE — Episode 2: "The Princess of the Far Side"

**Outline v1, head writer. Waiting on the showrunner's ruling. No script until then.**
Genre: siege, with a bittersweet, grown-up almost-romance. Music: koto, shakuhachi and strings,
around the main theme. On the far side the ship's receiver picks up nothing from Earth, only
the Moon's own stations.
Running time: about 35:25 (cold open 1:25 · title 0:10 · Act 1 8:15 · Act 2 9:05 · Act 3 8:10 ·
Act 4 6:40 · tag 1:00 · "What was real" 0:40). These are the sums of the scene targets.
Budget: about 4,300 spoken words, at 155 wpm plus silences. The E01 lesson applied: every
scene's word budget is below its seconds. Units: 32 (cold open, 30 scenes, tag).
Speaking roles: 9. Settings: 6.

**Picks up from script-v3.** The ship is hiding behind the Moon, where Earth can't see it, so
the bank can't take the air. If they leave the far side, the bank takes it. At six o'clock,
before Earth set, the bank's recovery had already taken one engine and half the air recyclers.
The Baron is in the LOT 14 trunk, without his coat and hat (those are on the carved horse's
head on the apron). The silver hatchet went over the rim. Mira has her clamp key and has
written VOSS in her notebook. PIP's reports are READ 1.3 seconds after he sends them.
Chichikov bought the parcel's *carriage*, and let them go.

**The siege in one breath, for viewers with no interest in astronomy:**
*The Moon has two faces. Earth watches one: the bank is waiting there. Commander Arakawa
watches the other, from the relay at L2. The crew is on the face she can see, and the Sun is
going down.*

## 1. Logline and why you keep watching

**Logline:** Hiding behind the Moon with half their air gone, a crew of fugitives begs help from
Kaguya, the Moon's oldest resident and keeper of the Valley of Lost Things. A Directorate
commander holds the only relay and waits for nightfall. A stranded pilot who never lies in the
air offers to fly them out, and the audience is the only one who knows she is lying about
everything else.

**Why you keep watching:** A princess who has never said no to anyone sets the crew impossible
tasks, and the only one who passes is the spy. The way out of the siege costs a memory, and the
person who offers to pay it is the one nobody expected. In a valley that holds everything Earth
ever lost, the captain walks past a jar with his name on it, and only we see it.

## 6. Running gags, escalated from Episode 1

1. **PIP's reports.** In E01 he files #1,204–1,207, and one is READ, seen only by the audience.
   In E02, behind the Moon, everything comes back UNSENT (3, 10, 23): he files on the Atlas, on
   Kaguya's unlicensed lost-property office, and on a staircase. Then the relay opens (25), the
   backlog goes out, and READ comes back on every one at 1.3 seconds. This time *PIP sees it*:
   "Someone reads them." The audience's secret has become his. Next escalation (ep 4): he
   starts writing *to* the reader.
2. **Nobody can remember Chichikov.** In E01 he was "man, average", "Mr Chichester", and in soft
   focus until the tag. In E02 he is on the bridge of the most observant officer in the
   Directorate, and she can't say when he came aboard. He has no lines at all, and nobody,
   including the audience at first, notices that he never speaks. Arakawa's log: *Voss observer
   aboard. Name:* — left blank.
3. **Correcting Earth's version, now turned on the Baron.** In E01 he corrected Earth ("my
   pigtail, and the horse"). In E02 Kaguya corrects *his* version of *her* (4: "I wasn't
   carried. I left. I'd finished my letter"; "Your spirit was in Russia, shooting ducks"). In 22
   Astolfo corrects them both: "Ariosto says I sniffed my wits back up my nose. I used a funnel.
   Like a gentleman." Next escalation (ep 3): Sancho corrects Quixote's correction of Cervantes.

## 7. The five things I'm least sure about

1. **The Baron stays in the Valley.** He stops fading there, so the goodbye in 26 is real where
   E01's was a fake, and he arrives in ep 10 by cannonball from the Moon. The alternative is that
   he stays aboard as the recurring stowaway the bible describes. Staying makes ep 2 cost more and
   frees ep 3 for Idris; keeping him aboard keeps a proven comic engine. Which do you want?
2. **The bank fix.** Kaguya pays the lien from a forgotten fortune (a deposit dormant since 1889),
   and the crew owe her an impossible task to be named. That creates a season-long debt to a
   friendly but dangerous creditor, and leaves the ship legally clear of the bank (though still
   wanted) for Mars. The alternative is that Chichikov buys the lien and Voss owns their air, which
   is darker and ties tightly into ep 5's "Follow the parcel", but it gives the escape to the
   villain. Which?
3. **"You've worn one before"** (16). Is that too explicit a clue for ep 2? Kaguya says nothing
   more, and the robe is the bible's own image of concealment. A softer version is a long look and
   "Not you", leaving the reason out.
4. **PIP's cost.** He keeps his memory but loses one label, the oldest one: the first sender of the
   parcel. Is that a big enough price for the "real choice" (PIP volunteered to forget everything),
   and does touching the parcel's origin cause trouble for ep 10's restored label?
5. **The moon-lady's song** (21): "Too lonely and cold is my home in the moon" (Pu Songling, Giles
   1880), heard on the receiver as Kaguya's unseen neighbour, played with dignity and no joke. The
   character bank flags Chang'e as an owner decision. Do we keep the song, or cut it to a koto
   phrase?

## 8. "What was real" (about 40 seconds, read by PIP)

> PIP: What was real. The far side of the Moon is real, and it is not dark: it gets as much
> sunlight as the side we see. It simply never faces Earth, so no signal from Earth reaches it
