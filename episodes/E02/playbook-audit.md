**Headline.** A beautiful, sad, clever script with two first-rate set pieces (14, the branch and the
invoice; 23, the robe) and a superb villain-side duel (18, 25). Its weaknesses: **Act 3 opens with five
talk scenes in a row** (17, 19, 20, 21, 22 — about six minutes before the robe); **the escape plan is
explained in full (24) and then works exactly as explained**; and **the limb run (28) repeats Episode 1's
"Mira calls the ground in the dark" engine**. Kaguya, always seated behind a blind, is also the most static
guest we could have — animation can turn that rule into a spectacle instead of a limit.

## The five highest-value upgrades

**1. Turn Kaguya's blind into a shadow-play screen** [26][27]. Keep the rule (she speaks only from behind a
blind indoors, and her eyes over the fan are a silent beat). Light the blind from behind so she is a
silhouette on paper: a silhouette needs no lip-sync and can act with her whole body — the fan, the sleeves,
a laugh that shakes her shoulders. And in 9, each task she brushes on a slip comes alive for 3 s in ink
(the silver-rooted branch grows; a dragon's jewel glints; for Mira, the Valley map redraws itself as heaps
arrive; for Lucía, the brush stops). An original ink style of our own, built from Heian court painting in
general, never any film or anime. Cost: A for the silhouette, B for the slips. It turns our most static guest
into the episode's look.

**2. Cut 17 and lighten Act 3's talk run.** Move 17's last insert (Mira's fresh page, "J.V." and "YOU'VE
WORN ONE BEFORE") to the end of 16, so Act 2 ends on the written question [15]. Arakawa's "six hours" and
Lucía's "there's a bird on your ship" become the cold head of 19, heard over the leap. In 22 (written "fast up
to the hatchet"), give it a physical gag: in the low gravity the Baron sinks slowly into the silver chaff heap
as he speaks, until only his helmet and moustache show at "enough of him to unpack" [28]. Act 3 then reads
loud (crow) → talk (vote) → quiet (edge) → comic (heap) → heart (robe). Saves ~40 s. No line changes in 16,
19 or 22.

**3. Stop explaining the escape (24).** Heist rule: a plan that will work is shown in pieces, not explained.
Keep PIP's return, "I have thirty-one", Lucía's questions to Seo and "I was wrong to wait", Kaguya's loan and
its reason, and the envelope as a **silent** insert (three circles, EARTH SEES / SHE SEES). Cut the spoken
explanation of how sunset, the word and the Moon in between will save them, down to Seo's "Then you call the
ground. I fly. Low." The audience learns the plan as 25–29 execute it — Arakawa's two drifting readouts in 25
already explain why she won't follow. Saves ~35 s and gives Act 4 surprise.

**4. Make the limb run (28) a different engine from Episode 1's dark wall.** Keep the lines. Stage it as a
side-on race [19] over the real far-side terminator plate (27): the ship a small silhouette skimming the dark
ground, and — new — the *Steadfast* seen as a light dropping into the top of frame, two hours behind and
getting closer each cut [9] [14]. Inside, one shot only of dash-lit helmets; Lucía's "She's shaking!" plays in
the engine bay as a Disadvantage gag [18]: holding a cracked mounting together with both arms and one boot,
ribbon in her teeth, before "…Let her shake." The episode then has a real action set piece. Cost: B.

**5. Draw Jax's lie in 14, then let the invoice erase it** [26][10]. While Jax tells the storm of light and the
creature with scales like solar panels, cut to it in a heroic, over-coloured cutaway (Jax, taller, fighting
with a spanner). When Lucía reads the invoice, each item she reads replaces a piece of the cutaway with the
plain truth: the storm becomes her welding sparks, the jaws become her bench vice, the jewel-throated monster
becomes the bank's coffee machine with its green light. Kaguya's laugh plays over the last plain image. This
is the episode's SHORT (cut to about 60 s, from "Tell me." to "It's framing it."): needs no context, has an
escalation and a button. Cost: B.

(Also: 23 should borrow E01 scene 2's grammar for PIP's forgetting — the labels and report lines on his
screen dimming letter by letter — so the audience reads the loss without explanation. Keep 23 out of promo
cuts, as the script says. And drop the four "VISIBLE MOUTH" limits: the puppets lip-sync, so Lucía, Jax and
Seo can play their big lines on their faces; Kaguya, Arakawa and Chichikov keep their rules because those
are character rules, not pipeline rules.)
