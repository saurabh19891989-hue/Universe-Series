# Episode 8 — "Havoc Under the Mountain": sources

Rule (bible 7a): at least three sources from at least two cultures, mixed; mechanisms, images and
traits taken; nothing retold scene by scene. This episode draws on **five** literary sources from
**four traditions** (Chinese, Korean, K'iche' Maya, Greek), plus one recurring Russian character
and one fact from Chinese astronomy. Card numbers refer to `research/library/sources/`. The plot
is ours and appears in none of them: the Voss contract and its clauses, the Residence on Io, the
Chancery of Dignities, the Year Clock, the Seat carried as freight, the precedence farce, the
State Barge, the fall through the belts, the catch in the Great Red Spot, the stroke, the
lifeboat, and Arakawa's file.

**Showrunner ruling (season-01.md) applied to every item below:** Sun Wukong is honoured in some
temples, so he is the hero; the comedy is aimed at the contract and the officials. No Buddha,
bodhisattva or deity appears, is named or is joked about.

## 1. *Journey to the West* (Xiyou ji), chapters 1–7 (China, c. 1592) — card 12
- **Edition read (by the library):** Timothy Richard, *A Mission to Heaven: a great Chinese epic
  and allegory* (Shanghai: Christian Literature Society, 1913), Internet Archive, Cornell copy.
  Public domain everywhere (Richard d. 1919). **Not used: Arthur Waley's *Monkey* (1942), which is
  in copyright; Anthony Yu's translation; any film, television series, cartoon, stage design or
  game.** Richard's Christianising vocabulary ("God", "angel") and his misattribution of the
  author are not repeated.
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **the Register struck out by its own subject.** In the novel he takes the brush
    and strikes out his own name and every named monkey. Ours: the Voss Company's Register of
    Dignities has an unprinted rule that any owner may strike his own entry; the pen has been
    laid in front of him twelve times; he does it at the climax, by his own choice, to save the
    crew. What a struck entry looks like (a line you can read under, against clean paper) is ours.
  - *Mechanism:* **the post with no rank.** In the novel he is made keeper of the horses, works
    hard ("the horses grew fat"), asks the rank and is told it is "the position before getting any
    rank at all". Ours: the Voss title "Grand Protector of Jupiter" is filed at Grade Zero in
    those words; because it has no rank he cannot give notice; and the same empty grade is how
    the crew get through the gate.
  - *Mechanism:* **the gate that does not know you** (until you hold a post, the guards cannot
    admit you). Ours: the flip-board's "The unknown are not admitted."
  - *Mechanism:* **the empty title as pacification** (a grand name granted "only as an empty
    title"). Ours: the Chancery's four hundred dignities, "each held by somebody who used to be
    difficult". We do **not** use or joke about his own title in the novel and in worship (Great
    Sage, Equal of Heaven); the Voss title is invented.
  - *Mechanism:* **proof of travel that proves you never left.** Ours, transposed as the library
    card directs: "The Residence: Jupiter, and all that Jupiter holds", meaning everything inside
    Jupiter's magnetic field. On screen he says only: "I ran to the edge of everything once, and
    found I had not left. The mountain that followed, I earned, and whose it was is not your
    business." The hand, the wager, the writing on the pillar and the figure who set the wager
    are **not shown, named or joked about.**
  - *Image:* **the mountain on top of him.** Ours: a clause, "the dignity shall rest upon the
    holder", with the literal weight of the mountain; and our own turn, that the weight was a
    cork.
  - *Image:* **the iron staff kept in his ear, the size of a needle.** Ours: for twelve years it
    has been his sewing needle; it becomes the staff once, in one held shot. He strikes no one.
  - *Image and trait:* stone-born; king of a mountain of fruit; eyes that see through any
    disguise; seventy-two changes; a pinch of his own hairs becoming copies; leaving on a cloud.
    **To verify before lock:** the eyes, the seventy-two changes, the hairs and the cloud are
    from the novel's first chapters but were not among the passages the library card records as
    read in Richard; check each against Richard's text or the Chinese original (public domain)
    and cut any that cannot be sourced there.
  - *Trait:* vain, quick, joyful, honest about his own skill and inventive about rules; asks the
    rank of every post.
  - *Quotation (Richard, ch. III, public domain):* "There is an end of the Registration. I am no
    longer under your control." Used once, in 22, lower-cased, as his own line.
  - *What Earth gets wrong (card):* he was a stable-master before he was a rebel, and the insult
    was a job title ("I made havoc over a job description"; "Earth leaves out the horses"); he
    struck out every named monkey, not only himself ("Earth thinks I am vainer than I am").
- **Not taken:** any scene of the novel; the heavenly court or its ruler as characters or as a
  joke (it is "a great court" in one line); the peaches, the elixir, the furnace; the wager and
  the palm as such; Guanyin; the monk and the pilgrimage; the fillet on his head; the tiger skin;
  the dragon kings' armour; the five-hundred-year sentence as a number (the Voss term is one
  year of Jupiter). No opera aria, costume or face-painting pattern is copied; the music uses
  the instruments only.

## 2. *Hong Gildong jeon* (Korea, 17th century or later) — card 17
- **Edition read (by the library):** H. N. Allen, *Korean Tales* (New York and London: Putnam,
  1889), tale IX. Public domain everywhere (Allen d. 1932). A free retelling; used for
  mechanisms only.
- **Taken:**
  - *Mechanism:* **eight identical men, each with the same scar.** Ours: eight copies of *The
    Unscheduled*, each with the same sulphur scar down the port side; Arakawa's plot reads
    "9 OF 9".
  - *Mechanism:* **the trap set for the moment you bow.** Ours: the Hall of Deference, whose
    floor opens when a visitor bows to the Board.
  - *Mechanism:* **demand the rank you were denied.** Ours, inverted: after twelve years of
    asking his rank, he asks instead about his precedence, and wins with it.
  - *Link:* Seo's cover papers carry the name Hong Gildong (canon, E02). She recognises the
    eight copies ("I know this story"), and Idris, who knows about her papers (E05), lets her
    know that he knows.
- **Not taken:** the plot; the temple raid and Allen's hostile picture of monks; the island and
  its people; any screen version.

## 3. *Popol Vuh*, Book II (K'iche' Maya, 16th century) — card 18
- **Edition read (by the library):** Lewis Spence, *The Popol Vuh* (London: David Nutt, 1908), a
  summary and commentary. Public domain. A living sacred text: used for structure only.
- **Taken:**
  - *Structure:* **houses of ordeal, each with a trick solution.** Ours: three, none of them
    Maya in look or content: the Gate of Rank (solved by the empty grade), the Hall of Deference
    (solved by not bowing), the lake (solved by twelve years of watching it).
  - *Mechanism:* **wooden figures set up so that visitors will bow to them.** Ours: the Board of
    the Voss Company in wax under glass. ("A real director would have asked what you cost.")
- **Not taken:** the Twins, the lords of Xibalba, the named houses, the ball game, any sacrifice
  imagery, any Maya design.

## 4. Pu Songling, *Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio* (China, c. 1700) — card 19
- **Edition read (by the library):** H. A. Giles (London: De La Rue, 1880), vol. I, tale V.
  Public domain everywhere.
- **Taken:** *Mechanism:* **the power that fails the moment you brag about it.** Ours: the
  Chamberlain's boast that he is always the senior officer present, having had the others
  transferred, is what pins him to his own chair when the dignity devolves.
- **Not taken:** the priest, the paper moon, the moon-lady, the examination.

## 5. Homer, *Odyssey* XXI (Greece) — card 15 (for the season secret; never named on screen)
- **Edition read (by the library):** Samuel Butler's prose translation (1900), Project Gutenberg
  #1727. Public domain everywhere.
- **Taken:**
  - *Image:* the bow that strong men cannot string; the owner turning it over to look for worm
    in the horn; stringing it as easily as a bard strings a lyre; the string singing like a
    swallow. Ours: the Bow of Office ("three wardens tried"), Jax's look along the horn, the one
    move, the one clear note "like a bird".
  - *Image:* the arrow through a row of axe-heads. Ours: one ring only, the eye of the winch
    hook. The twelve are left for the finale.
  - *Motif:* Noman. Ours, lightly and once each: the warrant's "nobody", small n; a warrant for
    a title with "nobody in it"; the Europa entry registered to "No One" (which also points to
    Verne's Nemo, card 14, for E09). The Cyclops trick itself is **not** used: it is too plain a
    signal before episode 10.
- **Not taken:** any event of the poem; any name from it; anything that would let a viewer who
  does not already suspect it name the captain.

## 6. Nikolai Gogol, *Dead Souls* (Russia, 1842) — card 02 (recurring character)
- **Edition read (by the library):** D. J. Hogarth's translation (1915), Project Gutenberg #1081.
- **Taken:** Chichikov, the forgettable collector of names on a register, as the witness on the
  contract ("P. I. Ch—") and the silent observer who adds a name to his list. No dialogue.

## A fact, not a source
In Chinese astronomy Jupiter is the Year Star (Suixing): it takes nearly twelve years to circle
the sky, and that circuit lies behind the twelve-year cycle. Ours: the contract's "one year,
local". To be confirmed in the science check before the line "Where I come from, this planet is
called the Year Star" is locked.
