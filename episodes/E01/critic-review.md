## OVERALL VERDICT: READY AFTER FIXES

This is a good pilot. The Baron is a real character, the comedy has a voice, the act breaks turn, the plants survive, and the ending costs something. It is not ready to lock, for four reasons:

1. **Two plot holes a sharp viewer will name in the comments.** The bank can repossess the ship by remote control (cold open) but apparently forgets to at six o'clock. Chichikov offers a fortune for something he says he can buy at six "for the price of a sandwich".
2. **The climactic action beat (the hatchet throw) has no clear geography, and its physics is wrong.**
3. **The stowaway reveal has no mechanism.**
4. **The runtime claim is not honest.** The script plays at about 38½ minutes, not 35:50. One scene is over the 2½-minute ceiling (21), one is at it (6), and the cold open overruns the rule.

All of these can be fixed in one revision pass without touching the structure.

## 2. COMEDY: **FIX** (strong voice, one overused shape, a few stock jokes)

**Is each character funny in their own way? Mostly yes.**
- Jax sells: the aunt, Section twelve, "We can work with 'The'".
- Mira deflates.
- PIP is literal: "man, average" / "Average".
- Lucía is blunt.
- The Baron is the offended guest ("Earth has no respect for horses").
- Noakes runs the bureaucratic euphemism, which is an excellent extra engine.
- Chichikov is polite precision.

**Exceptions:**
- Lucía's "I'm not being right at you. I'm being right near you." (17) is embroidery, not bluntness.
- "Put a jumper on!" (0) is a British idiom in the mouth of an American-voiced captain (bible §4).

**Repeated joke shape: "It isn't X. It's Y."** It appears at least ten times, and the audience will feel the machinery by Act 2:
- "isn't a job. It's volunteering." (1)
- "It wasn't a compliment. It was a time." (3)
- "Your record isn't late… It's blank." (3)
- "Those aren't opposites… They're departments." (4)
- "It's not personal. It's a category." (6)
- "That's not cold. That's personal." (15)
- "No. It changes what the money is." (17)
- "It isn't money. I've checked." (20)
- "It's not a compliment. You lie like…" (21)
- "It's the polite word for one." (18; a variant)

Keep the best four ("departments", "blank", "category", "I've checked") and rewrite the rest.

**Jokes that explain themselves:**
- "No one believes it. Including him." (29, action line). This tells the editor what the "Darts" joke means, which proves it isn't working.
- Clause nine is explained twice, by Chichikov and then by PIP reading sub-clause four (22). Cut PIP's.
- "Customs vehicle one has stopped. It is still asking nicely." (24) re-explains the PLEASE card. It's a good button, so keep it, but don't add more.

**No stretch of more than 60 s in a comedy scene lacks a laugh or a turn**, except the deliberate second half of 21. **PASS** on that test.

## 3. DRAMA AND CHARACTER: **FIX**

**Jax:** **PASS.** He listens to the end (21), watches the Baron forced to tell the tidy version, and his "Neither" follows from that. Making Jax silent in 17 was a smart punch-up.

**Mira:** **PASS.** The want from 7 ("write down what's there before it's sold") pays off cleanly in 27–28, and "Nothing, sir. Earth hasn't said yes." is the right weapon in the right hand.

**Lucía:** **FIX (must).** Her want is the strongest temptation in the episode: zeroing her mother's water debt (16). In 22 she gives it up in one laugh line: "Oh, thank God. I hated it." Her mother's account is never mentioned again, not even in 30's cost count. That cheapens her and breaks Drama rule 1 for her: the cost must be real.

**The Baron's sadness:** earned, not sentimental, in 6 ("You used to."), 14 (the held wide) and 22 (tidy and terrified). Scene 21 drifts toward sentiment in its back half ("To be seen from it… Children."), and it is too long for its job.

**The cost at the end:** **lands.** The fee, the job, the wages and their names are all counted, and the silence is held. But see Lucía above, and the bank hole (MUST-FIX 1), which, once fixed, sharpens the cost: they don't just lose the ship at listed ports, they lose its air in minutes.

**Is Chichikov frightening enough?** In Acts 1–3, yes. "We supply her water." is chilling, and the soft-focus rule is good. In Act 4 he only sips tea and writes a name. After "Then at six I buy him for the price of a sandwich" he never acts, so the threat deflates. Give him one move: for example, a silent insert in 25 of his hand typing just before Earth suddenly asks "A reason?". Then Earth's delay becomes his doing, and the tag's "It has moved" reads as if he let it happen.

**Is the villain right about one thing in every scene (Drama rule 2)?**
- 5: "a long time to be loyal". Right.
- 12: "who listens?". Right.
- 16: relisting cures him. Right.
- 22: the ink stays. Right.

**PASS.**

**Do we care by minute 10?** About the Baron, yes; he arrives at about 7:30 honest time. About Jax, mostly: the blank record is a good hook. About Lucía, not yet: she has one scene and an intercom line before she vanishes for 7 minutes.

**Would they click episode 2?** The tag (knocks in the trunk, then *nobody*) is strong. The *destination* for episode 2 is only an old man's whim ("Go to the far side"). MUST-FIX 1 turns it into a necessity.

## 4. CLARITY OF PREMISE: **FIX**

A first-timer will get these, without a lecture:
- **Legends are real.** The horn plays itself (15), and Jax knows him as "the swamp guy" (6).
- **Someone is doing it on purpose.** The contract is dated the 24th, the deletion was the 27th, and Mira writes VOSS.

The middle rule, that **deleting an address makes them fade**, is muddied in two places:
- **Scene 14.** "My stories have stopped. A little, three months ago… The letters stopped. A man who gets no letters begins to wonder if he has an address." That places the cause three months *before* the delisting and makes it about *not being written to*. A newcomer can't tell which rule is the real one: listing, or being listened to. 12 ("we are buying him an audience") and 21 ("You are the audience") pull the same way. Fix the 14 line so the two rules become one: "Three months ago the post stopped. Then the street names began to go out, one a week. Today, the door."
- **Vocabulary.** The script uses *withdrawn*, *delisted*, *deleted*, *relisted*, *no longer anywhere*, *residue*, *thin* and *struck off* (Mira's other meaning). Pick two words, *delisted* and *nowhere*, and use them consistently. Keep "withdrawn" only on official screens, where the euphemism is the joke.

**Exposition in disguise (quoted):**
- 0, PIP: "Everything you say takes one point three seconds to reach her, and everything she says takes one point three to come back." Trim to "She is in Lagos. You are arguing with the past."; the joke teaches the rule.
- 11, PIP: "A man who is not listed cannot receive anything, Captain. Not a parcel. Not a letter. Apparently not even ink." Acceptable, because the last clause makes it a joke.
- 16, Chichikov: "You would be relisted by tonight. Somewhere again. Your stories would come true." It states the cure as a rule. Acceptable as a sales pitch.
- 17, Mira: "PIP. When was the Silver Hatchet withdrawn?" This is "as you know": she processed the seizure herself at 14:09 and wrote entry 41 in 4. Fix: "Withdrawn on the twenty-seventh. This is dated the twenty-fourth."
- 22, PIP: "Clause nine, sub-clause four…" It doubles Chichikov's explanation. Cut it.
- 27, Mira: "One camera in orbit has seen this floor, by the light bouncing off the crater walls. And a laser has measured every metre of it." A science lecture, and inaccurate (see 6).
- 18, Noakes: "The Atlas office is three floors below the Voss Company." World-building in one line. Keep it; it's in conflict.

### Cause and effect

1. **The bank (MUST-FIX 1).** The cold open establishes that the bank disables the ship *by remote, in flight*: engine, lights, heating. At 18:00 the same remote takes "the rest of the vessel, including the air". Scene 30 says only "The bank will take the ship at any listed port." Something must stop the 18:00 signal, and the script has the answer already planted. The far side is where "No signal reaches it" (29), and it is the season's episode-2 setting.
2. **Chichikov's offer (MUST-FIX 2).** Why pay 11% more than the ship for a parcel, and a contract for the Baron, when at 18:00 he can "buy him for the price of a sandwich" (22)? Jax's own argument in 19 says the same: "He gets sold at six anyway, cheaper." The script implies the answer ("A parcel is a string… I need both ends"), but never says what the courier's signature *buys* that the auction can't.
3. **Seized goods vs the delivery slip.** Mira says in 6, "He can't sign for seized goods. No one can." In 11, Jax pitches a delivery signature anyway, with Mira present as the witness. Add a clause to PIP's 41(c) in 7: "…and if delivered there, they were never abandoned."
4. **The contract Mira reads (17).** Where did it come from? In 16 Chichikov leaves nothing on the table. In 18 Mira says "A man left it on a table." Add an INSERT in 16 as he rises: the contract left behind, the little arrows drawn.
5. **The two-key clamp.**
   - 22: "Seal the port."
   - 23: "Bay nine's sealed! His car's at two!"
   - 25: Noakes still needs Earth's authority to *lock* the clamp.
   - 27: Mira routes them back to bay nine anyway.
   - 28: one key (hers) can *RELEASE* a two-key clamp, and taking it means the clamp can't be re-locked. That logic holds.

   The words *sealed*, *locked* and *released* are confusing, though. Fix: in 23, "Bay nine's airlock is sealed! We go round outside!" In 25, use one verb pair throughout: the clamp is *closed*; Noakes wants it *locked*; Mira *opens* it.
6. **How Mira knows where they are (27).** She guides them "boulder by boulder" with no position data. One line: "PIP, give me your wheel count." / PIP: "Sending." Or: "I have your transponder. It's still listed."
7. **The stowaway (MUST-FIX 4).** The LOT 14 trunk was in the customs cage (6). Nobody carried it out: Lucía carried the Baron and Jax the parcel (22–23). In 29 the Baron stays on the apron as the ramp rises. In the tag he is in that trunk in the hold. And a *thinned* Baron, whose lock and stove fail, cannot do an impossible thing to get there. Plant it plainly. In 23 or 24, the trunk is strapped to the post-chaise's roof ("His luggage!" / "It's always been on the roof."). In 29, during "They load", Lucía heaves the trunk aboard with "He's not coming. His luggage is." It plays as a mundane sneak, which keeps the thinning rule intact.
8. **The hatchet throw geography (MUST-FIX 3).** See 6. Jax is *at the hooked rover*, and the grapple line runs from the rover's tail back to the crawler. He could chop it with the hatchet in his hand, so why throw it, and why does "eighty metres" matter? Fix: Jax and the parcel are already at the ship's ramp, the rover is hooked 80 m back, and Jax throws from the ramp to cut the line at the rover.
9. **Noakes "faint, radio" (27).** He is standing beside Mira at the console (25, 28 CONTINUOUS). Make it "NOAKES (O.S., beside her)".
10. **The READ receipt (30).** A receipt cannot appear on PIP's screen until about 2.6 s after sending (1.3 s out, 1.3 s back). The timestamp "READ — 00:00:01.3" is fine, but hold the insert 2.6 s. That is better anyway: it means someone was *waiting* for it.
11. **The wage count:** "eight months" (1, 5), then "eight months, three weeks and six days" rounded to nine (30), then "Don't make it ten". **Consistent.**
12. **The contract dates:** the 24th vs the 27th. **Consistent** with 10's "It's the twenty-seventh", 17, and the tag's "The twenty-seventh".

| Claim (scene) | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light delay about 1.3 s each way (0, 25, 28, 30) | **OK** | The Earth–Moon distance (~356,000–407,000 km) gives 1.19–1.36 s one way. Noakes's 2.6 s round trip is right. |
| One-sixth gravity (14, 23, 24, 29) | **OK** | 1.62 m/s², 0.165 g. Lucía's 3 m strides and the long hang over the gully are plausible. In 24, everyone floating an inch off their seats is consistent with the cab and its passengers falling together. |

| Permanent shadow, extreme cold, old ice, "Probably. Not certainly." ## Would a stranger watch all 35 minutes and click episode 2, and where do we lose them?

A stranger who reaches the holding bay will very likely finish. The Baron is the hook, and from "delicious by a fish" through the horn, the signing and the chase, the show earns its length. The tag (polite knocks in the trunk, then *nobody* refreshing on the warrant) is a real reason to click episode 2, especially once the far side becomes a necessity rather than a suggestion.

The most likely place to lose an *engaged* viewer is **around minute 24–26**. By then the "sign or don't sign" question has been argued in four straight talk scenes (17–20). Then the episode's longest and quietest scene (21, about 2:50 of two backs on a boulder) begins. It is beautiful, but its first minute repeats what we already know.

Cut 21 by 45 s, halve 19 and 20, and that sag closes. Fix the bank and Chichikov holes too, so the comments talk about the Baron and not the logic.
