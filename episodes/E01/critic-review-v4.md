## 5. Animation fit

Nearly everything is playable by the rig: talk, walks, takes, simple props, vehicles on paths, side-on
action, cutaways. No crowds. Specific flags:

- **14, the Clause 9 transformation** is the most effects-heavy beat in the episode (moustache combing,
  coat buttoning itself, cuffs, a redrawn cutaway, then springing back, buttons popping, beanstalk) and it
  is *not* a signature scene. Cheaper staging: hard pose swaps with a small sound on each (wild → tidy in
  one cut; tidy → wild in one cut, three button sprites flying), the cutaway layer cross-dissolving to the
  flat lounge and wiped up into the beanstalk. Same joke, about a third of the work. (S7)
- **8, the swamp drawing tearing and blowing away like ash:** particles. Cheaper: it splits down the middle
  and the two halves drop out of frame. (S12)
- **15, Lucía carrying the Baron overhead:** contact, but cheap if drawn as one combined rigid sprite (he
  lies stiff as a plank, which is funnier anyway). Note it in the shotlist.
- **19, the apron** (rover up the ramp road, a fired grapple line, the slew, the throw, Mira bounding, the
  ship lifting) costs more than the SHORT. Keep it side-on and in two plates; the throw as one glint on one
  arc is correct and cheap.
- **11, Mira steering from the back seat:** one arm reach, fine.

**Drawn cutaways:** about ten instances (swamp, eels, palace, rope, hatchet, swamp tearing, two cannonball
panels, bees/lounge/beanstalk, failed flickers). Right for a Baron episode, not overused; 8 has three and
that is the ceiling. The palace folding into the boulder and the failed flickers in 15 are the best uses.

**Reaction faces:** all five written by name, once each. Could reuse JAX — THE SALESMAN'S FREEZE on
"CAPTAIN: nobody" in the tag (the grin stays, the eyes move): it is exactly that moment. (N2)
**Physical business:** well written throughout (the hand and the spanner, Jax leaning into the letters,
Lucía steering with her knee, the contract floating, the hand opening and closing after the throw).

**PIP on the wrist is never introduced.** From 4 on he talks from Jax's wrist; no shot shows a stranger
what that is (5's "the little amber light" is the first mention). One insert at the top of 3 fixes it
(S13). E02 still "introduces" a forearm relay as new (E02 scene with REPORT #1,208): E02 must be conformed
(see section 7).

## 6. Voice notes

Mostly playable and well aimed (volume, pace, who it is aimed at). Two problems:

1. **The working voices are Kokoro** (STATUS.md). Kokoro does pace and nothing emotional. Notes like
   "(almost a whisper)", "(laughing already)", "(a huge laugh, from the boots)", "(very calm, very high
   voice)", "(genuinely hurt)" will be ignored by the engine. Each needs an edit-room equivalent: whisper =
   −8 dB, slower, close EQ; high voice = pitch shift; laughs = separate laugh stems placed in the gap;
   "the words breaking" (14) = chopped in the edit. The writer should mark which notes are edit-built.
   Good opportunity: render the tidied Baron's lines in 14 ("I took a scheduled shuttle…", "The shuttle
   had a lovely lounge") with a blander voice blend — "as if someone else is talking" becomes literal.
2. **Overwritten notes** (descriptions, not directions): "(soft, unhurried, delighted to meet you, as Jax
   jumps)" (4) → "(soft, unhurried)" and move "Jax jumps" to the action; "(softly, the way you hold a
   baby)" (9) → "(soft, slow)"; "(taking it in the chest, like a soldier)" (4) → "(a beat; then quiet,
   proud)". Nearly every line carries a note; lines that play themselves should lose theirs.

Agents cannot hear: the owner needs a listening reel of 0, 4, 13 and 15 before lock.

## 8. Rights, rules and science

**Rights.** Guests are public domain: the Baron (Raspe/Bürger; every tale used — swamp and pigtail,
Oczakow cannonball, frozen post-horn, hatchet to the Moon, the bean, straw rope, the Sultan's bees, the
King of the Moon, the fish — is in the book); Chichikov (Gogol). Doré 1862 is public domain and the brief
says "in spirit, never traced". No film designs. Two echoes to note:
- "Filing it would be courageous" is the bible's acknowledged borrowing of the *Yes, Minister* idea ("that
  would be courageous"). It is one word and canon, so I do not ask for a change, but the showrunner should
  know it is the one line in the episode a British viewer will place.
- "It's not personal. It's a category." leans on "It's not personal, it's business". Low risk; N3 offers
  "It's not an insult. It's a category." if you want it clean.
No other line I can place in a known film or TV show.

**Rules.** No living-religion figure. No AI-disclosure wording anywhere. "What was real" is present.
Production note (not the script): ShadowCam is a NASA instrument on Korea's Danuri orbiter; its images
carry partner credits, so check the licence of any ShadowCam-type plate before logging it in `assets/`
(scenes 3, 9, 12, WWR).

**Science.**
- Vacuum silence: used well (the silent knock, the chase, the throw; the tag's knock is the first we hear).
- Light delay: Temi, Noakes's "two point six seconds" (correct round trip), the clamp's 1.3 s, READ 1.3 s.
  **One slip, M7:** in 0 the drone stops 1.3 s after "Accepted". The drone is the bank's, commanded from
  Earth: the acceptance takes 1.3 s to arrive and the order 1.3 s to come back, so 2.6 s.
- Low gravity: hang time, the floating contract, Lucía's carry: correct. "One-sixth gravity. A child could
  do it" is the Baron's logic failing, which is the joke: fine.
- Shackleton at the south pole, the Sun skimming the horizon, Earth low and nearly still: correct in 2, 6,
  11, 13, 19. **One slip, M6:** 15's jump "Black below. Sun above." At the pole the Sun is never above; it
  is on the horizon.
- 20: Earth setting behind the limb a few minutes after leaving the pole, the far side unlit by Earth:
  correct.
- "What was real": accurate as written (floor "may not have seen the Sun for billions of years" is properly
  hedged; one point three seconds each way; no direct signal to the far side; the illustrations and the
  composite Earth are declared). N4 suggests naming that the pole flyover is a visualisation made from
  laser data, which is more exact than "footage".
