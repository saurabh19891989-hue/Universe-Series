# Episode 1 — playbook audit (proposals only; the locked script is unchanged)

Writer/story analyst, 28 Nov 2026. Audits `episodes/E01/script-v3.md` (locked) against
`canon/SCENE-PLAYBOOK.md` (engine numbers in brackets). Nothing here changes canon: READ stamp 1.3 s,
reports #1,204–#1,207, 31 labels on the parcel and 32 postings (label one fell off in the spring),
Lucía's eight months, the board at 1,094 until the tag. Every upgrade below keeps all of that.

**Headline.** This is a strong script. Its engines are clean (polite escalation, rule-then-break, one line
beats the list, object that carries it, physics as comedian, the rug-pull of Clause 9). Its weakness is
not story but **staging written for a no-lip-sync photoreal pipeline** (backs of heads, insert-only lines,
soft focus) and **one run of three talk scenes in Act 3**. Animation lets us fix both cheaply.

## The five highest-value upgrades

**1. Draw the Baron's stories (and PIP's one) as cutaway tales** [26]. In 6 (the swamp and the horse,
5 s), 8 (the King of the Moon: the picture shows a palace; the next shot shows the boulder), 13 (the rope cut
and re-tied, the hatchet flying to the Moon, 5 s each) and 21 (the cannonball swap, ~20 s). Draw them in one
engraving-like style of our own, taken from the public-domain 1862 Doré plates, never from any film. They
obey the script's existing thinning rule: each act's cutaways have about 10% less colour, so the audience
*sees* his stories failing. PIP gets one too: in 7, the 1841 man knocking for an hour with his eels, in a
Victorian ledger-print style. No dialogue changes. Cost: B, but each cutaway is short and reusable in E10.

**2. Make Clause 9 (scene 22) visible.** When the Baron is relisted and speaks the approved story, the
contract tidies him on screen: his moustache combs itself into two symmetrical curls, his coat buttons
itself, and the cutaway that starts behind him (the Sultan's bees) turns into a beige shuttle lounge mid-air.
On "Neither", the moustache springs back wild and the cutaway bursts into the beanstalk climbing to the Moon
for his last line. This turns the episode's best idea into its best picture. Dialogue unchanged; colour rule
unchanged (the red seeps back, then drains). Cost: B.

**3. Fix the Act 3 talk run (17, 18–19, 20).** Merge 20 into 17: Lucía's "if you don't sign, I walk" and
"you promise like it's money" are said in the rover, while she drives one-handed and waves the contract; the
date reveal still lands through Mira's loupe on a bump in the road; PIP's "thirty-two times" plays on the
wrist. Keep 18–19, but stage the corridor as a side-on walk along the crater window [19] so it moves. Act 3
then runs loud (rover) → quiet (corridor) → quiet-loud (the rim) → loud (signing). Saves about 50 s. Lucía's
packed bag must still be at her feet in 22 (she packs it off-screen; one insert at the top of 22 does it).

**4. Open on a picture, not on black.** Keep every line of the cold open, but the first frame is the real
Moon in the window and a cheerful bank tow-drone unbolting the left engine, which drifts slowly past the
glass while PIP says it has been taken (polite escalation, first item, seen). The coffee machine's light is
the third item and the button, as written. This meets "motion and a problem in the first 5 seconds" and gives
the trailer its first shot. Cost: A–B.

**5. Translate Chichikov's "never in focus" into a cartoon gag.** Soft focus on a flat cartoon reads as a
render fault. Instead: he is the only character drawn with a fainter, unfinished line and slightly washed
colour, he never casts a shadow, and when anyone looks straight at him the camera's framing drifts off him by
itself. PIP's log for him (5) is an insert of a blank grey silhouette labelled MAN, AVERAGE. In the tag, his
line and colour snap in, full strength, for the first time — the jolt the script wants. Cost: A.

(Also, across the script: drop the "mouth hidden" staging where a lead's big line is the point — Jax's
"Neither", Mira's "Then the map is wrong, sir", Lucía's "Good" in 22 — and let the puppets act them. Keep the
insert grammar for objects.)

## Showcase pick: scene 21 — "Outer rim: the cannonball story" (2:20–2:40 with the cutaway)

**Why this one.**
- It is the heart of the episode and of the season (it plants the ep 10 cannonball promise and "you lie like
  a man who once had to lie for his life").
- It shows what animation can do that live action can't: the cannonball swap in mid-air, drawn in the
  Baron's own style, intercut with two small figures on a boulder before the **real** Earth on the lunar
  horizon (SVS 4944 when verified).
- It is cheap where it should be (two seated figures, visors, suit radio — the rig's strengths) and spends
  the budget on one 20 s cutaway (B cost).
- It stands alone with one title card ("An old liar. A man about to sell him.") and holds a 40–50 s SHORT
  inside it (the cannonball swap to "You are the audience").
- Its risk is the opposite of the episode's other candidates: it tests whether we can make a quiet scene
  gripping. If it works, the format works.

**Runner-up:** 23 + 24 + 26 as one chase sequence (about 1:50): lowest risk for the rig (rigid vehicles,
four heads in a cab, side-on), the best stand-alone SHORT, but less proof that the story carries.
