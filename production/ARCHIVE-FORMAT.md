# Archive format — "PIP's file on the case" (v1, 28 Nov)

A free, in-house way to make every second move, built and tested on the first 4:52 of Episode 1
(cold open, main title, scenes 1–5). Sample files: `samples/e01/E01-archive-sample-*`.
Shot list: `episodes/E01/archive-sample-shotlist.json` (generated by `scratchpad/arch/build.py`).
Legal record of the footage: `assets/archive-vault.json`.

## The idea
Each episode is PIP's case file. PIP (the ship's brass postal-audit computer) has reconstructed events
from ship cameras, real space feeds and "misfiled" Earth archives. The archive film is part of the joke:
it is cut against the *subtext* of the line, and PIP labels each clip in his dry filing voice.

## Rules as built
1. **Full-screen picture always moves.** Five sources, in this sample:

   | source | seconds | shots |
   |---|---|---|
   | Archive film inside PIP's Archive Viewer | 180.9 | 52 (23 films) |
   | Animated screens (bank call, Atlas, reports, readouts, licence, board, clock, cargo cam) | 48.8 | 17 |
   | Ship-camera clips of our own characters (lens, sandwich, pilot, stamp, grin) | 23.4 | 9 |
   | Real NASA footage, full-bleed with a telemetry strip | 18.9 | 5 (+ the Moon keyed into 4 cockpit windows) |
   | Main title (10 s), sources slate (6 s), calling-card insert rendered in code (3.8 s) | 19.8 | 3 |

2. **Archive Viewer.** 4:3 film plays in a brass-and-cream console (one generated bezel picture,
   `scratchpad/arch/bezel_agy1.jpg`, made with the Antigravity CLI from PIP's master; its black screen is keyed).
   The two dials have live needles, a frame counter runs, the screen has scan lines, gate weave, flicker and a slow
   push. One warm look for all film; black-and-white stays black-and-white (auto-levels, faint paper tone);
   colour film is warmed and saturated. Film is shown at about 1.6× (640×480 → 1000×770), never 3×.
3. **Exhibit slate** on the console's panel: `EXHIBIT n`, a literal title, a place/year line, sometimes a red
   rubber stamp (`REPOSSESSED`, `NOTED`, `FILED`, `SEIZED`, `AVERAGE`). Typed on at each cut. Rules for writers:
   literal, bureaucratic, never winking; ≤ 6 words in the title; the sub line carries the joke's second half.
   Consecutive cuts on one joke keep the number and say `(CONT.)`.
4. **Comm cards.** Whoever speaks gets a small brass card (bottom right; bottom left on ship-cam clips so the
   clip's corner mark stays visible; on the console panel in viewer shots): face cropped from the approved master
   with a slow push, flicker and rolling scan line; name and role in PIP's terms ("MAN, AVERAGE · FACE NOT
   RETAINED", "UNIDENTIFIED · TRICORN HAT"); a waveform from the **real amplitude of the line's stem** and a TX lamp.
   Two cards stack for an exchange. PIP's card is his lens, glowing with his voice.
5. **Subtitles** burned in small at bottom centre (one line at a time); the `.srt` is kept beside the render.
6. **Sound**: final dialogue stems from the conform sheet (radio/wrist treatments as made; customs-hall reverb on
   hall lines), our score cues and effects, ambience under every scene. All archive and NASA audio is discarded.
   Measured only — nobody here can hear it.
7. **Sources slate**, 6 s, at the end.
8. Ship-camera clips: never cropped (camera stays at zoom 1), corner star mark never covered, no clip with a
   talking mouth under someone's line. The customs-counter clip was not used: it carries a mosaic over Jax's mouth.

## How footage was found, checked and logged
- **Search**: the Hugging Face list `davanstrien/prelinger-archives-open` (1,903 open-licence Prelinger films; its
  `metadata.jsonl` searched by keyword), archive.org `advancedsearch.php` on `collection:prelinger AND licenseurl:*`,
  and the Library of Congress National Screening Room JSON API (dates 1890–1930). NASA footage was already logged
  in `assets/e01-plates.json`.
- **Rights check** per item, from its own metadata: Prelinger `licenseurl` = public domain; LOC films published in
  the US 1903–1906 (the record's `created_published`) plus the collection rights statement. No NC/SA items.
- **Vault**: 37 items checked, 36 downloaded (2.7 GB) to `scratchpad/vault/dl`; 23 used. For Prelinger shots the
  exact range was then read from the MPEG-2 original with `ffmpeg -ss/-t` (the 640×480 h.264 derivative is too
  blocky for close-ups), deinterlaced to 24 fps. Every range, URL and sha256 is in `assets/archive-vault.json`.
- **Rejected for taste**: *Immigration* (1946: Ellis Island inspections of real people), *Salvaged Skills* (wounded
  veterans framed as "salvage"), *Financing the American Family* (corporate logo); IBM and airline-logo shots skipped
  inside *On Guard!* and *Plane Talk*; Market Street 1906 used only as "THE PAST", not for the "still running" joke.
- **Shot picking**: `scratchpad/arch/tools/sheet.py` scene-detects each film (ffmpeg scene filter), writes a labelled
  contact sheet with a motion score per shot; shots were picked by reading the sheets (title cards, static and
  damaged shots rejected).

## Cost of this sample (4:52 of finished video)
| step | wall time | CPU |
|---|---|---|
| Search + metadata + downloads (36 films, 2.7 GB) | ~25 min (network) | small |
| Contact sheets (scene detection on 36 films) | ~35 min on 3 workers | ~70 CPU-min |
| Choosing shots by reading sheets, writing slates, timeline (`build.py`) | ~2 h agent working time | — |
| Range fetch of 52 shots from MPEG-2 originals | 6.3 min | ~1 CPU-min |
| Final render, 1080p (86 shots, audio mix included) | 26.4 min on 3 workers | 88 CPU-min (18.2 CPU-s per video-s; 11.9 of it Python) |
| Review copies 720p/480p (2-pass) | ~6 min | ~12 CPU-min |

Per finished minute: roughly 40 min of working time (first time; most of it choosing shots) and ~18 CPU-min of
render, ~5.4 min of render wall time on 3 workers. Mix: −16.1 LUFS integrated, true peak −1.8 dBTP (1080p master);
review copies −16.3 LUFS, −2.7 / −1.7 dBTP after a limiter on the AAC re-encode.

## What 35 minutes would need
- About 380–420 archive cuts (this sample: 52 in 4:52, ~11 per minute) from ~120–160 films; with re-use of good
  films, a vault of ~150 films ≈ 12–15 GB of derivatives (or far less if only ranges are fetched).
- Choosing shots by eye from contact sheets took most of the working time. An automatic **search index is worth
  building** before episode 2: run `sheet.py` over the whole open Prelinger set (1,903 films; scene list + one
  thumbnail per shot + motion score) once, add a text tag per shot from a local image-captioning model, and keep it
  as a searchable table. Then the writer's "irony brief" per line becomes a query, and a human (or agent) only
  confirms 2–3 candidates.
- Render: ~18 CPU-seconds per second of video at 1080p on this machine → ~10.6 CPU-hours per 35-min episode,
  ~3.1 h wall on 3 workers (the overlays are cached per shot, so re-cuts only re-render changed shots).
- Working time: with the index, ~1.5–2 h per episode for picking and slate writing (one Opus builder), plus a critic
  pass on the slates for taste and rights.

## Honest weaknesses
- It is a **format**, not a film: about 60 % of the screen time is old film in a box. It is lively and funny when the
  cut lands, but it never shows our characters acting a scene; the leads are mostly small cards.
- Mostly black-and-white archive; the colour films (1948–1965) are faded. The "bright and colourful" wish is
  only partly met (NASA, ship cams, screens, 3–4 colour films).
- The comm-card portraits are stills with a push and flicker; small, but still stills.
- Cuts every 2–4 s on archive can feel restless over 35 minutes; some exhibits are long sentences.
- Some jokes need the viewer to read the slate quickly; a few slates are only on screen 1.5–2 s.
- The cold-open score cue was composed to the old 75.9 s cold open and is trimmed with a fade (unheard).
- Ship-camera clips are 8 s each; only five usable, so they repeat (lens three times, pilot twice).
- The calling-card insert's wood background is obviously procedural.
- The *Plane Talk* colour has faded to pink; it was left as found.
- The sources slate is silent for ~4 s at the end (no end sting placed).
- Nobody has listened to the mix.
