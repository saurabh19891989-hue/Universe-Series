# Look-and-sound test brief (shared by all bake-off agents)

Working show: **SECRET UNIVERSE** — a fast, funny, dramatic space adventure for YouTube.
30–40 minute episodes. The real universe (NASA/ESA footage) is the set; the story is fiction.
Premise in one line: Earth's legends were never invented, they were *overheard* — the heroes of
every culture's stories are real, alive, and living out in the real universe, and somebody is
deleting them from the map one port at a time. A broke courier crew gets caught in the middle.

Tone references (for feel only, never copied): Guardians of the Galaxy (found family, music),
Goodfellas / The Departed (speed, voiceover, betrayal), Yes Minister / Blackadder (dry wit).

## Characters used in the test

| Character | Voice | Look |
|---|---|---|
| **Jax Vale** — captain, narrator | Male, late 30s, American, wry, quick, a salesman's warmth; narration is confiding and fast | Lean, rumpled burnt-orange courier jacket with too many pockets, stubble, crooked grin, messy dark hair, brown skin |
| **Dr. Mira Sen** — cartographer | Female, 30s, light Indian-English accent, dry, precise, low-key | Bengali, dark braid, indigo/teal flight suit, brass cartographer's loupe on a chain, ink-stained fingers |
| **PIP** — ship's computer | British RP, clipped, officious civil servant; never raises voice | Small floating brass-and-cream unit, like a postal scale crossed with a desk lamp; one amber lens; a rubber-stamp arm |
| **Baron Munchausen** — guest legend | Male, 70s, theatrical, bombastic, slight German colour | Enormous white moustache, tricorn hat, moth-eaten 18th-century cavalry coat over a patched pressure suit |

Visual style target: graphic-novel realism, bold readable silhouettes, cinematic rim light,
limited palette (deep-space navy, burnt orange, teal, cream). 16:9, 1920x1080. Characters are
composited over real space footage. No NASA logos or insignia on anything fictional.

## Test scene (original; ~70 seconds)

Setting: bridge of the courier ship *The Unscheduled*, on approach to Shackleton crater, lunar south pole.

JAX (V.O.): Here's the thing nobody tells you about the Moon. It is a terrible place to be broke. I'd been broke on nine worlds, and the Moon was the only one that charged me for the view.

PIP: Captain. The destination no longer exists.

JAX: It's a crater, Pip. Craters don't leave.

PIP: The crater is present. The address has been withdrawn. I am now legally required to deliver this parcel to nowhere, and we are eleven minutes late.

MIRA: That's not a glitch. Somebody deleted it. Somebody deleted a place.

BARON: Deleted? Madam, I have been shot from a cannon, swallowed by a fish, and married on the Moon — twice — and I have never once been deleted. I find it extremely rude.

JAX: Who is this?

PIP: According to the registry: nobody. According to him: Baron Munchausen.

MIRA: (quiet) Jax. If they can erase a place... they can erase the people in it.

JAX (V.O.): And that was the moment. Right there. I could have handed over the parcel, taken the money, and been a happy, shallow man for the rest of my life. Instead I did the stupidest thing I have ever done. I asked a follow-up question.

## Rules for every bake-off agent

- Free tools only in this round. Record the licence of every tool, model, voice, sample and
  asset, and say plainly whether it permits use in a monetised YouTube video.
- Put deliverables under the repo folder you are given. Keep each file under 5 MB (MP3/JPG/MP4,
  not WAV/PNG where possible) and the folder under 60 MB.
- Put model weights, virtualenvs, caches and scratch files in your scratchpad folder, never in the repo.
- Do not run any git commands. Do not publish or upload anything anywhere.
- The machine has 4 CPU cores, 15 GB RAM, no GPU, and is shared with other agents right now.
- Report what you measured and what you could not check. Never describe a sample as good if
  you have not inspected it; agents cannot hear audio, so say so.
