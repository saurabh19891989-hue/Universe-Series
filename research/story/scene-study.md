# Scene study — what the best and most-shared scenes actually do

Writer/story analyst, 28 Nov 2026. For the showrunner. Feeds `canon/SCENE-PLAYBOOK.md`.

**Purpose.** We are a 2D animated comedy-drama (jointed puppets over real NASA video). The picture
will sit a notch below live action, so the writing has to be stronger than average. This file studies
53 entries (about 45 scenes plus channels and methods) from many film cultures, animation, viral clips
and YouTube-native channels, and records
**technique only**: what happens in one line, the mechanism, how it is shot and paced, its length,
and what made it quotable or clippable.

**Rules for using this file.** Nothing here is to be copied: no plot, character, design, line or music.
Dialogue is quoted only as a few words where needed to name a scene. We take the engine, never the car.

**How claims are marked.**
- **[V]** = the "top ranked / went viral / famous" claim (or the fact named) was checked by web
  search on 28 Nov 2026; a source is given.
- **[M]** = from memory, not checked this session. Treat as likely but unverified.
- Running times are approximate unless marked [V]; scene descriptions are from memory of the work.

## A. Classic cinema and TV (drama, comedy, romance, suspense, action)

### 1. Casablanca (US, 1942) — the duel of anthems
- **Happens:** occupying officers sing their song in a café; the resistance leader has the band play the
  other side's anthem, and the whole room drowns them out.
- **Mechanism:** music as a weapon; a crowd chooses a side without a speech; the minor character who
  weeps while singing carries the emotion. Status reversal by volume.
- **Shot/pace:** ~2 min. Starts with one table, builds by cutting to more and more faces joining;
  ends on a single close-up of tears and a defiant shout.
- **Clippable because:** no context needed — two songs, two sides, one wins. [M]

### 2. The Godfather (US, 1972) — the baptism
- **Happens:** a christening in church is cross-cut with a series of killings ordered by the godfather.
- **Mechanism:** music and ritual against the image; dramatic irony (we know what "I renounce" means
  here); the vow spoken over the crimes.
- **Shot/pace:** ~5 min; the organ never stops; the cuts get shorter as the vows go on.
- **Clippable because:** the contrast is the whole point and reads in seconds. [M]

### 3. Goodfellas (US, 1990) — "funny how?"
- **Happens:** a man tells a joke at a table, a friend calls him funny, and he turns it into a threat
  until the table is terrified — then reveals he was joking.
- **Mechanism:** status flip inside a two-hander; a tiny word repeated until it becomes dangerous;
  a rug-pull release that is itself a threat.
- **Shot/pace:** ~2 min; tight, mostly singles; the laughter of the table stops on a cut.
- **Clippable because:** escalation from nothing to life-or-death in under a minute. [M]

### 4. Goodfellas — the long walk through the club kitchen
- **Happens:** one unbroken shot follows a couple in through the back door, the kitchen, to a table
  set in front of the stage.
- **Mechanism:** a world shown by movement, not dialogue; power shown by doors that open.
- **Shot/pace:** ~3 min single take with a pop song. [M]
- **For us:** the bible names Goodfellas as the model for "the climb". Animation can do "one move"
  through a set cheaply with a camera pan across a long painted plate.

### 5. Jaws (US, 1975) — the Indianapolis speech
- **Happens:** three men drunk in a boat cabin compare scars; then one quietly tells a true war story
  about men in the water and sharks.
- **Mechanism:** the story inside the story; comedy (scar contest) turned to dread; a long monologue
  played almost without cuts; the quiet before the attack.
- **Shot/pace:** ~4 min; laugh, laugh, then silence and a near-static close shot. [M]

### 6. North by Northwest (US, 1959) — the crop-duster
- **Happens:** a man waits at an empty country crossroads in bright sun; a plane attacks him.
- **Mechanism:** menace in open, bright, empty space (the opposite of the dark alley); long waits with
  nothing happening build dread; geography made very clear first.
- **Shot/pace:** ~7 min; minutes of waiting before any action. [M]
- **For us:** our lunar plates are bright, empty, silent landscapes. This is the model for daylight suspense.

### 7. Hitchcock's "bomb under the table" (method, not a scene)
- **Mechanism:** show the audience the bomb, then let characters chat — surprise gives seconds of shock,
  suspense gives minutes of tension. [M] (widely quoted from Hitchcock's interviews with Truffaut).

### 8. When Harry Met Sally (US, 1989) — the deli
- **Happens:** in a busy diner a woman proves a point to her friend in the loudest possible way; a
  stranger at the next table delivers the topper.
- **Mechanism:** a private argument made public; the button line given to a bystander, not a lead.
- **Shot/pace:** ~2½ min; two-shot, then the reaction of the room, then the stranger. [M]

### 9. Fawlty Towers (UK, 1975) — "The Germans"
- **Happens:** a hotelier concussed and told not to mention the war mentions it constantly to guests.
- **Mechanism:** one rule set up ("don't mention it"), then broken in escalating ways; the character is
  desperate not to do the thing and the desperation makes him do it.
- **Shot/pace:** set-bound, long takes, physical comedy (the goose-step) as the peak.
- **Rank:** Fawlty Towers was No. 1 in the BFI TV 100 industry poll (2000). [V]
  [BFI TV 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BFI_TV_100)

### 10. Only Fools and Horses (UK, 1982 and 1989) — the chandelier and the bar fall
- **Happens:** (a) two brothers carefully plan to lower a chandelier and catch it; the cut reveals they
  have released the other one. (b) A man posing at a bar leans on a flap that has just been lifted.
- **Mechanism:** pride and preparation, then a single physical collapse; the audience sees the setup
  (the open flap, the wrong chandelier) a beat before the victim — dramatic irony in two seconds.
- **Shot/pace:** each payoff is one wide shot plus a reaction; both under 2 min of build.
- **Rank:** the bar fall topped several fan polls (Gold 2015 and 2022; RadioTimes 2021); the chandelier
  came second in the 2015 and 2021 polls. [V]
  [STV](https://news.stv.tv/entertainment/del-boy-falling-through-bar-voted-top-only-fools-and-horses-moment),
  [Radio X](https://radiox.co.uk/news/tv-film/only-fools-horses-best-scene-bar-poll)

### 11. Monty Python (UK, 1969) — the dead parrot
- **Happens:** a customer returns a dead bird; the shopkeeper denies it is dead.
- **Mechanism:** a denial loop: one side keeps escalating the evidence, the other keeps escalating the
  excuses; a two-hander on one set; the absurd is played with total seriousness. [M]

### 12. Blackadder Goes Forth (UK, 1989) — the final scene
- **Happens:** after a whole series of jokes, the characters go over the top in slow motion; the image
  fades to an empty battlefield, then to a poppy field with birdsong.
- **Mechanism:** a laugh-machine show stops laughing; the slowed piano version of its own theme; we are
  not shown the deaths. The image replaces the punchline.
- **Shot/pace:** ~1½ min; slow motion, no dialogue at the end. The ending was rebuilt late because the
  scripted version looked wrong on set. [V] [Goodbyeee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodbyeee)
- "Voted greatest" — **not verified**; widely praised. [M]

### 13. Sholay (Hindi, 1975) — the coin
- **Happens:** a friend settles every choice by tossing a coin; when he dies the other finds it has two heads.
- **Mechanism:** a small object as a running device for the whole film, then a reveal that rewrites every
  earlier toss as a choice of love. Plant, forget, pay off when it hurts.
- **Shot/pace:** the reveal is an insert on the coin in a hand, then a reaction. [V]
  [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/sholay50-popular-dialogues-that-made-its-characters-immortal)
- Also from Sholay: the villain's "how many men were there?" scene — a three-word question that became
  a national catchphrase; humiliation of henchmen as menace. [V] (same source)

### 14. Deewaar (Hindi, 1975) — the two brothers at the bridge
- **Happens:** the rich brother lists what he owns; the poor brother answers with one short line about
  their mother.
- **Mechanism:** a speech that turns on one line; status reversal by values, not money; the short answer
  beats the long list. [M]

### 15. Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (Hindi, 1995) — the train
- **Happens:** a strict father finally lets his daughter go; she runs alongside a moving train to take
  her lover's outstretched hand.
- **Mechanism:** a release given by the blocking figure (the father's permission is the climax, not the
  kiss); the reach across a moving gap; music and running.
- **Rank:** the film has played daily at one Mumbai cinema since 1995; the train climax is copied and
  homaged in many later films. [V]
  [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/19/iconic-bollywood-romance-marks-30-years-of-non-stop-run-at-mumbai-theatre),
  [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/ddlj30-an-iconic-train-sequence-that-hindi-cinema-keeps-revisiting)

### 16. Baasha (Tamil, 1995) — the interval block
- **Happens:** a mild auto-rickshaw driver who takes every insult finally does one thing that shows
  who he used to be, and the film cuts to the interval.
- **Mechanism:** hidden identity revealed by one act, not a speech; the "mass" moment the audience has
  been waiting for; the interval as a structural cliffhanger in Indian cinema. [M]
- **For us:** the Jax secret is this engine at season scale. Do not copy the scene.

### 17. Baahubali: The Beginning (Telugu, 2015) — the closing question
- **Happens:** the film ends with a loyal guardian revealing he killed the hero.
- **Mechanism:** an ending that poses one plain question; the question itself becomes the marketing.
- **Spread:** "Why did Kattappa kill Baahubali?" became a national talking point for two years, with its
  own hashtag; the makers amplified it. [V]
  [Hollywood Reporter India](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/features/interviews/baahubali-the-epic-decoding-the-marketing-coup-that-was-why-did-kattappa-kill-baahubali)
- **For us:** every season (and every tag) should leave one short, sayable question.

### 18. Drishyam (Malayalam, 2013) — the last scene
- **Happens:** an ordinary man signs a register at a new police station and walks out; the audience then
  learns where the body is.
- **Mechanism:** a rug-pull that was planted in plain sight earlier (a construction site seen in passing);
  a cosy family drama surface hides a thriller.
- **Spread:** remade in at least four other Indian languages and abroad. [V]
  [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/drishyam-phenomenon-cross-border-appeal-ordinary-man-s-fight-his-family-171387),
  [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/entertainment/how-drishyam-went-global-from-malayalam-to-remakes-in-7-languages/3062853/)

### 19. Ikiru (Japan, 1952) — the swing
- **Happens:** a dying clerk, who has finally done one good thing, sits on a swing in the snow in the
  park he built, singing softly.
- **Mechanism:** the climax is quiet; told partly after the fact, at his wake, so the swing is the one
  image we are given; a small song, not a score. [M]

### 20. Seven Samurai (Japan, 1954) — the recruiting test
- **Happens:** recruits are tested by a man hiding behind a door with a stick; the best one senses it
  and refuses to walk through.
- **Mechanism:** a test that reveals character in three tries (one fails, one passes, one sees the trick);
  escalation in threes. [M]

### 21. Oldboy (Korea, 2003) — the corridor
- **Happens:** one man fights a corridor full of men with a hammer.
- **Mechanism:** side-on, lateral camera that tracks along the corridor like a scrolling game; the whole
  geography in one frame; exhaustion shown, not cut around.
- **Shot/pace:** one long take (shot over many attempts). [V]
  [Yahoo/Empire](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/iconic-oldboy-hallway-fight-influenced-170000079.html)
- **For us:** lateral staging is the cheapest way for 2D puppets to do action: one plane, walk cycles, no
  depth.

### 22. Parasite (Korea, 2019) — the peach montage and the jingle
- **Happens:** a family cons its way into a rich household step by step; a sister memorises her fake
  history by singing it as a little jingle.
- **Mechanism:** a con montage cut to grand classical-style music (music against the image: grandeur
  for a dirty trick); a diegetic mnemonic tune that is funny because it is so practical.
- **Spread:** the jingle got its own official release and online remixes. [V]
  [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/parasite) — scale of spread not verified.

### 23. Infernal Affairs (Hong Kong, 2002) — the rooftop
- **Happens:** two moles — a cop inside a gang and a gangster inside the police — finally meet face to face.
- **Mechanism:** both know; the scene is about what each will admit; calm wide frame of the city against
  a gun held still. The source of the US remake. [V]
  [Wikipedia: The Departed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Departed)
- **For us:** the bible's "two moles" engine. The rooftop meeting is the shape of a late-season scene.

### 24. Jackie Chan action comedy (Hong Kong, 1980s–90s)
- **Mechanism (from Tony Zhou's analysis):** start the hero at a disadvantage; use the environment as the
  weapon; keep every shot clear; the action *is* the comedy. [V]
  [No Film School summary](https://nofilmschool.com/2014/12/jackie-chans-9-principles-action-comedy)

### 25. In the Mood for Love (Hong Kong, 2000) — the noodle-stall walks
- **Happens:** two neighbours keep passing each other on the same stairs, with the same waltz in slow motion.
- **Mechanism:** repetition with variation: the same place, the same music, a different dress, a closer
  pass; restraint makes the romance. [M]

### 26. The Lives of Others (Germany, 2006) — the last line
- **Happens:** years later a writer's new book is dedicated to a codename; a former spy buys it, and the
  shop assistant asks if it is a gift.
- **Mechanism:** the whole film pays off on a short line about who the book is for; the man who was
  invisible is finally named. [M]

### 27. Cinema Paradiso (Italy, 1988) — the kiss reel
- **Happens:** a man watches a reel his dead friend left him: every kiss the old censor cut.
- **Mechanism:** an object carrying a lifetime; a long montage with no dialogue; the gift explains the
  relationship after the person is gone. [M]

### 28. City of God (Brazil, 2002) — the chicken chase opening
- **Happens:** a gang chases an escaped chicken through the streets; the narrator ends up between the gang
  and the police; freeze frame; jump back years.
- **Mechanism:** open in mid-chaos with a comic object; freeze-frame on the worst moment; narrator
  rewinds ("to understand this, go back"). Colour shift marks the time jump. [V]
  [Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@MattyJay/film-form-analysis-city-of-god-opening-sequence-3c675ced1c7)

### 29. Pan's Labyrinth (Mexico/Spain, 2006) — the Pale Man
- **Happens:** a girl is told to eat nothing at a monster's feast; she eats two grapes; the monster wakes.
- **Mechanism:** a rule stated plainly, then broken by the character we love; the audience screams
  "don't" — suspense from our knowledge of the rule. [V]
  [JustWatch](https://guides.justwatch.com/us/pans-labyrinth-pale-man-eat-fairies-explained)

### 30. Wild Tales (Argentina, 2014) — the plane prologue
- **Happens:** passengers chatting on a flight discover, one by one, that they all know the same man.
- **Mechanism:** a cold open built as a chain of small coincidences that turns into a rug-pull; each
  reveal goes faster than the last; ends before the title. [M]

## B. Animation — TV, family, anime

### 31. Up (Pixar, 2009) — the marriage montage
- **Happens:** a couple's whole life, from wedding to death, without a word.
- **Mechanism:** wordless montage with one musical theme varied; small repeated objects (a jar of savings
  broken again and again for life's emergencies) measure time; the dream deferred.
- **Length:** about 4 minutes, wordless; the score's "Married Life" won a Grammy. [V]
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_opening_sequence)

### 32. The Simpsons (US, 1990) — Homer falls into the gorge
- **Happens:** a man tries to jump a gorge, falls, is rescued, and the rescue fails so he falls again.
- **Mechanism:** pain without injury (animation physics); the rescue itself becomes the second fall;
  escalation by repetition.
- **Rank:** one of the most-used clips of the show; the creator's favourite moment. [V]
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_the_Daredevil)

### 33. The Simpsons (US, 1996) — "Steamed Hams"
- **Happens:** a man hosting his boss covers one small cooking disaster with a lie, then a bigger lie,
  until the house is on fire.
- **Mechanism:** the lie that grows; a two-hander where one man keeps raising the stakes to avoid one
  small admission; each excuse is more specific and worse.
- **Spread:** a 1990s segment that became a huge remix meme on YouTube from 2015–2018. [V]
  [The Daily Dot](https://www.dailydot.com/unclick/steamed-hams-simpsons-meme/)

### 34. Futurama (US, 2002) — the dog that waits
- **Happens:** after an episode of comedy, the final montage shows a dog waiting for his owner outside a
  shop for years, growing old.
- **Mechanism:** a gut-punch tag in a comedy; silent montage with a song; the audience knows what the
  character never will (dramatic irony as grief).
- **Rank:** often ranked the show's top episode (fan vote 2013; Variety 2022). [V]
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_Bark)
- **For us:** we have a stray dog from ep 3 onward. Handle with care: do not copy the waiting image.

### 35. Avatar: The Last Airbender (US, 2006) — the old man's song
- **Happens:** an uncle who is always cheerful goes alone to a hilltop tree on his dead son's birthday and sings.
- **Mechanism:** the private moment overheard by the audience only; the comic support character shows a
  grief we never knew; a short sung tune, not orchestra.
- **Rank:** the episode is among the show's most acclaimed. [V]
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tales_of_Ba_Sing_Se)

### 36. Wallace & Gromit: The Wrong Trousers (UK, 1993) — the train chase
- **Happens:** a dog rides a toy train in a chase and, when the track runs out, lays new track in front of
  himself at full speed.
- **Mechanism:** "laying the track ahead": the solution is improvised as fast as the problem arrives; tiny
  scale played as epic; clear geography.
- **Rank:** audiences applauded at the premiere; often called one of the best chase scenes on film. [V]
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wrong_Trousers),
  [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/wallace-and-gromit-train-scene)

### 37. Road Runner cartoons (US, Chuck Jones) — the rules
- **Mechanism:** a short list of fixed rules (the victim is always the cause of his own trouble; gravity is
  his greatest enemy; he is humiliated more than harmed). Rules make gags fair and endless. [V]
  [Open Culture](https://www.openculture.com/2015/03/chuck-jones-9-rules-for-drawing-road-runner-cartoons.html)
- **For us:** gravity is one-sixth on the Moon. That is our "gravity is the enemy" with a twist: hang time.

### 38. Spirited Away (Japan, 2001) — the train
- **Happens:** a girl and silent companions ride a train across a flooded plain; nothing happens.
- **Mechanism:** *ma* — Miyazaki's term for the deliberate empty interval between actions; the film rests
  so the next storm lands. Miyazaki explained *ma* to Roger Ebert by clapping. [V]
  [RogerEbert.com](https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/hayao-miyazaki-interview)

### 39. One Piece (Japan, 2007) — the ship's funeral
- **Happens:** a pirate crew burns the wrecked ship that carried them; the ship speaks to them once.
- **Mechanism:** an object (a vehicle) as a member of the family; the farewell is earned by years of use;
  the object speaks only once, at the end.
- **Rank:** fans rank it among the saddest moments of the series. [V]
  [ComicBook](https://comicbook.com/anime/news/one-piece-going-merry-farewell-episode)
- **For us:** PIP and *The Unscheduled*. Our ship already has a voice; save its most tender line.

### 40. Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse (US, 2018) — the leap
- **Happens:** a boy finally jumps from a skyscraper as a hero.
- **Mechanism:** the image flipped upside-down so the fall reads as rising — a picture only animation
  makes cheaply; the song's first beat lands on the glass breaking.
- **Rank:** often called the film's high point. [V]
  [/Film](https://www.slashfilm.com/1305066/leap-faith-spider-man-into-spider-verse-best-action-scene-ever)

### 41. Bluey (Australia, 2020) — "Sleepytime"
- **Happens:** a child's night of dreams across the planets, set almost entirely to a classical piece.
- **Mechanism:** a music-led, nearly wordless episode; the parent's care shown by small repeated acts.
- **Rank:** IMDb top-rated episode list (9.8); named among the best TV episodes of 2020; ~8 minutes long. [V]
  [Upworthy](https://www.upworthy.com/grown-ups-are-calling-this-bluey-episode-the-best-episode-of-television-ever-made),
  [Classic FM](https://classicfm.com/discover-music/periods-genres/film-tv/bluey-soundtrack-classical-music)

## C. Clips that went viral online (short-video era)

### 42. "Charlie Bit My Finger" (UK, 2007)
- **What the clip contains:** 56 seconds; a baby bites his brother's finger; the brother's pained, polite
  complaint; the baby laughs.
- **Why shared:** a tiny status reversal (the smaller one wins); a real reaction; one quotable phrase;
  no setup needed. Was YouTube's most viewed video at the time. [V]
  [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2021/05/21/999360273/viral-charlie-bit-my-finger-video-to-leave-youtube-sell-as-nft)

### 43. "BBC Dad" (South Korea/UK, 2017)
- **What the clip contains:** a professor on live TV; his small daughter dances in; a baby in a walker
  follows; his wife slides in to drag them out. ~45 s.
- **Why shared:** intrusion into dignity; escalation in exactly three entries, each funnier; the man's
  attempt to keep a straight face. The BBC's most-watched YouTube video. [V]
  [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/bbc-dad-breaks-silence-on-interview-that-went-viral)

### 44. Key & Peele, "Substitute Teacher" (US, 2012)
- **What the clip contains:** a substitute reads the register and mispronounces every ordinary name with
  total confidence, and is offended by the corrections.
- **Why shared:** a status figure who is wrong and certain; a list with a variation each time; the third
  and fourth names top the first. About 230 million YouTube views (Wikipedia, 2026). [V]
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_Teacher_(Key_%26_Peele))

### 45. Schitt's Creek, "fold in the cheese" (Canada, 2016)
- **What the clip contains:** a mother and son cannot work out one step in a recipe and blame each other.
- **Why shared:** an instruction loop: neither will admit not knowing; cheap two-hander on one set; the
  audio became a TikTok sound people act out. [V]
  [Distractify](https://www.distractify.com/p/fold-in-the-cheese-tiktok)

### 46. Reaction-face culture: Vadivelu (Tamil) and Baburao in Hera Pheri (Hindi)
- **What the clips contain:** a boastful or confused comic man, beaten, cheated or baffled; the frozen face.
- **Why shared:** the reaction face becomes a reusable template for any situation. A Vadivelu character
  ("Pray for Nesamani") was among India's top trending hashtags in 2019; Baburao's reactions and catchphrases
  are long-running meme templates. [V]
  [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/memes-new-stressbuster-in-times-of-covid-19-819354.html),
  [Know Your Meme: Hera Pheri](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/hera-pheri-2000-film)
- **For us:** design a few signature reaction faces (PIP's lens, the Baron's moustache, Lucía's folded arms,
  Mira's loupe eye) as deliberate "sticker" moments. They spread the channel without the full episode.

### 47. Mr Bean (UK) — physical comedy without words
- **What the clips contain:** one man, one small problem (an exam, a turkey, a church service), solved
  badly and silently.
- **Why shared:** no language needed, so it travels to every country; each clip is a complete 2–5 minute
  problem-escalation-button. The main channel passed 35 million subscribers in 2025. [V]
  [Banijay](https://www.banijay.com/blog/2025/08/13/mr-bean-reaches-35-million-youtube-followers-in-35th-anniversary-year/)

## D. YouTube-native animation and story channels

### 48. The Amazing Digital Circus (Glitch Productions, pilot 2023)
- **What it shows:** an indie animated pilot of roughly 26–30 minutes reached ~100 million views in its
  first month and 350 million+ later; a cold open that drops a new character into a strange world with no
  explanation, and a fast run of distinct, loud personalities. [V] (views, length)
  [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/the-amazing-digital-circus),
  [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/amazing-digital-circus/)
- **Caution:** made by a full studio with an existing audience (SMG4/Glitch), fully animated. [M for the
  production details]

### 49. Hazbin Hotel (Vivziepop, pilot 2019)
- **What it shows:** a ~30–32 minute Patreon-funded indie pilot passed 100 million YouTube views (Feb 2024);
  musical numbers are its most-shared clips. [V] (views, length)
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazbin_Hotel)

### 50. Smiling Friends (Adult Swim, 2020–) — the 11-minute unit
- **What it shows:** an adult animated comedy built in ~11-minute episodes, each a single problem with a
  strong button. Started as an unannounced pilot. [V] (length, approximately)
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiling_Friends)

### 51. Storytime animation channels (e.g. TheOdd1sOut, Jaiden Animations) [M]
- **What they do:** narrator-led, simple drawn characters; a joke or visual change every few seconds;
  cutaway gags illustrate the narrator's exaggerations; videos typically 8–20 minutes.
- **Lesson:** cheap animation holds viewers when the picture changes often and the narration lies or
  exaggerates against it.

### 52. Explainer animation (e.g. Kurzgesagt) [M]
- **What they do:** open on a question or a startling claim in the first seconds; a new image every few
  seconds; one clear idea per section; a calm narrator.

### 53. Retention practice on YouTube (method, not a scene)

## E. What the 53 entries have in common (the patterns the playbook is built from)

1. **One plain engine per scene.** Almost every famous scene runs one device you can name in four words
   (rule then broken; lie that grows; three intruders; one line answers a list).
2. **The audience knows first.** Suspense (bomb under the table, the Pale Man rule, the bar flap) beats
   surprise. Our canon already does this (Seo the rat from ep 2).
3. **Threes and lists.** BBC Dad, Substitute Teacher, Seven Samurai, Fawlty Towers: three or more
   variations, each worse, and the last breaks the pattern.
4. **Status flips.** Goodfellas, Charlie, Deewaar, Infernal Affairs: the scene is "who is on top", and it changes.
5. **Small object, big feeling.** Sholay's coin, the Up jar, the Paradiso reel, the ship's funeral.
6. **Silence and music do the crying.** Blackadder, Up, Futurama, Ikiru, Spirited Away: the emotional peak
   usually has *fewer* words, not more.
7. **The most-shared clips need no context.** Every viral entry (42–47) is understood in its first three
   seconds. A clip is a premise, an escalation and a button.
8. **Animation wins where the camera can't go.** The gorge, the track-laying, the upside-down leap, the
   storyteller's exaggerations drawn as cutaways. We should spend our animation budget there, not on
   two people sitting and talking.
9. **Length is not what kills a YouTube video; the first 30–60 seconds are.** Indie animated pilots of
   26–32 minutes have reached 100–350 million views (48, 49); 8- and 11-minute units dominate TV animation
   (41, 50). The deciding factor in all the guidance is the opening and the cadence of new hooks.
