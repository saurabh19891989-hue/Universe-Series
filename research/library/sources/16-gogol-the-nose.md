# 16 — Gogol, "The Nose" (Russia)

- **Author / tradition:** Nikolai Gogol (St Petersburg tales).
- **Original language and date:** Russian, 1836.
- **Edition actually read:** *The Mantle and Other Stories*, translated by Claud Field (London: T. Werner Laurie, 1916) — Project Gutenberg #36238.
- **URL opened:** https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/3/6/2/3/36238/36238.txt
- **Rights:** US public domain (1916). Field's death date not verified for UK/EU; paraphrase is safe.
- **What I read:** "The Nose" in full (barber's breakfast; the nose in uniform at the bazaar; the newspaper office; the police return; the ending). "The Mantle" (The Overcoat) not read this session.

## Story mechanisms
1. **The part of you that outranks you.** Major Kovalyov's nose leaves his face and appears in a gold-embroidered uniform and plumed hat with the rank of state councillor — **higher than its owner**. When he confronts it, it says it is itself, and judging by his buttons he must be in a different department.
2. **The authority that will not print your loss.** The newspaper refuses his advertisement: it might be satire, last week a lost "poodle" turned out to mean a cashier; "that is a matter for a doctor."
3. **Travelling on someone else's papers.** The nose is caught boarding a coach for Riga with a passport made out long before in the name of an official.
4. **Returned but not reattached.** A police official (short-sighted: he first took it for a gentleman) brings the nose back wrapped in paper; it will not stay on until, later, it is simply back on the face. No explanation.
5. **The narrator shrugs.** The tale ends by admitting it is incomprehensible, and that such things do happen, rarely.

## Images and objects
- A nose baked into a breakfast loaf.
- A nose in a uniform, half buried in its stiff collar, inspecting the wares in front of a shop at the Grand Bazaar. (In Field's translation the meeting is at a bazaar; the better-known Russian text sets it in Kazan Cathedral, and I understand the shop setting comes from the version passed by the censor — not verified this session.)
- A flat, smooth blank where a face should be, discovered in a small mirror.
- A newspaper clerk offering snuff to a man with no nose.
- A passport issued to a nose.

## Character behaviours (Kovalyov, in our words)
- Vain about rank (he calls himself "Major" though his civil grade is lower), anxious about women's opinion.
- He tries every office in order — police, newspaper, doctor — as if a bureaucracy could return a body part.
- He is more upset about losing status than about losing a nose.
- Quotation (Field): "But I am not talking of a poodle, but my own nose; i.e. almost myself."

## What retellings get wrong
- It is usually read as pure surrealism. In the text it is precise satire about rank: the horror is that his own feature has a better career than he does.

## Sensitivities
- Low. Russian setting; avoid "vodka-and-bears" shorthand.
