# 07 — Arsène Lupin, Gentleman-Burglar (three stories)

- **Author / tradition:** Maurice Leblanc (France), magazine stories 1905–1907, collected 1907.
- **Original language and date:** French, 1905–1907.
- **Edition actually read:** *The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsène Lupin, Gentleman-Burglar*, translated by George Morehead (Chicago, 1910) — Project Gutenberg #6133. (The Teixeira de Mattos translation was not consulted.)
- **URL opened:** https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/6/1/3/6133/6133-0.txt
- **Rights:** US public domain (1910 translation of 1907 book). Leblanc d. 1941: public domain in EU/UK since 2012 and in India. Morehead's dates are not known to me — paraphrase freely, quote sparingly. Per canon: use only the 1905–1907 stories. Note that in this first story Leblanc used Conan Doyle's "Sherlock Holmes" by name; he later renamed him "Herlock Sholmes" after objections.
- **What I read:** I "The Arrest of Arsène Lupin" (complete), II "Arsène Lupin in Prison" (opening letter, the night of the theft, the cell visit; the resolution by search), IX "Sherlock Holmes Arrives Too Late" (the riddle, the theft, the meeting with Holmes, the ending).

## Story mechanisms
1. **The narrator is the thief.** "The Arrest" is told in the first person by a charming passenger, Bernard d'Andrézy, who courts Miss Nelly while everyone hunts for Lupin. At the gangway the detective Ganimard arrests *him*. The story was Lupin's own account to the author. (Perfect for a guest-narrated episode.)
2. **The clue that names everyone.** The wireless message says Lupin is aboard, first class, fair hair, a wound on the right forearm, travelling alone under the name "R——". Suspicion lands on Rozaine; Lupin robs Rozaine and leaves a card accepting his reward.
3. **Identity borrowed from a dead man.** D'Andrézy died in Macedonia three years before; Lupin travels on his papers. (Echo: Jax with no records before seven years ago.)
4. **The wound as proof.** Ganimard simply strikes the right arm; the cry of pain proves the identity. A body mark beats any paperwork. (Odysseus's scar logic — see 15-odyssey.)
5. **The accomplice who chooses.** Lupin slips the loot into the Kodak he hands Nelly. She could betray him; instead she lets the camera fall between ship and pier. Love decides the evidence.
6. **The theft announced by letter, with an expert's postscript.** From prison he writes to Baron Cahorn listing what to pack and ship — and notes that the largest Watteau is a copy (the original burned), and he doubts a chatelaine's authenticity. *The thief is the best appraiser in the room.*
7. **The hired detective is the thief's man.** The Baron hires "Ganimard", who sleeps through the theft; the real Ganimard later realises the man was not him.
8. **The riddle solved because the host told it at dinner.** At Thibermesnil, the host recites the secret-passage riddle in front of the painter Velmont (Lupin), who solves it that night. Holmes deduces exactly that the next day. Lupin returns the goods because Nelly is now a governess in the house and he promised her; then he picks Holmes's pocket and sends the watch back.

## Images and objects
- A ship cut off from land but tied to it by wireless — a floating island under suspicion.
- A Kodak falling into the black water between hull and pier.
- A white rose taken from a deck and kept; later another one left on a box beside a bamboo — forgotten, or left on purpose.
- A burglar's letter on Santé Prison letterhead, with a postscript of art criticism.
- A ruby ring turned palm-inward on a stolen hand.

## Character behaviours (in our words)
- Lupin courts the victim's world; he enjoys the people he robs and is a little in love with the drama.
- He keeps his word to women he respects, and sulks when they do not admire him for it.
- He mocks detectives warmly, then is unnerved by a man who really sees him (Holmes's look feels like "being photographed").
- Quotation (Morehead, "The Arrest"): "What a pity that I am not an honest man!"

## What retellings get wrong
- Holmes in this story is about fifty, clean-shaven, foreign-cut clothes, a satchel and a heavy cane — no deerstalker, no cape. He arrives by train, refusing the car sent for him.
- Lupin is caught in the very first story. His legend begins with an arrest.
- The romance with Nelly is not a conquest: she sees the stolen ring and walks away; he never sees her again in these stories.

## Sensitivities
- Rights: medium confidence per canon; avoid later stories and every screen design (no top hat-and-monocle look from posters, no recent TV series elements).
- Period remarks about "the morbid curiosity peculiar to women" — drop.
