# 12 — Journey to the West (Xiyou ji), chapters 1–7

- **Author / tradition:** Traditionally attributed to Wu Cheng'en (Ming China, 16th c.), from older story cycles about the monk Xuanzang's pilgrimage.
- **Original language and date:** Vernacular Chinese, published c. 1592.
- **Edition actually read:** Timothy Richard, *A Mission to Heaven: a great Chinese epic and allegory*, Shanghai: Christian Literature Society, 1913 — Internet Archive (Cornell copy).
- **URL opened:** https://archive.org/download/cu31924074502034/cu31924074502034_djvu.txt
- **Rights:** Public domain everywhere (Richard d. 1919). Waley's *Monkey* (1942) is copyrighted and was not used. Helen Hayes's 1930 version was not found on the Archive.
- **What I read:** Richard's introduction (on authorship); ch. III (the visit to the underworld and the Register), ch. IV (the Master of the Horse), and the chapter "Imprisoned for 500 years" (the wager on the palm and the Five Elements mountain); the havoc chapters by search.

## Edition warning (fuel for the "Earth told it wrong" joke)
Richard attributes the novel to the Taoist master Qiu Chuji (Changchun), a well-known old misattribution, and translates the Jade Emperor as "God". The Archive catalogue then lists the author as Li Zhichang, Qiu's disciple who wrote a *different* "Journey to the West" (a real travel diary). Three layers of mis-labelling on one book. Use Richard for events; take nothing from his theology.

## Story mechanisms
1. **The Register edited by its subject.** Arrested in a dream by two summoners, the Monkey King beats them, storms the Ten Courts, sits in the judge's chair and demands the Register of Life and Death. He searches every class of creature, finds no monkeys with names, then finds a separate list: "Soul No. 1350", Sun, the stone monkey, 342 years. He takes the brush and strikes out his own name and every named monkey. From then on those monkeys do not grow old. *A listing removed by its owner* — canon's clue for Jax.
2. **It was only a dream — and still true.** He wakes on a bundle of straw, but the deletion holds. Paperwork done in a dream is binding.
3. **The title with no rank.** Invited to heaven by an envoy (in Richard, "the angel of the planet Venus"), he is made Master of the Horse. He works hard; the horses grow fat. At his welcome banquet he asks the rank. "It has no rank" — "then it must be very high." No: it is "the position before getting any rank at all". He overturns the judgment table and leaves.
4. **The gate that does not know you.** At heaven's South Gate the guards stop him; the envoy explains: until you have an official position, nobody knows you and they cannot let you in. (Exactly how the Atlas works.)
5. **The empty title as pacification.** To stop his rebellion heaven grants him "Great Sage, Equal of Heaven" — the text says to let it be "only an empty title".
6. **The bet you cannot lose that you cannot win.** He wagers he can leap out of a palm the size of a lotus leaf. He leaps to the "end of heaven", sees five pillars, writes "The Great Sage, Equal of Heaven, has travelled thus far" and leaves a "stinking mark" at the foot of the first. Back on the palm, he is shown his own writing on the fingers. The hand turns over and becomes a mountain on top of him.
7. **Proof of travel that proves you never left.** The graffiti is the evidence against him.

## Images and objects
- A stone egg on a mountain top that hatches a monkey.
- An iron club kept in his ear, the size of a needle.
- A register with a number for every soul and a brush heavy with ink striking through a whole column.
- A thousand horses grown fat under a lowly stablemaster.
- Five pink pillars of flesh at the edge of the world, with writing on one.

## Character behaviours (in our words)
- Vain, impatient, joyful; asks the rank of every post.
- Brave to the point of comedy: beats up the underworld's clerks and then demands to see their files.
- Honest about his own skills and dishonest about rules.
- Quotation (Richard, ch. III): "There is an end of the Registration. I am no longer under your control."

## What retellings get wrong
- He is a stable-master before he is a rebel; the insult that starts the "havoc" is a job title.
- The deletion from the Register is done to all the named monkeys, not only himself — a collective delisting.
- The "Equal of Heaven" title is an appeasement with no power, not a promotion.

## Sensitivities
- Per canon: Sun Wukong is a hero; avoid every screen and game design.
- The Buddha (Tathagata, "Julai") is a religious figure of a living faith; he must not appear as a character or be the butt of a joke. The palm image can be transposed to Voss or to Jupiter's magnetosphere, never to the Buddha. Guanyin likewise.
- Richard's Christianising ("God", "angel") should not be repeated.
