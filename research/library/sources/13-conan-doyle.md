# 13 — Arthur Conan Doyle: A Study in Scarlet (ch. 1–2), "The Final Problem", "The Speckled Band"

- **Author / tradition:** Arthur Conan Doyle (Britain).
- **Original language and date:** English; *A Study in Scarlet* 1887; "The Adventure of the Speckled Band" 1892 (*The Adventures*); "The Final Problem" 1893 (*The Memoirs*).
- **Editions actually read:** Project Gutenberg #244 (*A Study in Scarlet*), #834 (*The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes*), #1661 (*The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes*).
- **URLs opened:** https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/2/4/244/244-0.txt · https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/8/3/834/834-0.txt · https://gutenberg.pglaf.org/1/6/6/1661/1661-0.txt
- **Rights:** Public domain in the US (all Holmes stories since 2023) and in UK/EU/India (Doyle d. 1930). Per canon: use the books, never screen versions (no deerstalker-and-"elementary" iconography; Doyle never wrote "Elementary, my dear Watson" in these texts).
- **What I read:** *Study* ch. 1–2 in full (the meeting, the "limits" list, the brain-attic, "The Book of Life"); "The Final Problem" (Moriarty's description and visit, the Reichenbach ending, Holmes's note); "The Speckled Band" (the sister's account, the room, the solution and Holmes's admission).

## Story mechanisms
1. **Deliberate forgetting.** Holmes did not know the Earth goes round the Sun, and now that he knows he will "do my best to forget it": the brain is a small attic, and every new fact pushes out an old one. *For our thinning Holmes: he has always curated his own memory; thinning is the attic emptying without his consent.* And he is a detective with "Astronomy — Nil" in a series set among the planets.
2. **The article that predicts the man.** Watson mocks a magazine piece, "The Book of Life", claiming a trained observer can read anyone's trade from nails, sleeve and boots — Holmes wrote it.
3. **The list of limits.** Watson's numbered inventory of Holmes's knowledge (literature nil, chemistry profound, sensational literature immense) — a character introduced as a file.
4. **The method borrowed by the friend.** At Reichenbach, Watson reads the tragedy using Holmes's own method: two lines of footprints going out, none coming back, torn ferns, the alpine-stock left as a marker, a note under the cigarette case. (Canon ep 9: "Mira lends him her method" — here it is the friend who uses the master's method on the master.)
5. **The enemy's diary of inconvenience.** Moriarty opens a notebook: "You crossed my path on the 4th of January ... on the 23rd you incommoded me ... by the middle of February I was seriously inconvenienced." Villainy as a dated grievance file.
6. **The dying words misread.** In "The Speckled Band" the sister's last words, "the band", plus gipsies with spotted handkerchiefs nearby, lead Holmes to an "entirely erroneous conclusion ... from insufficient data". He reconsiders when he notices that the bell-rope is a dummy, the ventilator opens into the next room, and the bed is clamped to the floor.
7. **The locked room is a delivery route.** The room is sealed, but the bed has been fixed in place so that something passing down the dummy rope from the ventilator must reach the sleeper. *The impossible crime is a logistics problem.*

## Images and objects
- A spider at the centre of a web with a thousand threads that he feels every quiver of (Holmes on Moriarty — a phrase that rhymes with Anansi).
- A forehead "domed out in a white curve", a face that oscillates slowly from side to side "in a curiously reptilian fashion".
- An alpine-stock leaning on a rock at the edge of a waterfall; a silver cigarette-case with a note under it.
- A bell-rope attached to nothing; a ventilator between two rooms; a bed bolted to the floor; a saucer of milk; a small dog-lash tied in a loop.
- A steel poker bent by a furious visitor and quietly straightened again by Holmes.

## Character behaviours (in our words)
- Holmes plays the violin carelessly to think; is proud of a narrow, deep expertise; is cool under threat (lays his pistol on the table).
- He admits error openly and fast; his merit is reconsidering.
- He sets up his own death in a letter written as calmly "as though in his study", with filing instructions ("pigeonhole M., blue envelope").
- Quotation (*Study*, ch. 2): "Now that I do know it I shall do my best to forget it."

## What retellings get wrong
- He is ignorant of astronomy and proud of it.
- He is wrong in "The Speckled Band" at first, and says so.
- Moriarty is in exactly one story here and is described as a professor-turned-organiser who "does little himself", not a cackling genius.

## Sensitivities
- "The Speckled Band" uses Roma ("gipsies") as a red herring with prejudice, and an exotic Indian snake (the "swamp adder", not a real species) plus a violent Anglo-Indian villain. Do not reuse these.
- Holmes's drug use (not in these passages) should not be played for laughs.

## Episodes
Ep 9 (main source: the locked room as a delivery route, the misread dying words, the borrowed method, deliberate forgetting vs thinning), ep 7 (spider in the web), ep 10 (the enemy's diary of inconvenience for Voss or Pell).
