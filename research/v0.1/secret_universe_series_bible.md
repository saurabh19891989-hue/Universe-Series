# SECRET UNIVERSE — The Missing Universe
## Series bible and production-agent review brief | v0.1 | 27 November 2026
**Status:** development proposal, not approved production canon. All 150 episode concepts are original draft pitches. Names and title need clearance. The sources below are a researched starting library, not a claim that all texts have been read or every asset downloaded/licensed.

## 1. Mandate and positioning
Create an English-language serialized space adventure-comedy for an international teen/adult audience. Real astronomical objects and scientific data are the stage, not the subject of a documentary. Build original characters and plotlines from cross-cultural narrative traditions; where justified and cleared, introduce literary characters under their historical names. British-style verbal irony and institutional absurdity meet fast-moving criminal/political intrigue. The viewer should return for the characters even if they have no interest in astronomy.

Working logline: A debt-ridden courier, an uncompromising cartographer, an unhelpfully literal ship computer and a fugitive archivist steal nothing on purpose, yet find themselves carrying the only evidence that whole communities have been erased from the official map of existence. Their attempts to get paid turn into a fight over who can own the routes between stars.

Format proposal: 10 seasons × 15 episodes; 8–12 minutes initially, with each installment a satisfying miniature adventure and a consequential serialized change. Animated limited-motion characters composited over archive-derived environments, reusable interior sets, data-based 3D maps and original graphics. Avoid long educational narration, generic stock montages or an expensive fully animated feature-film look. Pilot length and audience are assumptions for reviewer approval. No child-directed positioning assumed.

## 2. World rules and dramatic engine
- Setting: a fictional far future, with invented settlements, species, institutions, ships and routes. A real place never implies that a real civilization or settlement has been found there.
- The Atlas is a fictional navigation and legal registry, not a map of the physical edge of the universe. Erasing a port removes access to trade, citizenship and services; it does not remove the astronomical body. The original Atlas protected vulnerable populations; a captured institution converted that protection into power. This is the core long-range revelation.
- Gate travel is an explicitly fictional technology. Gates connect only installed endpoints; ordinary light and spacecraft obey real physics where relevant. Each crossing consumes a scarce gate allocation and cannot be undone; no travel into the past, teleportation on demand, or instantaneous ungated communications. Distant galaxies are not reached by normal short-duration flights. Log fictional route time, crew elapsed time, local calendar and communications delays. Show remote views where travel is not established. A gate may have been installed long ago, but no claim is made about real wormholes.
- The universe has no single cultural authority. Loka, underworld, heaven and spiritual realms are treated as concepts in religious texts, not assigned modern celestial coordinates. Myth may inspire fiction without proving science or vice versa.
- Every episode changes at least one persistent state: alliance, debt, evidence, access, reputation, relationship or duty. No automatic sitcom reset.
- Episode engine: a job with a clear objective → hidden obligation → rival with different incentives → astronomical constraint → costly choice → comic reversal → material consequence. Vary genres: heist, rescue, courtroom, family drama, chase, negotiation, mystery, satire, survival, folktale and character bottle episode. Do not use identical beat timing mechanically.

## 5. Comedy and pacing rules
Reference works are viewing/analysis references, not footage or script sources. Study Yes Minister / Yes Prime Minister for bureaucratic doublespeak; Blackadder for status reversal and verbal wit; Red Dwarf for confined-space odd-couple conflict; Only Fools and Horses for schemes that implode; Jeeves and Wooster for etiquette and farce; Goodness Gracious Me for cross-cultural misunderstandings without stereotypes. Original jokes only. Goodfellas offers compression via voiceover, ellipsis and consequences; The Godfather offers escalating obligations, loyalty and family power (it is often deliberately slower, so borrow the dramatic architecture rather than its tempo). Reference analysis: https://culture.ciluk.co.uk/understand-britain/comedy/ ; https://www.comedy.co.uk/tv/yes_minister/ ; https://bfidatadigipres.github.io/sight%20and%20sound%20greatest%20films%20of%20all%20time%202022%3Cbr%3E63%3D/2023/02/04/goodfellas/ .

Writing rules: Characters play absurd situations seriously. Humor comes from incompatible incentives, precision, status and subtext. Understatement after disaster; delayed callbacks; jokes must reveal character or change a situation. No laugh track, meme slang, national caricatures, constant quips or recognizable catchphrases. Allow earnestness and silence after costly decisions. Alternate active sequences with short negotiation or relationship beats. Each scene must change information, leverage or relationships. Use voiceover to compress transitions, not explain what visuals already show. Aim for 6–10 scenes and ~1,000–1,500 spoken words in a 10-minute pilot (adjust after voice timing). The first 20 seconds establish a specific problem. The first minute establishes the goal and consequence. End with a resolved immediate conflict and a new durable consequence. Keep recaps under 15 seconds if necessary. No forced cliffhanger in every installment.

## 7. Production workflow and agent responsibilities
1. Story editor reads season arc and canon ledger; selects episode goal, literature mechanic, conflict and irreversible change.
2. Research agent validates astronomical setting and literary source (specific passages), writes source cards and rights/sensitivity findings. Reject untraceable material.
3. Script agent drafts a beat sheet and timed dialogue, with a shot ID and asset status for each scene. Keep plot ahead of exposition.
4. Continuity critic independently tests causal logic, character voice, travel rules, outstanding promises, pacing, science claims and cultural handling. Revise before generating expensive visuals.
5. Asset agent searches official APIs/archives for approved visual items, downloads only needed files, records original URLs, exact credits, license snapshots, checksums and file paths. Cache assets locally and reuse only where geographically and narratively appropriate; respect service limits.
6. Visual agent builds storyboard and animatic; reuse original rigged characters/interiors and data-based background assets. Generate only unique guest/environment assets. Match lighting, scale, perspective, motion, and visual palette. Never present generated images as observations.
7. Audio/editor agent produces original or commercially licensed voices, soundscape and score, captions, language-neutral graphics, timed edit and original thumbnail. No music copied from reference shows. Keep separate dialogue/music/effects stems and subtitle track for later localization.
8. Independent final reviewer checks credits, license evidence, science/fiction labeling, edit rhythm, character consistency, accessibility and platform policy. Human owner signs off before upload; agent may not assume publishing permission.

Suggested project layout: canon/ (cast, factions, timeline, gate rules, promises); research/ (literary cards, science dossiers); assets/ (manifest, rights, source masters, proxies, characters, interiors); episodes/E001/ (brief, beat sheet, script, shotlist, animatic, edit, review, credits); releases/ (approved exports and subtitles). Use version control for text/metadata; store media on local disk. Every episode records input/output canon changes and resolves or schedules its open questions. Avoid generating all 150 scripts before pilot validation.

Production-agent minimum output for each episode: 1-line hook; prior-state references; verified literary inspiration; verified astronomical dossier; 6–10 scene beats; script with duration; shot plan with asset IDs, credits and classification; asset acquisition manifest; continuity diff; rights/cultural signoff; rendered animatic and edit; thumbnail concept and final title; upload checklist. If visuals unavailable, revise story or use clearly labeled original reconstruction; never block the pipeline on one external clip. Suggested pilot shots: 35–55 edited shots, 4–6 interior/exterior environments, 2–4 speaking characters; review based on actual throughput rather than fixed numbers.

## 8. Pilot: E001 — The Parcel Nobody Ordered
Cold open: Jax's ship is impounded over an invoice. To cover the charge, he takes a delivery job without reading the destination. On the lunar approach the destination vanishes from the navigation registry. Mira, a cartographer whose credentials were suspended for questioning maps, recognizes the deletion pattern. PIP reports that the delivery is both overdue and legally impossible. A customs officer tries to confiscate the parcel; a rival bidder offers more money than the entire ship is worth. Jax must decide whether to hand it over or save a person caught in the seizure. He chooses the person, loses his fee and acquires an enemy. The crew escapes with a partial coordinate, revealed only because Mira notices the parcel's invoice contains an impossible location. Ending: the customs warrant has their names, and the destination is still officially nonexistent.

Pilot visual plan: lunar polar terrain and orbital flyover from NASA/USGS; original cargo ship and depot; Shackleton crater as the real geography, fictional interior and docking operation; original navigation overlay; exterior/interior intercut; no fake NASA insignia. Actual crater lighting, topography, time/location claims to be checked. Suggested cold-open line: “The good news is we own the ship. The less good news is that the ship appears to own our bank account.”

Pilot test: E001 introduces stakes; E004 introduces bureaucratic farce; E036 tests literary crossover and spectacle (make a standalone proof-of-concept scene before producing it in sequence). Produce E001 animatic first, then E002–003 to test a connected arc. Compare full production time, new asset count, asset acquisition failure rate, minutes of original animation, retention curve, click-through, returning viewers and qualitative feedback. Metrics are diagnostic, not promises of success. Suggested gate: audience understands fictional premise and core cast without explanation; pilot is entertaining without astronomy interest; facts and rights documented; second episode materially faster to make without visual degradation. Reviewer sets numerical targets after baseline.
